# Reviewer 2 report: "Trust Is Not Transitive" (round 7)

Role: empirical software engineering and artifact evaluation. Focus: verifiability, transparency, evaluation design.

## Scores (1-5)

| Criterion | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Novelty | 4 | An exact, linear condition for adversarial conditional contracts over guard DAGs is a real contribution, though the core is a small fragment. |
| Rigor | 3 | The calculus and proofs hold up under my attacks. The evaluation is weaker than described: several "independent" checks reuse the rule under test. |
| Relevance | 3 | Honest about contracts being assumptions. No real hierarchy, practitioner or independent modeller; RQ4 verdicts are largely typed in by the modeller. |

## Recommendation: major revision

The theory is probably right. My independent probes below support it. The problem is the instrument and how it is described. Most fixes are cheap, which is why a revision should be demanded rather than the paper rejected.

## Findings, by severity

### Critical

**1. A reported measurement is a hard-coded zero.**
Location: `experiments/v2/harness.py:152`; `scripts/make_macros.py:65`; `main.tex:1024` ("no accepted specification had an unsafe contract-respecting run (\resDiffUnsafeAccepted{})").
Problem: `unsafe_accepted` is initialized to 0 and never updated anywhere. A grep finds no other write. The paper prints a constant as an experimental result, and the macro provenance makes it look traced.
Fix: compute it. For each accepted spec, search models through `reference.wf` and `reference.evaluate`, or delete the sentence and field.

**2. Contract satisfaction, the central definition, is never executed by any experiment.**
Location: `formal/calculus/reference.py:78` (`wf`), `:117` (`violating_model_exists`); `harness.py:183`; `run.py:75-132`; `main.tex:1022, 1024, 1028`, Table I.
Problem:
- `reference.wf`, the executable form of the lower half of Figure 1, has no caller.
- E3's "brute-force search for violating models" does not search models. It enumerates paths and reuses the credit rule (`req <= omega`, `reference.py:131`) and the reduction from the completeness proof: earlier guards escalate, and only the final guard's E is varied. So it is a path-wise restatement of `chk`. "Uses no part of the checker" is true of the import, not of the logic.
- E6 says each replay had "all contracts respected", yet `witness_model` returns no E or S. `harness.py:183` checks a re-derived partial condition. Acceptance itself is tautological, since the witness sets the verdict to `acc`. The `failing_obligation` field is never read by any oracle (finding 6).
- The E4 adequacy check compares only `check`. No `eval_def` or `wf_def` appears in it, so "closes the loop between the theorems' definitions and the tested code" holds for one of the three definitions.
Fix: replace the E3 and E6 oracles with real enumeration through `wf` and `evaluate`, exhaustively for sizes up to 3 and sampled above. My scratch version took under 40 s for 164,470 hierarchies and found no disagreement. Extend adequacy to `eval` and `wf`. Reword E3 to match what it does.

**3. Results cannot be tied to the code, and "written before the runs" cannot be checked.**
Location: `results/v2/environment.json`; `results/manifest.json`; `main.tex:967`; `PROTOCOL.md:3`; `docs/ERRATA.md:46`.
Problem:
- `git log --all` shows no commit touching `experiments/v2` or `src/tint/hsl`. All of `results/v2`, the protocol, the calculus and the checker are untracked. The recorded commit `3833cae` does not contain the code that produced the results.
- `PROTOCOL.md` was last saved at 10:59:15, the same second as `core.py` and `mutate.py`, and 2 minutes before the oldest surviving result. Nothing external dates it. Everything happened within hours on one day.
- `hsl/__init__.py` and `engine.py` were last saved at 12:21:21. `e8` (11:01), `e12` (11:03) and `e7` (11:05) are older. The errata list reruns for E3, E4, E5, E6, E9, E10 and E13 only. E7 compares `check` verdicts and Table I cites it as evidence for Algorithm 1, so the protocol's "affected experiments rerun in full" was not met. "Findings are unchanged" is asserted, not shown.
- `e9` was saved at 12:23:57, 156 s after that source edit, but its recorded timings alone sum to at least 278 s. Either E9 started before the last source save or the mtimes mislead. I cannot tell which, and neither can a reader.
Fix: commit, tag, and rerun everything from the tag, including E7, E8 and E12. Record the tag's commit and a clean-tree flag in `environment.json`, with PRISM and Java versions. Make the protocol commit an ancestor of the run. If that cannot be shown for this study, replace "written before the runs" with what can be shown, and do not imply preregistration.

**4. E7 is misdescribed, and the PRISM baseline is neither independent nor a fair comparator.**
Location: `run.py:194-229, 240`; `main.tex:962` ("monolithic verifiers that know nothing of our theorems"), `:1030`, `:1053`, `:1110`.
Problem:
- `prism_model` hard-wires the composition rule. Lines 214 and 222 (`requires <= evidence`) decide whether a contract binds, so E(n) never appears in the model. The theorem's worst-case choice of E is already built in. That is the rule under test, so "know nothing of our theorems" is false for PRISM.
- The Z3 encoding is better: it has per-invocation `hold` variables and does not use `credited`. It is still a flat propositional restatement of Figure 1 by the same author, not a model of any implementation. "Monolithic" should be explained in that sense.
- The MDP has no probabilities. `Pmax>0` is graph reachability.
- E9 forces `-explicit`, and the paper does not say so. In this install the symbolic engines fail: `-mtbdd` and `-hybrid` both give `UnsatisfiedLinkError` on `prism.dll`. So the baseline's limit partly reflects a broken toolchain, and I could not measure the symbolic engines.
- In `diamonds`, goals grow as 2d and the model guesses the safe set first, so states grow like 2^(2d) times the node count (2,693, 49,325, 845,333). This is an encoding artifact, confounded with depth. It is not the 2^d path explosion that Table IV's axis implies.
- PRISM's version is not recorded.
Fix: remove the claim. Give PRISM per-invocation obligation variables so that the reduction is not assumed. Fix the native library, report explicit and symbolic engines, and keep goals fixed while depth grows. State clearly that neither encoding is independent of Figure 1. Line 1110 and line 962 currently contradict each other.

### Major

**5. Recertification has a soundness hole: certificates survive a behavioural change.**
Location: `engine.py:137-147`, `:36-45`; `main.tex:900`.
Problem: `_identities` hashes rules, guards and conditions, but not the `checks` predicates, although they decide outcomes at `_unestablished`. I built two engines with `c = True` and `c = False`: identical fingerprints, opposite decisions, `affected_routes` empty, and the old certificate opened the route on the changed engine. So "changing a stage stales exactly the routes it precedes" is false for checked obligations. An obligation's kind is also unhashed. Likewise `component_digest` uses source text only: two closures with different captured thresholds hashed the same with opposite decisions, and that is the idiom `run.py:306` uses. Helpers and imports are not covered, so a prompt in a module constant changes nothing. Line 902 discloses models only, not parameters or transitive code.
Fix: hash check predicates per node and obligation kinds, and include closure values. Require deployers to declare a config digest, and make its absence a reported residual obligation. Add a test. State the guarantee as "direct source, declared version and config".

**6. E5 omits the code that the certificate theorems depend on, and the headline 100% hides this.**
Location: `mutation.py:20-21`; `main.tex:1026`, Table I.
Problem: targets exclude `gated`, `prefix_fingerprints`, `certify`, `affected_routes`, `witness_model`, `parse`, `view_for`, `Engine.__init__`. "We mutated the checker and engine" reads as complete. I ran the same operators and oracle on those functions in scratch: 111 mutants, 94 killed, 17 survived. About 5 are annotations and several more are likely equivalent, but these are not:
- `gated`: `available[child] & out` to `|` survives. This turns the meet into a union, so a route with a deterministic gate on only one path counts as hard and opens without a certificate. It is exactly the rule the paper reports fixing in the failures subsection, and nothing tests it.
- `witness_model`: both `failing_obligation` mutants survive.
- `RouteCertificate.valid` boundaries survive, and all four switch edge-label mutants at `engine.py:71` survive, so no test covers branch-swap detection.
Also: 52 of 136 kills are crashes or timeouts, which the paper does not break out. 100.0% comes from tests written after reading the survivors of the same 141 mutants, on a denominator after hand exclusion; the paper does disclose the post hoc tests. The number 102 has no result file and cannot be regenerated because `mutation.py` has no differential-only mode.
Fix: extend targets, report kills by mode (differential, unit, crash, timeout) in the result file, and add a differential-only switch. Use a held-out mutant set for the strengthened oracle.

**7. "Linear" is measured only on accepting hierarchies, and E9's "known worst case" is not the checker's worst case.**
Location: `run.py:402, 409` (`assert report.ok`); `main.tex:537, 788, 962, 1037`.
Problem: a chain of guards each missing one obligation takes 0.2, 0.79, 3.2, 13.4, 63.7 s at 2k to 32k nodes. That is exact quadratic growth from storing a witness path per finding. The Complexity theorem's F term technically covers it, but the abstract and conclusion say linear without qualification, and a million-node rejected spec is infeasible (about 17 hours by extrapolation, and 500 billion stored path entries). Diamonds are the worst case for enumeration, not for the checker. Separately, that theorem's "within ‖H‖" for masks is not met: `bit` stores `1<<i` (quadratic memory in goals) and `sum(...)` over a scope costs |scope| big-integer additions per layer. With 200 full-scope layers, time grows about 2.5-3.6 times per doubling of goals. E9's goal sweep has one layer, so it cannot see this residual after the failure the paper reports as fixed.
Fix: make witness paths lazy or use a parent-pointer representation, and report a rejecting family. State "decision is linear; full witness output is O(|V|·findings)". Build masks by bytes. Add a many-layers family.

**8. E13 verdicts are largely inputs, and the closing inference is circular.**
Location: `experiments/v2/cases/*.json`; `main.tex:1095`; `ERRATA.md:48`.
Problem: the verdict depends on whether the modeller wrote an assumed obligation in `evidence`. Writing it in NeMo and LlamaFirewall flips both to accepted with zero findings. Giving Trust or Escalate NeMo's obligation flips it to rejected with 3 findings. The asymmetry is the modeller's: `iid_calibration` is granted on raw-view guards in ToE, CA and DevAgent L2, while `input_free_of_addressed_instructions` is denied for NeMo. The models were also changed after results, with two verdicts flipping; that is disclosed, but E13 was then not labelled exploratory as the protocol's last line requires. So "the published designs with the strongest structural guarantees are the ones it accepts" is a result of the encoding. Table V's caption says published designs but includes DevAgent and the policy inventory. `devagent_exposed_judge` also differs from `devagent` in more than the judge (no `env` goal, different requires), though it is presented as a variant.
Fix: drop the sentence. Define encoding rules beforehand (one threat model; every obligation grounded in a quoted source sentence), publish the quotes, and report the sensitivity table. Have a second person encode blind and report agreement.

**9. The justification paragraph does not hold up.**
Location: `main.tex:962`.
Problem:
- "of the kind that could have shown it wrong": E12 cannot fail unless Clopper-Pearson is coded wrongly, and E6's acceptance is tautological.
- "independent reference semantics": same author, same reductions (finding 2).
- "know nothing of our theorems": false (finding 4).
- "known worst case": false (finding 7).
- "no corpus of industrial hierarchies exists" has no evidence of a search. Public NeMo, LlamaFirewall and Guardrails configurations exist and could be mined.
- "small-scope hypothesis gives confidence" is assumed, not argued, and is weak here (finding 10).
- "neither bears on these claims" holds for RQ1-RQ2 but not RQ4, nor for the conclusion's "an engineer can check, build in or knowingly accept", which is an unevaluated usability claim.
Fix: rewrite claim by claim. Say which link each experiment can falsify. Add at least a small mined corpus or a modelling study, or shrink RQ4 to "illustration".

**10. E3's bound means less than "every hierarchy up to 4 nodes".**
Location: `harness.py:111-137`; `main.tex:1022`; `PROTOCOL.md:15, 29`.
Problem: the "bounded grammar" is undefined in the paper. It has 2 goals, 1 obligation, 1 view, and rule names fixed. With one obligation, partial evidence (req={o1,o2}, Ω={o1}) cannot occur, and multi-finding cases and view findings are absent. The Engine is not in E3. The count is inflated: only 2,356,028 of the 34,444,800 size-4 specs (6.8%) have every node reachable, and the rest repeat smaller hierarchies. Only 495,328 contain a join, and 4 nodes allow only a single small join, so two successive diamonds never occur. Filtering to fully reachable gives 106.9 million at size 5, about 3 times the work now spent, so the protocol's "what enumerates within one hour" bound was not actually reached (146 s used). Size-4 stopped from raw product growth, not from the protocol's limit.
Fix: define the grammar in the paper and report distinct reachable counts. Enumerate reachable-only forms to size 5 or 6, with 2 obligations. Better, prove a small-scope lemma (one witness goal and one witness obligation suffice), since this structure seems to admit it.

**11. Protocol deviations are not all reported, and the E12 rule was failed literally.**
Location: `main.tex:967, 989, 990, 1017, 1037, 1053`; Table II against `PROTOCOL.md`.
Problem:
- E12's rule is "coverage at least 1-delta". Two laws gave 0.949. The Monte Carlo explanation is reasonable but post hoc; Table II repeats the rule as if met.
- E11's "zero locality violations" is in Table II but not in the protocol. It came with a harness narrowed after a failed first run. The narrowing looks justified, but it is a post hoc rule and not labelled exploratory.
- "Timings are medians of seven runs" is untrue for Table IV: `tint` is a median of 3, and Z3, enumeration and PRISM are single runs (`run.py:421-439`).
- The E9 fit uses 5 of 7 sizes (nodes ≥ 10000), not in the protocol. This happens to be conservative, but should be stated.
- E5's decision rule yields no pass or fail, yet 100% is reported as a result.
Fix: add one table of deviations. Set a tolerance for E12 (for example a lower Wilson bound or a one-sided test across 40 laws). Label post hoc rules exploratory.

**12. E12 does not test what Table I says, and its reporting is selective.**
Location: `run.py:530-583`; Table I (`certify`, `gated`; E12); `main.tex:1017`.
Problem: E12 never calls `certify`, `gated`, `evaluate` or a closed route. It calls `binomial_upper` on linear cascades of 2-5 guards. It tests a textbook interval, not the Certified risk theorem's pipeline: no DAGs, no partial recertification, no mix of runs. RQ1 is the wrong home for it. The paper gives only the understating direction of the product (18 of 40, up to 5.7 times), and omits that it does not understate in the other 22 and that the median true/product ratio is 0.89.
Fix: run E12 end to end through `certify` and `Engine.evaluate` on random DAGs, with one layer changed and only stale routes recalibrated, then test coverage of the deployed risk. Report the ratio's full distribution.

**13. The engine does not enforce the premises of the Certified risk theorem.**
Location: `engine.py:87-89, 130-135`; `main.tex:886-890`.
Problem: a hand-made certificate with `delta=0.99, samples=1, upper=0` opened a route and accepted. Nothing checks Σδ_r ≤ δ, a minimum N, or that the shares were sized for the current |Q|. If routes are added, old certificates keep δ/|Q_old|. Default `budget=1.0`, so the budget check is off unless set.
Fix: take δ as an engine parameter, reject a total share above it, and require budget explicitly. Otherwise list these as deployer obligations in the assumptions subsection.

### Moderate

**14. E4's generator is much smaller than advertised, and E4/E6 compare very little at engine level.**
Location: `harness.py:28-54`; `main.tex:967`.
Problem: on 20,000 samples from the seed, reachable size has mean 4.4, median 4, p90 8; 57.5% have at most 4 reachable nodes and 24% at most 2, overlapping E3. 16.4% have no route, 39.6% have a join. So "randomly on larger ones" is weak. Engine comparisons use scripted stubs and always-true predicates, with one layer per node; a failing checked obligation and shared layers never occur. Engine against `evaluate` compares two short walkers.
Fix: size by reachable nodes and report the distribution. Add failing predicates and shared layers to the generator.

**15. Reproducibility from documented commands.**
Location: `run.py:52, 136-138, 234`; `scripts/tasks.py:28-35`; `ARTIFACT.md`; `main.tex:967`.
Problem:
- Running the documented `tasks.py proofs` changes only timings, yet that alone makes `manifest.py --verify` and `verify_numbers.py` fail, because `proofs.json` is hashed with timings in it. I confirmed that it is the only differing file. It can also change a printed macro (slowest cvc5 0.03 rather than 0.01 in my run). `tasks.py verification` does not refresh macros or the manifest, and no single documented command does.
- E4 depends on CPU count: `specs // workers` and seeds `MASTER+i` give a different sample, and a different total, on another machine (8 CPUs gives 39,996 specs from 6 seeds, not 40,000 from 20). With two CPUs, `Pool(0)` fails.
- PRISM is hard-coded to `.tools` and `java.exe`, so E7 and E9 raise `StopIteration` off Windows, and `verification` has no way to skip them. ARTIFACT's `PRISM_HOME` is ignored here.
- Table macros (`resVersusTable`, `resCasesTable`, `resProofsTable`) have no provenance entries.
- "Every number in this paper is generated from the result files" is false: 102, "about nine minutes", "about one second", 0.007, and the first-run slopes.
- ARTIFACT is mostly the old study: "not a completed ... evaluation", "20 pages", and no per-experiment commands or times.
- "link withheld for anonymous review" sits beneath a named author.
Fix: split timings into a separate unhashed file, fix worker-independent seeds and counts per task, read `PRISM_HOME`, and provide `tasks.py reproduce-v2`. Write a one-page ARTIFACT table of claim, command, output and time. Macro-ize the literals and reword the sentence.

**16. Fail-closed is not fully true.**
Location: `engine.py:203-204`; `main.tex:934`.
Problem: `_condition` has no handler. A raising switch condition propagates out of `evaluate`, so there is no Outcome. It does not accept, but "an exception in a stage ... denies" is false, and `_condition` was an E5 target. The test suite has no raising switch.
Fix: catch and deny, add the test.

**17. "Each provably necessary" overstates the corollary "Every obligation counts".**
Location: `main.tex:739, 911, 1165`.
Problem: the corollary concerns guards, but residual lists include veto gates. In a two-node spec (veto gate with obligation o, then a guard covering the goal) the residual lists (v, o), and removing o leaves the hierarchy accepted. Line 739 is careful. The conclusion and line 911 are not.
Fix: say "each residual at a guard with an open goal". Compute necessity per entry, by re-checking with the entry removed, and report it.

**18. E1's vacuity claim and T8 are weaker than stated.**
Location: `main.tex:923, 999`; `prove.py:318-324, 381-388, 434`.
Problem: premises are sat for 56, `unknown` for 2 (`T2 gate-uncredited`, `T9 derive`), and n/a for 1. "0 vacuous" counts only `unsat`, and the protocol's rule "not vacuous" is not established for those two. The script exits 0 if cvc5 fails. cvc5 receives Z3-printed SMT-LIB, so its independence excludes the printer and the encoding. T8 is close to empty: `Safe` is uninterpreted and assumed per step, `nil-safety` is `x ⇒ x`, `spent` is unused, and the budget case is one arithmetic step with no induction and no link to `CumulativeBudget`. Counting it in "all structural inductions" flatters coverage. The Route risk proof restricts scopes but does not say what happens to gates whose φ lies in G_s with a false promise; A must also be intersected with G_d.
Fix: give explicit models for the two `unknown` cases. Report "56 sat, 2 unknown, 1 n/a". Fail on cvc5. Tie T8 to T1 and the trace budget, or mark it as a remark. Patch the proof.

**19. Threats section omits the main threats.**
Location: `main.tex:1110-1116`.
Problem: it is honest on trusted base and same-author reference, but silent on the following: same-day uncommitted runs; a sole modeller for E13; verdict sensitivity; the toolchain limit on PRISM; the mutation target restriction; post hoc edits to the oracle; synthetic generator bias; and reuse of the same rule across oracles. Line 1110 says Z3 and PRISM give "independent derivations of the verdicts", which finding 4 refutes for PRISM.
Fix: add these, per finding.

### Minor

**20.** E10's "in line with" the Decision cost theorem conflates a stage count with time, and every stage is a bare guard, so no gates, evidence or views are measured. Report a realistic mix.

**21.** E11's 1,406 hierarchies are survivors of 6,000 attempts (accepted, at least 2 routes), and changes are guard versions only, never gates, switches or topology. The locality check tests under-approximation only, so "exactly" is not tested. The whole-policy comparison ignores that the whole bound is tighter by the paper's own factor of 1.25.

**22.** E8 is sound as a continuity check and has teeth (about 55% and 70% allowed). It compares one threshold per study and shares its scorer and judge code with the original. Its protocol line names seeds 10-19, while the primary rows are seeds 0-9. Add a sentence.

**23.** The title's "Scalable" and the abstract's million-node headline sit awkwardly with line 1114, where realistic hierarchies are small. Lead with exactness and incrementality.

## Summary for the editor

The manuscript's theorems survive independent attack. Its evaluation claims to be more independent, more complete and more traceable than the artifact shows. Require findings 1-8 before re-review, with results rerun from a tagged commit.
