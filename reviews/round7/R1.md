# Review R1 (round 7): rigor and novelty

Manuscript: "Trust Is Not Transitive: A Sound, Complete and Scalable Verification Approach for Hierarchical LLM Guardrails", IEEE TSE.
Reviewer focus: formal methods.
Basis: `paper/main.tex` from `\begin{document}`, `formal/calculus/{core,prove,mutate,reference}.py`, `src/tint/hsl/{__init__,engine,rules}.py`, `experiments/v2/{harness,run}.py`, `experiments/v2/cases/*.json`, `results/v2/*.json`. No repository file other than this review was changed.

## Scores (1-5)

| Criterion | Score | Reason |
|---|---:|---|
| Novelty | 2 | The modelling is useful. The theorems are available-expressions analysis on an uninterpreted loop-free program, plus the law of total probability and Clopper-Pearson. No guarantee ever discharges an assumption. |
| Rigor | 3 | The core (soundness, completeness, meet, DAG) is correct and I could not break it. Route risk is false as stated, certified risk is not supported for the incremental use the paper advertises, and "exact", "linear" and "each provably necessary" are overclaims. |
| Relevance | 3 | The problem is real. Contracts carry all the weight, so verdicts on published designs follow from what the authors wrote into `requires`. |
| Verifiability and Transparency | 3 | The artifact is strong and failures are reported well. However, one reported result is a constant that nothing can change, one reported check covers less than stated, and several evidence claims exceed what the harness does. |
| Presentation | 4 | Clear and well organized. The precise caveats in the body are contradicted by the abstract and conclusion. |

## Recommendation: major revision

The core calculus can stand. The risk and certificate section needs repair, the exactness and complexity claims need precise restatement, and the paper must either strengthen the calculus or accept that its contribution is a modelling discipline rather than a deep composition result.

## What I checked and found correct

So that the findings below are read fairly:

- **Theorems 1 and 2, Lemmas 1 and 2, Theorem 3 (algorithm correctness).** I rechecked each by hand. The statements are true and the proofs are complete up to small omissions (for example, `E(n)` is not specified in the escalate case; the script uses TOP).
- **Independent brute force.** I wrote my own search over the semantics of Fig. 2, with `S` ranging over subsets of Goals and `E` over all obligation subsets at every reached node, including veto gates. It uses nothing from `reference.py`. On 100,000 random DAGs (2-5 nodes, 67,818 accepted), `hsl.check` had 0 disagreements.
- **Monotonicity, Routes, Prefix locality, Decision cost, Non-interference, Taint.** True as stated, and the obligations match the statements.
- **Obligations.** I reran all 59 in Z3 (Z3 5.3.3) in memory: all unsat. I did not rerun cvc5; I read the recorded results (59 unsat). The induction steps are correctly formed. The argument generalization in the gate case (IH at `Acr`) and the explicit Frame instances in completeness are sound.
- I attempted a counterexample to Theorem 3 with shared nodes, `then == otherwise`, and an empty goal set, and found none.

## Findings, ranked by severity

Labels: **[ERROR]** the statement or claim is false or unsupported as written. **[GAP]** the claim may be repairable but the evidence or proof given does not establish it. **[TASTE]** my preference.

### Critical

**1. [ERROR] Theorem "Route risk" is false as stated, and the paper's definition of a deterministic gate worsens it.**
Location: Sec. "Risk Is a Sum over Routes", `thm:risk`; Sec. Syntax ("A gate whose layer has no obligations is a deterministic gate").
- The premise allows "all contracts hold, except possibly the promises about G_s". That exception covers every promise about G_s, including those of the deterministic gates that make a route hard. The proof then says those gates' "contracts are unconditional", which is exactly what the premise has waived.
- Counterexample: `gate(g, phi_s, epsilon, {}, guard(n, A, {}, deny))` with `G_s = {phi_s}`, `G_d = {}`. Route `n` is hard. Let `g` pass an unsafe proposal, which the premise permits. Then `R = R_n > 0`, while `Q` is empty and the sum is 0.
- Separately, by the paper's definition a veto stage whose layer has `req = {}` is "deterministic", so a learned classifier with no declared obligations makes a route hard. This is the failure Sec. "Failures" says was fixed. The code (`gated`, `node.layer is None`) has the right condition. The paper does not.
- The proof also glosses a step: a gate with `phi` in `G_s` must be handled under the restriction, and "promises about G_s" needs a per-goal reading of a contract.
- Fix: define deterministic as `layer = epsilon` (an explicit tag), not `req = {}`. Restrict the exception to guards and veto stages. State the deterministic gates' correctness as a premise. Write the restricted hierarchy out.
- Related overclaim (Sec. Limits): "Only the hard routes are robust" to adaptation. A hard route can still have a goal of `G_d` covered only by a guard's contract. Hardness concerns `G_s` only. Either forbid guards from covering `G_d`, or define a stronger "fully gated" route.

**2. [ERROR] Theorem "Certified risk" does not cover the incremental use it is built for, and the engine does not enforce its confidence accounting.**
Location: `thm:certified` proof ("sum_r delta_r <= delta"), Eq. (cp) with `delta_r = delta/|Q|`, Sec. "Incremental Recertification"; `Engine.__init__`, `RouteCertificate.delta`.
- `delta_r` depends on `|Q|`, but the proof line assumes the share budget still sums to `delta` after reuse. Append a guard to a cascade: existing fingerprints do not change, so old certificates at `delta/q` stay open, and the new route gets `delta/(q+1)`.
- Executed: two routes certified at 0.05/2, a third added and certified at 0.05/3. The engine opened all three with total share 0.0667, above the nominal 0.05.
- The engine never sums `c.delta` and has no `delta` parameter. It also accepts any `delta` below 1, so a certificate at 0.9 passes.
- The guarantee is also per configuration. Over a sequence of T versions with recertification, the chance that some deployed configuration is wrong can grow toward `T*delta`. The paper's CI narrative invites exactly this reading.
- "`<= epsilon`" is not part of the probabilistic statement; it is a runtime check. Say so.
- Fix: make the premise explicit (`sum over open routes of delta_r <= delta`) and enforce it in the engine. Use a fixed allocation (a per-route weight, or alpha-spending across versions) and state the guarantee over a version sequence.

**3. [ERROR] Fingerprints do not bind what the certificate measured.**
Location: Sec. "Incremental Recertification" ("A certificate should stop applying exactly when what it measured may have changed", "stales exactly the routes it precedes"); `component_digest`, `prefix_fingerprints`, `_identities`.
Executed counterexamples, all with unchanged fingerprints or digests:
- Changing a runtime predicate in `checks` flipped a route from accepted to denied. `checks` are not in the identities, though a failed checked obligation decides the run.
- Adding a goal to `Goals` changes `Y`, so the measured `k_r` is about a different event. Neither Goals nor the statistical set is hashed, nor the labelling oracle.
- Closures from one factory (`mk(.1)`, `mk(.9)`) have equal digests. Thresholds are the usual tuning parameter.
- `functools.partial(print, 1)` and `functools.partial(len, 2)` have equal digests: `getsource` fails and the fallback is `repr(type)`. These are different code, so the abstract's "bound to the code" is false here, not merely "code, not behaviour".
- Callees and base classes of a component are not hashed either.

The caveat in the paper (model files) covers only part of this.
Fix: include checks, Goals, `G_s` and a label-definition digest in the fingerprint. Refuse components without a stable identity rather than falling back to `repr(type)`. Replace "exactly" with a stated list of what is bound.

**4. [ERROR] "Every rejected hierarchy has a concrete contract-respecting execution" and "decided exactly" hold only for an abstraction in which every node is an independent free variable.**
Location: abstract; text after `thm:complete`; conclusion; E6; title ("Complete").
- `P`, `V`, `W`, `E` and `S` are free per node, but "A switch routes on a trusted condition" and HSL carries condition and rule names. Two nodes with the same deterministic condition are correlated in every real deployment.
- Executed: `switch(s1,c, gate(g,phi,s2), s2)`, `s2 = switch(c, guard a, deny)`, Goals `{phi}`, `a` with empty scope. Checker verdict: rejected, witness path `s1, s2, a`. On the real engine with the check removed, `c=True` runs through the gate and `c=False` denies at `s2`. The witness cannot execute, and no proposal violates `phi`.
- The paper's caveat only addresses guards doing more than they declare. This case involves no guard and no contract: the trusted condition itself rules the path out.
- The same holds for same-named rules, for the same layer at two nodes on one input, and for logical relations among goals (`S` ranges over all subsets).
- Linear time depends on this. If equal condition names denote one Boolean, exactness is coNP-hard: use one clause stage per CNF clause and a final guard with an open goal, which is reachable iff the formula is satisfiable.
- The harness hides this. `enumerate_small` gives all switches condition `'c'` and treats them as independent. `wired_engine` keys stages by node, so `random_hierarchy` needs unique names. E6 replays `Scripted` stubs reading a table by node, so it shows the model is executable with free stubs, not with real conditions.
- Rejection also occurs outside `chk`. Executed: all goals established by gates, plus one view-tagged evidence item unused at the accepting guard. `chk` holds, `check` reports `view`, and the engine refuses construction. The view rule has no counterpart in the semantics at all, so `check` is not `chk`, and "every rejected hierarchy" is false as written. Theorem 3's careful "no behavioural finding" is lost in the abstract.
- Fix: state the theorem as exactness relative to the free-stage abstraction. Say plainly that it is path-insensitive. Either add shared condition variables (and report the complexity change, and offer the free analysis as the sound approximation) or forbid repeated conditions. Move the view rule into the semantics or make it a warning.

**5. [ERROR] A reported result is a constant.**
Location: E4, "no accepted specification had an unsafe contract-respecting run (`\resDiffUnsafeAccepted`)"; `experiments/v2/harness.py:152`, `scripts/make_macros.py:65`.
- `unsafe_accepted` is initialized to 0 in `differential` and never updated anywhere in `experiments/v2`, `tests` or `scripts`. The paper reports a value no run could have changed.
- The neighbouring `semantic_disagreements` is a real measure, but it is a different quantity and rests on the weak oracle of finding 11.
- Also, `reference.wf` is never called by any experiment or test, so the implementation of `⊨` is never executed. E5's statement that the mutation score measures the oracle therefore cannot speak to `wf`.
- Fix: implement the check (run `wf` plus `evaluate` on sampled and enumerated models of accepted hierarchies) or delete the sentence, record it in the errata, and add a macro lint that fails on a field never assigned.

### Major

**6. [ERROR] "Linear" is false in general, and Theorem "Complexity" fails for the implementation.**
Location: abstract, contribution 2, conclusion; `thm:complexity`; `check` in `src/tint/hsl/__init__.py`; Sec. Failures, E9.
- The theorem's own bound contains `(|V|+|E|) * |Goals|/w`. With `|Goals|` proportional to `|V|` this is quadratic in the specification over `w`. The docstring says "linear for fixed |goals|". The abstract drops the condition.
- `gap_path` scans a node's predecessors at each step, so one step costs in-degree, not O(1). The proof charges "at most |V| steps" to `F`, which counts path length. Executed: a balanced switch tree into one guard, with `m` missing obligations:

  | m = leaves | V | time (s) | total path length F |
  |---:|---:|---:|---:|
  | 2,000 | 4,000 | 0.22 | 24,012 |
  | 4,000 | 8,000 | 0.87 | 52,013 |
  | 8,000 | 16,000 | 3.49 | 112,014 |
  | 16,000 | 32,000 | 19.1 | 240,015 |

  `V+E+F` roughly doubles and time quadruples.
- `bit = {goal: 1 << i ...}` costs `|G|^2/w` words, and `sum(...)` per layer costs `|scope| * |G|/w`, outside the claimed `||H||`. `sorted(h.goals)` adds a log factor. Executed on one node: 2^18 goals 2.1 s, 2^20 goals 57 s. This is the same class of defect as the first failure reported in Sec. Failures, and E9's largest goal count (32,768) sits below where it shows.
- Fix: state "O((|V|+|E|) * |G|/w + ||H|| log ||H|| + F*Delta), linear for bounded |G|", or memoize one lacking predecessor per (node, goal) and build masks in place. Remove the unconditional "linear" from the abstract.

**7. [ERROR] "Each provably necessary" is stronger than the corollary and is false for the reported residual list.**
Location: conclusion; Sec. Limits ("Corollary shows each is needed"); `cor:necessary`; `evidence_by_kind` at veto gates.
- The corollary concerns guards with an open goal. The checker also lists veto-gate obligations when the goal is open at that gate.
- Executed: `gate(v, g, V{o}, {o}, gate(d, g, eps, {}, guard(a, ...)))`. Residual is `(v, o)`. Removing `o` from `v`'s evidence: still accepted. The obligation is not necessary.
- The same occurs if a later guard covers the goal.
- Fix: add a true necessity computation (an obligation is needed iff removal changes the verdict, which is cheap) and report it, or restrict the claim to guards. The corollary also needs `uniq` stated.

**8. [GAP] Mechanization claims exceed the script.**
Location: Sec. "Theorems for Engineering", Sec. Mechanized Proofs, contribution 4, E1, E4.
- "Each is proved by structural induction ... and machine-checked" appears over a list including `cor:affected`, which is on no list in the script. It is omitted from the trusted-base enumeration too, although it is the only theorem at graph level behind recertification.
- "All structural inductions are discharged": the central step of Theorem 3 ("Unfolding the equations of chk shows ...") is an induction that is not mechanized, and no definition of unfolding or path exists in the script. Only the algebra of Lemma 2 is.
- "The premises of each obligation are themselves checked to be satisfiable": `results/v2/proofs.json` records `unknown` for `T2 gate-uncredited` and `T9 derive`, and `n/a` for `T2 gate-cases`. `vacuous` counts only `unsat`, so `\resProofVacuous{} = 0` conceals 3 of 59 unsettled. One is the key completeness case. I believe those premises are satisfiable, but the text says they were checked.
- Three T8 obligations prove little. `nil-safety` and `cons-safety` are tautologies over uninterpreted `Item` and `Safe`, unconnected to T1. `budget-step` is one step, with no trace induction, and it updates on gate pass, not on acceptance as the theorem says. T9 and T6 are congruence facts. Reporting "59" invites overreading.
- Each theorem exists only as loose obligations. There is no top-level closed statement, and the existentials are wired by hand: the guard cases take `q`, `o` as free premises with no obligation linking `Not(sub(G,A))` to them, though `gate-cases` does exist for gates. Mentioned as "script is trusted", but the reader should know that this is what is meant.
- Definitions do not match exactly:
  - The script has one sort `Ob` for goals and obligations. `S` is not constrained within `G`.
  - The witness is `TOP - q`, whereas the paper uses `Goals - {psi}`, so the machine-checked completeness is a slightly different theorem.
  - The paper's `scope ⊆ Goals`, `phi ∈ Goals` are not in the script.
  - The code accepts undeclared scope and `establishes` names silently (executed), contrary to Algorithm line 1 "reject on ... undeclared name".
  - With `S ⊆ Goals`, a non-goal scope makes the paper's completeness false (the guard cannot lawfully accept), so the discrepancy is not cosmetic for the tool.
- "On 2000 terms the equations agree with its values": `adequacy` covers `check` only, not `eval` or `wf`. So "closes the loop" does not hold for the semantics.
- "Independent solvers": cvc5 reads the SMT-LIB printed by Z3 from a formula built by the same script. The exit code ignores cvc5. Worth stating.
- No proof mutant weakens the title rule: there is none making `E` global rather than per node. There is also no mutant on `uniq`, `ids` or Frame. Add both.
- Fix: correct the sentences, and mechanize Theorem 3 and `cor:affected` in Lean or Isabelle. The theorem count is small enough that this is days of work, and for a paper whose claim is "verified", it is the natural next step now rather than future work.

**9. [ERROR] Novelty: the composition condition does not compose contracts.**
Location: Key idea; Sec. Background on assume-guarantee; Sec. Related ("what is new here is an exact characterization").
- `chk` factors into (i) a per-node syntactic test `req(l) ⊆ Omega_n` and (ii) available expressions with gen-only transfer on a DAG. Omega is a constant at the node. No gate or guard guarantee can ever discharge another's assumption, yet Background says that is what compositional rules are about. Assumptions are discharged only by a declaration.
- Consequence 1: a rejection can be silenced by adding the name to `evidence` with kind `assumed`. On `nemo_rails.json`, add `input_free_of_addressed_instructions` to the rails' evidence and it passes. So the checker certifies that assumptions have been listed. That is useful bookkeeping, but it is not what "verifying a composition" suggests.
- Consequence 2: completeness is nearly immediate. Semantics that havoc everything except declared contracts have all paths feasible, and gen/kill problems have MOP = MFP (Kildall; Kam and Ullman). This is modular verification by havoc-and-assume over a passive loop-free program (Barnett and Leino), with atomic facts so no SMT is needed. Monolithic Z3 "also scales" because the problem has no hard core, as the paper itself notes.
- Consequence 3: the title is an axiom, not a result. `E` is indexed by node by definition. When two guards read one identical sanitized view, trust in that fact is transitive, and the calculus simply requires a second declaration. Saying "that indexing is the formal content of our title" is honest, but no theorem shows transitive reasoning fails; there is only an example.
- Fix options, any of which would change my novelty score:
  - (a) Add obligation flow: gates or guards with verdict-conditioned guarantees on later obligations (`esc` guarantees something, a gate passing establishes an obligation at a successor), with a transfer that may also kill on a view change. Exactness then is no longer immediate.
  - (b) Prove a separation: exhibit a class where per-layer reasoning with transitive evidence is unsound while the calculus is exact, and quantify it. Add a formal comparison with a transitive variant.
  - (c) Add correlated conditions and shared invocations with a complexity classification.
  - (d) Give synthesis: the minimal obligation or gate set making a hierarchy safe, or optimal reordering, which also answers the introduction's unanswered question "Is the order of two layers significant?".
  - (e) A precise characterisation of when order matters.
- Cite the dataflow and modular-verification lineage. `references.bib` has no Kildall, Kam-Ullman or Barnett-Leino.

**10. [GAP] The risk layer needs almost nothing from the calculus.**
Location: Sec. "From Safety to Risk"; contribution 3.
- Route risk is total probability over a partition, plus a zero term. Certified risk is Clopper-Pearson with a union bound. "No independence assumption" is true because nothing was composed.
- For statistical routes, no contract, realization or passing of Algorithm 1 is needed. `R_r` is measured directly on the deployed function. The premises are thus much stronger than necessary, and that hides how little is used. Stating the theorem with contract premises only on hard routes would be both stronger and more honest.
- Merkle prefix hashing is build-system caching and regression test selection (Bazel, Nix, RTS). The contrast with "whole-policy recertification" is weak, because stored labelled data can be replayed. The saved cost should be measured in new labels, and reuse conditions discussed.
- Fix: strengthen premises as above. Compare with these baselines. Consider a sequential or anytime-valid bound, which would make the incremental story real.

**11. [GAP] Conformance evidence is weaker than described.**
Location: E3, E6, E12, `tab:mapping`, Threats.
- E3: `violating_model_exists` is not a "brute-force search for violating models". It enumerates paths and the final guard's `S` and `E`, and it embeds the crediting rule (`gates` set) and the argument that intermediate stages impose nothing, which is the proof of completeness. A shared misconception in the gate rule survives. Models are never checked with `wf`, which is dead code. My own full search agrees with the checker, so the result stands, but the description does not.
- E6: `witness_model` has no `E` for intermediate stages and no `S`, so it is not a "concrete model" as Sec. Implementation says. For an uncredited veto gate on the path with `phi = goal`, the contract needs a failing obligation at that gate, and that is not returned. `contract_ok` checks only that the goal was not credited and not in scope; "all contracts respected" is not tested.
- E12: draws multinomials and calls `binomial_upper` directly. Only linear cascades appear, with no hard route, no DAG, and no `certify` or `gated` call for the coverage. `tab:mapping` lists `certify`, `gated` as evidenced by E12. It is a test of Clopper-Pearson.
- Small-scope: E3's alphabet has one obligation, and a single veto-layer type.
- "the implementation matches them on every test we could devise" (conclusion) sits badly with findings 3, 5, 12 and 13.
- Fix: run a true model enumeration calling `wf`; return full models and verify with `wf`; drive E12 through `certify` and `Engine.evaluate`, including DAGs, hard routes and reuse.

**12. [ERROR] Theorem "Session invariants" does not hold of the engine.**
Location: `thm:session`; `Engine.evaluate`; `CumulativeBudget`.
- `evaluate` spends `proposal.cost` on every acceptance, whether or not the budget gate was on the path. Executed: a switch bypassing the budget gate, `L=10`, a cost 100 proposal accepted, `spent = 100`; a following cost -5 proposal also accepted and spent became 95. So `s <= L` and non-negative costs both fail.
- The theorem's wording is about a gate, and the implementation is the claim of E4-style conformance. Also, nothing checks that the gate dominates accepting routes.
- Fix: side condition "the budget gate dominates every route"; check it, or spend only when the gate was passed; validate cost in the proposal.

**13. [ERROR] Implementation claims about fail-closed and views.**
Location: Sec. Implementation; `Engine._condition`, `Engine._run`.
- "An exception in a stage ... denies": `_condition` has no try. Executed: a raising condition propagates `ZeroDivisionError` out of `evaluate`. No acceptance, but no decision either, and `certify` crashes.
- A veto gate's rule receives the whole `Proposal`. Executed: a gate whose layer has view `structured` and a `structured`-tagged construction obligation read `proposal.text`. "A structured view cannot carry free text" is enforced only for guards, while the checker gives view-tied obligations to veto gates too. So Theorem NI's premise fails for such a stage.
- Fix: guard the condition; pass veto gates through `view_for`.

**14. [GAP] E13's verdicts follow from assumptions chosen by the authors.**
Location: Sec. RQ4, `experiments/v2/cases/`.
- NeMo and LlamaFirewall raw rails are given `*_free_of_addressed_instructions`, unsupplied, so they fail. Trust-or-Escalate, Conformal Arbitrage, and DevAgent's own `L2` are `raw` too, and read attacker-reachable text, but require only `iid_calibration`, so they pass.
- By the paper's own thesis a raw guard's promise needs that premise. In particular, DevAgent's route `n2` accepts on raw text with no such obligation, in the very example that condemns a raw judge.
- "The published designs with the strongest structural guarantees are the ones it accepts" is thus close to circular. Keeping the disclaimer "our models" does not fix the asymmetry.
- Fix: one rule for all raw-view contracts, applied mechanically (preferably in `hsl`: a raw layer implicitly requires an obligation). Report the sensitivity of verdicts to it. Rerun DevAgent.

### Minor

**15. [ERROR] `cor:affected` is false for `c = r` under strict "precede".** Changing guard `r` changes the event accepted-on-`r`. The proof silently uses "ancestors of `r`, and `r`". The fingerprint is right. Fix: "`c` is neither `r` nor an ancestor of `r`, in the old and the new graph". The second clause matters for edge changes.

**16. [ERROR] `prop:veto` (a): "equals prod m_i only under conditional independence".** For three or more guards, equality does not imply independence, and it can hold by coincidence. Fix: "for example under", or "if". The rest is right.

**17. [GAP] Theorem "Non-interference" is a congruence.** Equal arguments at every node give equal results, and the antecedent is far stronger than needed. Stronger: agreement only on the nodes of the run, or a declared-declassification form that names the score channel. The sentence "text alone cannot change its verdict", followed by the admission that text can change the score, needs rewording: the structured view is still a function of text.

**18. [GAP] Theorem "Algorithm correctness" statement.** "Hence Theorems hold for graphs" rests on an undefined notion of unfolding (a `deny` for `None`, reachable part only). Define it. The term and graph versions of `safe` should be related by a named lemma.

**19. [TASTE] Table `tab:related`.** One row Y on all columns, others mostly U, on columns chosen by the authors. The footnote is candid, but I would drop the table or add columns on which others win (temporal properties, real guards, empirical effectiveness).

**20. [TASTE] Sec. Setup "Every number in this paper is generated from the result files".** "102", "nine minutes" and "one second" are typed by hand. Either generate them or soften the sentence.

**21. [TASTE] Title and abstract.** "Sound, Complete and Scalable" should be qualified: relative to declared contracts and free stages. E9's slopes at 1.17 and 1.19 against a limit of 1.2 with an unverified attribution are honest, but they do not support "scalable" better than a word-count argument; publish operation counts.

## What would make this strong

1. Repair findings 1-5 and 12: these are the correctness items.
2. Extend the calculus with obligation flow and correlated conditions (finding 9). Prove a separation from transitive reasoning, and classify complexity.
3. Move Theorems 1-3 and `cor:affected` to a proof assistant, with a verified or at least proof-linked checker.
4. Give a verified route-level statistical story under version sequences.
