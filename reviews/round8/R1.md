The revision is substantial and I respect it. Findings 1-4 must be fixed before acceptance, but none undermines the core calculus. Each has a local fix. Finding 5 is a matter of wording and can be fixed in an afternoon.

## What I checked and found correct

- **Soundness, Frame, Completeness.** Rechecked by hand with facts threaded. The subtle points are right. `F` may strictly exceed `B` when a gate's obligations hold by chance, and the generalization over `F ⊇ B` handles this. In the completeness cases, the failing `o` lies outside `Ω ∪ B`, so realization survives. `T1`'s three hypothesis instances cover the gate case.
- **Independent brute force.** My own search over Fig. 1, with `S` over subsets of Goals, `E` over all obligation subsets at each reached gate and guard, and `F` threaded. It shares no code with `reference.py`. Results against `hsl.check`, on three generators:
  - 40,000 general random DAGs: 0 disagreements.
  - 30,000 gate-heavy DAGs: 0 disagreements.
  - 30,000 diamond families built to provoke false alarms: 0 disagreements, 23,985 refined, 502 false alarms correctly repaired.
  So the refinement is genuinely exercised, and the first pass was never unsound.
- **Exactness.** The general case is correct. Gate transfer is monotone, so the meets lie below every path, and a clean first pass suffices by Lemma Meet (a). Lemma Meet (b) needs one evidence set, and the paper's warning about the false generalization is right.
- **Transitive (a), (b); Order (a), (b), (c) as stated.** True. The `T11` and `T12` obligations say what the theorem says.
- **Complexity (b).** Membership is fine. The reduction is correct: rejected iff satisfiable. A path picks a consistent assignment, because a variable gadget gives exactly one of `t_v`, `f_v`. A tautological clause can never fire. The empty clause fires always.
- **The middle-case remark** (only guards consume facts). Correct: a violation exists iff, for some pair (open goal g, missing o), some path generates neither, which is a must-analysis on pairs.
- **Correlated conditions.** Hardness, soundness and the example are correct, subject to finding 8.
- **Route risk.** Now true. `H_d` passes because the first disjunct of `ok` and the second both restrict to `Goals_d`, and credit does not depend on goals. The hard-route step is correct. The counterexample of round 7 no longer applies, since deterministic gates are `ε`-gates and premise (i) covers them.
- **Corollary Affected.** Correct as restated.
- **Obligations.** I reran all 66 in Z3 in memory: none failed. `proofs.json` records the 66 as unsat in both solvers. I did not rerun cvc5.

## Findings, ranked by severity

Labels: **[ERROR]** the statement or claim is false or unsupported as written. **[GAP]** repairable, but what is given does not establish it. **[TASTE]** my preference.

### Major

**1. [ERROR] Provided facts are an assumption that appears on no list, so the paper's central promise about the residual is false.**
Location: abstract ("It shows which claims an assurance argument rests on, and which of them are needed"); text after `thm:complete` ("the claim then moves to the residual list, where someone must answer for it"); Sec. Worked Example ("it is not an assumption of the judge", "The residual list has one entry"); `tab:assumptions`; `Report`, `necessary`, `Engine.__init__`.
- A rejection can be silenced by a `provides` entry as easily as by evidence, and nothing records it. Executed: a guard needing an assumed `adv`, rejected. Prepend a deterministic gate with an always-true rule and `provides: [adv]`. Result: accepted, `residual = ()`, `checked = ()`, `construction = ()`, `necessary = ()`.
- The checker verifies that the name was declared on a gate. It cannot verify the implication that makes this fact true at each later stage: from "this rule passed" to "this obligation holds at n". In the semantics this is realization (`F ⊆ E(n)`), so it is a contract premise of exactly the kind the paper warns about, and it is exactly a persistence claim. Premise (i) of Route risk and the row "Deterministic gates are exact" cover the goal `φ`, not `D`. No row of `tab:assumptions` covers `D`.
- DevAgent is the example. Gate `c` checks one thing, `not facts.untrusted`, and is credited with `contract_holds_on_adversarial_text`. The whole argument for the raw judge rests on that step, which needs Taint, complete label recording and the judge's behaviour on the text that remains. It is declared kind `construction`, yet `report.construction` is `(n4, human_sees_provenance)` only. Run on `experiments/v2/cases/devagent.json`, the fact is on no list.
- Kind is also ignored at runtime. Executed: the same hierarchy with `adv` of kind `checked`. The engine builds with no predicate for it and accepts. This contradicts "A checked obligation has a runtime predicate, and failing it denies". The loop in `Engine.__init__` looks only at `evidence`.
- Related: a veto stage whose goal is statistical can provide a fact. Under premise (ii) of Route risk, "realization holds", that fact must be exact. A stage measured only statistically thus has one exact promise. This is permitted, but the sentence "No statistical contract ... appears here" hides it.
- Fix:
  - Add every consumed provided fact to the report as (provider, consumer, fact), by kind.
  - Extend the necessity test to removal of a `provides` entry.
  - Add a row to `tab:assumptions`: "Provided facts are true and persist".
  - Require a predicate for a provided `checked` fact, evaluated at consumption, or refuse that kind.
  - State in Route risk that facts provided by statistical stages must be exact, or forbid them from crediting.
  - Revise the DevAgent text: the judge's assumption is not gone, it has moved to the gate.

**2. [ERROR] Fingerprints still do not bind what was measured.**
Location: Sec. Incremental Recertification ("A node's fingerprint covers its stage ...", "A component's identity covers its ... closure values ... instance state"); `prefix_fingerprints`, `_value_digest`, `component_digest`.
All executed:
- **Gate layer view is not hashed.** For a gate, `own` holds the layer's name only, while a guard's `own` holds its scope, requirements and view. Changing a veto layer's view from `raw` to `metadata` left every fingerprint equal, and flipped a proposal containing the word `attack` from denied to accepted, because the engine then blanks the text. This is a stale certificate that stays open on a change inside the specification, the very failure Sec. Failures says was fixed.
- **Instance state with a custom `__repr__`.** `_value_digest` trusts `repr`. A closure over an object whose `__repr__` returns a constant gave equal digests for 0.1 and 0.9.
- **`__slots__`.** `getattr(component, '__dict__', {})` is empty for a slotted class. `Sl(.1)` and `Sl(.9)` had equal digests, and so did a `@dataclass(frozen=True, slots=True)` threshold.
- **Arrays.** A closure over a 5,000-element NumPy array, changed at one interior element, had an equal digest, because `repr` elides the middle. These are weights.
The caveat about callees and model files does not cover these: they are direct instance state and closure values, which the paper says are covered.
Fix: hash the full layer record for gates, as for guards. A unit test should assert that every field of `Node`, `Layer` and `Obligation` that the engine reads reaches the hash. In `_value_digest`, do not trust `repr`: walk `__dict__` and `__slots__`, use `tobytes()` for buffers, and refuse anything else.

**3. [ERROR] The premise of Certified risk is too weak, and `R` is ambiguous.**
Location: `thm:certified`; `Engine.__init__`, `certify`.
- The premise is `Σ δ_r ≤ δ` *over open routes*. If which routes are open depends on the data, the union bound does not apply. Counterexample: `K` statistical routes with equal `R_r`. Certify each at level `δ`, independent samples, and open only the route with the smallest `u_r`. The open share is `δ`, so the premise holds, yet the conclusion fails with probability about `1-(1-δ)^K`. The closing paragraph warns about recalibrating one route. It does not address this selection, and the theorem as stated admits it.
- The engine enables a version of this. Executed: three certificates for one route with bounds .2, .1 and .001, each with share .05, under `delta=.05`. The route opens at .001, with the share counted once, because `self.open[route]` is overwritten.
- `R` in Route risk is the risk of `H`. Here it must be the risk of the deployed function with closed routes denying; otherwise `R ≤ Σ_open u_r` is false whenever a closed route carries risk. The proof's remark about Monotonicity shows what is meant, but the statement does not say it.
- "Stateless prefix" is in `tab:assumptions` and not in the theorem or in Route risk. With a ledger-reading gate, "each determining one run" is false. `shadow` uses a fixed ledger, so calibration and deployment differ.
- Fix: state "the set of routes certified for this configuration, and their shares, are fixed before the data are seen, and `Σ δ_r ≤ δ` over all of them, open or not". Have the engine refuse duplicate routes. Define `R` for the deployed function. Add statelessness to the theorem and refuse, or flag, a stateful rule on a statistical route.

**4. [ERROR] Complexity (a) does not hold of the implementation on rejecting inputs.**
Location: `thm:complexity`(a); "Each witness adds at most `|V|`"; Sec. Failures ("since removed"); `check`, `_bits`.
- The theorem bounds deciding, and Sec. Implementation says the checker implements Algorithm 1. But `check(witnesses=False)` still enumerates every finding, and `_bits` costs `Θ(words)` per bit, since each `mask & -mask` and `mask ^= lowest` touches the whole integer. With one node, the bound gives `O(m/w + m log m)`. Executed, one guard, with witnesses off:

  | m | m goals, none covered (s) | m obligations, all missing (s) |
  |---:|---:|---:|
  | 2^17 | 0.83 | 0.98 |
  | 2^18 | 3.36 | 3.17 |
  | 2^19 | 11.5 | 11.6 |
  | 2^20 | 43.6 | 44.3 |

  That is a factor of 3.8 per doubling. A superlinear term has again been found in this code path, and E9's goal sweep is on accepted hierarchies, so it cannot show it.
- `outside` and `needs` are built for all declared layers, including unused ones, so there is a `|L|·|G|/w` term that the bound does not contain.
- `gap_path` shifts by `index` for each predecessor scanned, so a step costs `|G|/w`, not `O(1)`.
- In the abstract, contribution 2 and the conclusion, "near-linear" has no qualifier. With `|G|` proportional to `|V|` the bound is quadratic over `w`.
- Fix: stop at the first finding when only a decision is asked for, or add a term for findings, `F·(|G|+|O|)/w`, to the bound. Iterate bits by bytes. Build masks for reachable layers. Add rejecting cases to E9's goal and obligation sweeps. Say "near-linear for bounded goals and obligations" everywhere the phrase appears.

### Medium

**5. [ERROR] "Characterize when stage order matters" exceeds Theorem Order, and "put gates first" rests on moving evidence, contrary to the paper's thesis.**
Location: abstract; contribution 2; `thm:order`; "This answers the question from the introduction"; Sec. Worked Example ("Placing rules after the split would not help either, since Theorem Order favors them first"); conclusion ("Gates belong before guards").
- The theorem gives one sufficient direction, one commuting case and two examples. That is not a characterization. The missing cases are easy, so this should be fixed by proving more:
  - Adjacent guards always commute for `chk`, since neither alters `(A,B)`. This is not stated.
  - (b) is stated too weakly. Two adjacent deterministic gates commute even if they provide facts, because both are always credited and the sets reaching `k` are equal.
  - Two adjacent gates with any layers commute at `(A,B)`, in that equal sets reach `k`, whenever neither's credit depends on the other, that is, unless `D_1` meets `req(ℓ_2) \ (Ω_2 ∪ B)` with gate 1 credited, or the converse.
  - Guard before gate versus gate before guard: the former checks iff the latter does and also `ok(ℓ,Ω∪B,A)` at the earlier sets.
- (a) keeps `Ω'` fixed while moving the stage. By the paper's own rule, evidence belongs to one invocation. For a veto stage it is often a claim about the traffic there: `miss_rate_bounded` measured on what escalated past `n` says nothing about all traffic. The check stays green because `Ω'` is syntactic. So (a) is true of `chk`, but "put gates first" is justified only for deterministic gates, or after re-establishing `Ω'`. Only those gates have position-free contracts.
- "Preserves acceptance" reads as acceptance of proposals. It is acceptance by the check. Hoisting a gate changes the decisions: anything guard `n` accepted and the gate would deny is now denied. The text mentions cost and traffic, not this change of utility.
- The theorem says nothing about moving a gate across a switch, which is what the worked example cites it for. Moving a gate out of one arm also changes the other arm. On graphs with a shared gate, the move requires a copy. The theorem is on terms.
- Fix: state the local characterization above (a proposition, all easily mechanized). Restrict the slogan to deterministic gates. Reword (a).

**6. [GAP] Necessity is tested one entry at a time, which understates dependence.**
Location: `cor:necessary` (true as stated); conclusion ("a list of assumptions, each tested for necessity"); abstract; `necessary`.
- Executed: veto gates `v1` (needs `o1`), `v2` (needs `o2`), both for goal `g`, with evidence supplied, then a release guard of empty scope. Accepted, `residual = ((v1,o1),)`, `necessary = ()`. Remove both `o1` and `o2`: rejected. So the tool reports no necessary assumption for a hierarchy that depends on `o1 ∨ o2`, and `(v2,o2)` is not even on the residual, because the first-pass `relevant` sees `g` already available.
- Fix: since `chk` is monotone in the evidence, report a minimal sufficient set (greedy removal, polynomial in the flow-free case), or the minimal cuts. State that an entry reported as unnecessary may be needed jointly with another. Base the residual on dependence, not on the meet's availability.

**7. [ERROR] The grammar has no gate without a goal, yet the running example and the hardness proof need one.**
Location: Sec. Syntax; Fig. 1; Listing 1 (`"establishes": null`); proof of `thm:complexity`(b) (variable gates and clause stages establish nothing); Sec. Mechanized Proofs.
- `gate(n,φ,ℓ,Ω,D,H)` has a mandatory `φ`, and `S` is "the set of goals the proposal satisfies". Gate `c` of DevAgent is not a term of the calculus. Taking `φ` outside Goals fails under the paper's reading, since then `φ ∉ S` and a credited gate cannot lawfully pass.
- The script works only because `S` ranges over the shared sort, and the adequacy test quietly maps `None` to a dummy `'-'` placed in `S`. `reference.py` adds `None` to `A`. The paper's disclosure about the shared sort does not mention this.
- The reduction can be repaired with a dummy goal. Then the "one open goal" needs a second goal, and E14 tests a different construction.
- Fix: write `φ ∈ Goals ∪ {⊤}` with `⊤ ∈ S` always, in the paper and in the script (a dedicated constant with `Select(S, top)`), and state the reduction with it.

**8. [ERROR] Proposition Correlated conditions: wording, undefined semantics, and an unused opportunity.**
Location: `prop:correlated`; "so every rejection can be replayed"; `witness_model`.
- "use one switch per clause, on literals" does not work: a clause of `k` literals needs a chain of `k` switches, an escape to the next clause on a true literal and `deny` after the last. The idea is right; the sentence is not.
- The correlated semantics is not defined. One line suffices: models with `W(n)=W(m)` when `cond(n)=cond(m)`.
- The statement gives hardness only, though membership in coNP is immediate. Say coNP-complete.
- "every rejection can be replayed" is true only with free stages. A witness through two switches with one condition name is unexecutable on the real engine. The checker can see this syntactically, and it says nothing. The same holds for one layer or rule used at two nodes.
- Fix: complete the statement, and emit a warning, or filter paths for the correlated-condition case, when a witness repeats a condition with opposite branches.

### Novelty

**11. [GAP] Novelty has improved, but its depth is unevenly distributed.**
Location: `thm:transitive`, Key claim of the title; `thm:complexity`; E15.
- **Better.** Credit depending on facts gives a monotone, non-distributive framework, so MOP and MFP really differ, and the paper's false-alarm phenomenon is real. The coNP-completeness and the tractable boundary are new for this domain, and the paper is candid about its lineage (Kildall, Kam-Ullman, Barnett-Leino).
- **Still thin.** Transitive (b) follows in two lines from `E` being indexed by node. Soundness of `chk*` fails because locality was put into the semantics; the theorem shows the two definitions differ. It is a fair formal statement of the thesis, not a discovery.
- It also formalizes a narrow reading. The informal argument quoted ("`L_1` trusts `L_2`, `L_2` trusts `L_3`") concerns guarantees, whereas `chk*` is about evidence inheritance. Inside the calculus, chains of credited gates are transitive: `g_1` provides what `g_2` needs, `g_2` provides what `g_3` needs. The title's claim is therefore "supplied evidence does not transfer; only a gate's guarantee does". Say this. It is more interesting than the slogan, and it connects to finding 1.
- **Practical reach.** The hardness needs gates consuming conjunctions of facts from different arms. E15 reports no escalation and no flow, and DevAgent has one fact. So the most novel results have no observed instance. The paper says "real hierarchies have few provided facts", which is an expectation.
- **Directions, any of which would raise my score:** implement the polynomial middle class and characterize the boundary as a dichotomy. Give a fixed-parameter bound, since the states are at most `|V|·2^{|G|+|F|}`. Prove a separation for the variants of transitive reasoning about guarantees. Synthesis of minimal stages or evidence is now closer, since `chk` is monotone.

### Minor

**12. [GAP] Route risk's premises remain stronger than used.** Decomposition over statistical routes needs neither the check nor contracts; only the claim `R_r = 0` on hard routes does. The paragraph "Note what the composition check does not do" says this in prose. The theorem could say it: premises (i) and (ii) restricted to hard routes, and passing the check only for those. This also tightens what deployers must argue.

**13. [TASTE] Make the middle-case remark a proposition.** It is correct and it is the sharpest boundary in the paper, yet it has no statement or proof, and the implementation stays exponential on it. If E9's exponential family is of this class, the reported blow-up is an artifact of the implementation, not of the problem.

**14. [TASTE] Realization differs between gates and guards.** A gate has `P(n) ⇒ R(n)`, a guard has `R(n)` unconditionally. A blocking guard must thus satisfy realization although nothing depends on it. It is harmless, since denied runs are irrelevant, but `V(n)≠blk ⇒ R(n)` is more uniform.

**15. [TASTE] E14's distribution is skewed.** 2,879 of 3,000 formulas are satisfiable, so the interesting case (unsatisfiable, false alarm repaired) rests on 121 instances. Sample near the phase transition.

**16. [TASTE] Report chk-only "accepted" versus proposal "accepted" with different words** throughout. The collision causes the ambiguity in finding 5, and it also appears in Theorem Order and Sec. Failures.

## Does the paper match the script?

Yes, closely, and much better than in round 7. Fig. 1 matches `wf_def`, the `chk` equations match `check_def`, and `chk*` matches `transitive_def`, with Order (a), (b) as `hoist`, `commute`. Exceptions: goal-less gates (finding 7) and the shared sort (disclosed).

## What would make this strong

1. Fix findings 1-4. Finding 1 matters most: the value proposition is the list of assumptions, and it is incomplete where the paper's new mechanism lives.
2. Replace Theorem Order with the local characterization of finding 5.
3. State "supplied evidence does not transfer; a gate's guarantee does" as the thesis, and prove it for guarantees too.
4. Port Theorems Soundness, Completeness and Exactness to a proof assistant, which is the paper's stated next step and now its largest remaining rigor gap.
