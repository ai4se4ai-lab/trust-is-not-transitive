# R3 — agent safety/security referee, round 3

## Five-sentence summary

TINT implements explicit delegation checks, typed effect mediation, restricted judge views, and whole-policy statistical certificates in a deliberately limited sandbox. The latest revision improves the accuracy of topology enforcement, theorem premises, timing descriptions, checker-case counts, and historical-version scope. Earlier corrections also resolve the misattribution of uncertified fixed-threshold search to the calibrated runtime and make the scripted mixture's decision behavior explicit. These are substantive reliability improvements, but no learned-guard, real-task, adaptive-security, or provenance-refinement evidence has been added. I retain a reject recommendation for a completed TSE article because practical security value and a sufficiently distinctive engineering contribution remain unestablished.

## Strengths and resolved issues

- The runtime now requires equality with its supported edge set, aligning its accepted configurations with the fixed topology it actually executes (`src/tint/runtime/__init__.py`).
- The escalation theorem explicitly requires measurable policies, integrable costs, and an appropriate finite multiplier satisfying feasibility/complementary slackness. It avoids claiming that these follow automatically from known risks.
- Timing columns identify p95, fixed comparator order and lack of dedicated warmup are disclosed, and historical measurements/certificates are explicitly separated from the bounded current scorer.
- Checker reporting distinguishes repeated evaluations from unique generated variants/graphs. This prevents sample-count inflation from implying specification diversity.
- Confidence-budget enforcement, bounded default lexical inputs, exact supported mandatory predicates, retained historical instruments, and the obligation-to-code table improve the assurance boundary.
- The manuscript preserves its primary zero-utility result, the exploratory amendment, encoded-family failure, and equal calibrated/uncalibrated decisions. Its ethics statement appropriately describes local mock effects and artificial secrets.

## Major concerns

1. **Guard-targeting realism remains a programmed counterexample rather than an empirical security result.**
   **Location:** Section II/Table I, Theorem 0, Section IV-A, Section V-C family analysis; `src/tint/layers/__init__.py`.
   **Why:** The exposed judge is programmed to accept an exact reviewer-targeting phrase; the structured judge cannot receive that phrase and applies deterministic feature rules. The analytic mixture results correctly expose that the measured advantage is built into the authored mechanism and prevalence. The canonical secret-egress example is also stopped by the ordinary mandatory sink. These observations demonstrate possible invalid composition, not realistic learned-judge susceptibility or mitigation effectiveness.
   **Resolution:** Keep the counterexample, but add independent attacks against real judges under matched mediation, tools, privileges, and budgets. Include semantic cases unresolved by the sink and authorized cases requiring context removed by quarantine.

2. **Incremental practical SE value and journal-level novelty are still insufficient.**
   **Location:** RQ III; Section V-C/Tables III–V/Figure 3; Discussion/Conclusion.
   **Why:** The primary policy denies every benign fixture; exploratory calibration changes applicability rather than the detector's decisions. The evaluated hierarchy combines a lexical scorer with a deterministic cutoff, while useful software work, patch correctness, developer effort, maintained-defense comparisons, inference costs, and deployment behavior remain unmeasured. Because the component assurance principles and mathematical ingredients are acknowledged established techniques, the integration needs evidence of a distinctive engineering benefit. Better reporting of a small fixture law does not supply that evidence.
   **Resolution:** Evaluate real proposal-generating agents on executable development tasks, including a hard sink plus one semantic classifier and a relevant maintained defense with equivalent authority. Compare task success at matched safety/utility points and measure actual inference costs. Alternatively pursue a narrower negative-feasibility or assurance-method contribution with corresponding publication scope.

3. **Real-agent provenance completeness and execution correspondence remain external assumptions.**
   **Location:** Definition 1, Section III-D/obligation table; `src/tint/model/__init__.py`, `src/tint/agents/__init__.py`.
   **Why:** Trusted host code supplies dependency sets; mock HTTP records identifiers and PATCH writes a constant. No exercised model-output flow establishes that every payload-bearing field and derived output acquires complete labels. The paper now states this boundary honestly, so this is an evidence gap rather than an observed sandbox bypass. Likewise, finite PRISM checks and the control-state argument do not prove refinement to a real adapter.
   **Resolution:** Specify field/output dependency semantics, demonstrate multi-step read/derive/write/send behavior through an actual adapter, and test omitted references, secret-bearing targets/arguments, and derived-output reuse. Provide a bounded adapter assurance or refinement argument before extending deterministic claims to external effects.

4. **Static workload certification does not establish protection against the described choosing attacker.**
   **Location:** Threat model, Section III-B, Section IV-A, Section V-C shift/search discussion.
   **Why:** An attacker can deliberately choose accepting families rather than sample the iid calibration mixture. The 20% joint-risk target, 25.40% unsafe fraction among accepted exploratory proposals, and 100% encoded-family acceptance underline this difference. Correctly relabeling fixed-threshold core search resolves attribution but supplies no adaptive robustness evidence. A context identifier still does not establish when deployment remains within calibration scope.
   **Resolution:** Define and evaluate context assignment, certificate expiration/recalibration, and handling of uncertifiable/adaptive traffic, including utility consequences. Add independently authored held-out families; otherwise retain a strictly static workload-certification claim.

## Minor concerns/questions
