## Remaining major concerns

1. **A sufficiently substantial new contribution remains unestablished.** Locations: introduction/RQs (`main.tex:68–126`), Theorems 0–4, discussion (`main.tex:976–1001`). The revised mathematics is largely sound in scope, but counterexamples with undischarged assumptions, correct-monitor composition, disjoint-event addition, and fixed-family binomial certification organize established ideas rather than establish a new formal capability. The manuscript acknowledges this, appropriately. Consequently, acceptance depends on evidence that their integration solves an engineering problem beyond a conventional reference monitor plus calibrated policy selection, and that evidence is still absent. **Resolution:** identify and demonstrate a distinctive executable capability or independently measured engineering benefit, with a matched comparator that retains mediation, structured views, and policy-bound certificates. Alternatively, substantially narrow the contribution and venue framing to an experience/feasibility report.

2. **The implementation correspondence is now clear but remains an inspection-level assurance argument.** Locations: `main.tex:470–516`, Table II; `src/tint/spec/__init__.py:80–120`; `src/tint/runtime/__init__.py`, constructor/evaluation; `formal/cascade.pm:4–17`; `formal/check.py:8–33`. The new table successfully separates declarations from facts; I no longer treat that separation as an undisclosed assumption. However, direct-edge checks still operate on declared sets, the dispatcher still executes a hardcoded topology, and the PRISM model encodes canonical trace outcomes without modeling certificate applicability, provenance propagation, or authenticated approvals. The independent Boolean enumeration defines execution to exclude `bad` and then checks precisely that exclusion. Neither the new hand-specified states nor these checks establish a refinement/preservation theorem about the implementation. A refinement proof is not universally required for an engineering paper, but without one the formal validation contributes limited independent support to a paper whose empirical substrate is also very small. **Resolution:** provide a bounded implementation-level verification, a checked semantic/model correspondence, or independent fault-oriented validation of meaningful cross-component obligations. Keep the current result explicitly at canonical-model/inspection scope, as the revision generally does.

3. **The revised evidence more clearly reveals a scripted mixture demonstration rather than realistic engineering evaluation.** Locations: evaluation design (`main.tex:526–560`), family table/population analysis (`main.tex:757–783`), shift/search discussion (`main.tex:800–812`), PDF pp. 5–8. Acceptance behavior is fixed within the authored families, so the reported risk improvements principally estimate known mixture proportions. The corrected calibrated and uncalibrated checked policies still have identical decisions; the primary policy still has zero utility; the shift family still fails completely. This is useful validation of accounting and fallback behavior, but it does not answer whether formal assumptions hold in practice or whether the hierarchy improves actual task performance. The added disclosures resolve attribution concerns, not this evidence gap. **Resolution:** narrow RQ III and the claimed engineering contribution to certification mechanics, or add independently specified tasks and learned guards with a controlled integration comparison. No additional resampling of this fixture law would resolve the substantive limitation.
