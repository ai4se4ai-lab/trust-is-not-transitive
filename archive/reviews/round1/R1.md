# R1: Formal-methods review

## Scope and independence

I inspected the 11-page `paper/main.pdf` with PyMuPDF, checked definitions and proofs against `paper/main.tex`, and examined relevant implementation, formal-model, test, and result files. I did not read project logs, decisions, prompting documents, existing reviews, or other reviewers' reports. I made no source, data, or manuscript changes. I ran `tests/test_core.py` with bytecode and pytest cache generation disabled: **52 tests passed**. I inspected the retained PRISM output rather than rerunning PRISM; I did not rerun the experiments or independently verify the cited literature.

## Five-sentence summary

The paper proposes TINT, a restricted tool-agent middleware that combines mandatory deterministic mediation, explicitly declared trust edges, structured judge views, and configuration-bound statistical certificates. It distinguishes invalid assumption discharge from a failure of assume-guarantee logic, and distinguishes early acceptance from all-veto probability semantics. Its core safety argument assumes a correct completely mediating sink monitor and simultaneous bounds on disjoint unsafe-acceptance events, from which deterministic safety and a route-sum risk bound follow. The artifact implements a fixed topology with handwritten scoring and scripted judges, and its primary certified configuration denies every benign task while a separately disclosed arithmetic correction produces exploratory utility. The work is unusually candid and useful as a reproducible feasibility exercise, but it does not yet establish a sufficiently substantial formal or software-engineering contribution for TSE.

## Strengths

- The probability accounting is careful: joint unsafe acceptance, attack-conditioned misses, and unsafe acceptance conditional on allowance are distinguished; the unsafe-conditional product includes prevalence; route bounds are not confused with veto intersections.
- Theorem 1's probabilistic argument is valid under its stated simultaneous-certificate assumptions, and no independence assumption is needed for its union correction. The iid restriction for the implemented binomial certificate is appropriately stronger than general exchangeability.
- The common-shock mixture is explicitly model-specific, and the parity construction correctly demonstrates the insufficiency of pairwise independence for higher-order failure probabilities.
- The implementation binds a certificate to the declared policy configuration and checks it again during evaluation. Exact-action/epoch approval and conservative unknown-reference labels are sensible restricted implementation choices.
- The stronger naive-with-sink comparator and equal-threshold uncalibrated comparator prevent attribution of complete mediation or unchanged semantic detection to the new certification mechanism.
- The primary failure, correction, missing historical synthesis counts, distribution-shift failure, absence of real models, and absence of a refinement proof are disclosed prominently rather than buried.

## Major concerns

1. **The formal contribution is predominantly an organization of standard observations, while the integration benefit remains unestablished.** Locations: introduction (`main.tex:68–100`), Theorems 0–4 (`main.tex:278–446`), related-work discussion and conclusion; PDF pp. 1–4 and 8–9. The counterexample deliberately violates a component assumption and removes the mandatory sink; Theorem 1 assumes precisely the monitor correctness and route-risk facts needed for the conclusion; the remaining probability and optimization arguments are elementary consequences of intersection containment, disjointness, conditioning, and Lagrangian minimization. The manuscript honestly disclaims mathematical novelty, so I do not regard those disclaimers as an error, but the resulting contribution must be carried by a demonstrably useful integration. The primary result has zero utility, and the corrected calibrated method makes the same decisions as its uncalibrated checked control. **Resolution:** identify a concrete new executable obligation or compositional capability unavailable in the closest approaches and demonstrate its engineering benefit with an appropriate controlled comparison; otherwise recast this as a smaller feasibility/experience contribution rather than a completed TSE research contribution.

2. **The bridge from declared trust obligations to actual executable guarantees is too weak for the central assurance narrative.** Locations: Definitions 6–7 and Section III-A (`main.tex:211–241, 267–275, 299–331`); implementation limits (`main.tex:457–472`); `src/tint/spec/__init__.py:80–120`; `src/tint/runtime/__init__.py`, constructor and `evaluate`. The DSL checker validates sets of string-valued requires/provides/evidence declarations and selected interface tags; it does not establish that a caller actually produces the evidence, that a callee's implementation satisfies its guarantee, or a semantic implication between contracts. The runtime hardcodes the three-layer topology and implements metadata, interface, and fingerprint checks directly, rather than executing general edge-specific evidence discharge. These restrictions are disclosed, but a reader cannot derive which formal premise is actually guaranteed by which runtime operation, and which merely survives as a trusted-host assertion. **Resolution:** provide an obligation-to-code correspondence table and a small operational semantics distinguishing syntactic compatibility, runtime evidence validation, and external assumptions; prove a preservation result for the implemented dispatcher or demonstrate independent faults that the executable trust checks catch beyond the fixed sink and version checks. Do not describe set membership alone as semantic assumption discharge.

3. **The formal validation does not substantially validate the implementation or the composition machinery.** Locations: `main.tex:465–467, 535, 573–582`; PDF pp. 5–6; `formal/cascade.pm:4–17`; `formal/check.py:8–33`; retained `results/raw/prism.json`. The PRISM model has a trace identifier and a checked Boolean, with rejection of the two bad canonical traces directly encoded in its transition relation. It models neither provenance propagation, exact approvals, trust-edge evidence, certificates, nor score routing, and contains no nontrivial probabilistic transition. The independent Boolean check defines `execute = semantic_allow and not bad` and then asserts that execution is not bad; this is a useful sanity check but offers little independent assurance. The manuscript correctly admits the absence of a refinement link, so this is a limitation in evidential strength rather than a hidden false claim. **Resolution:** model the meaningful obligation state and failure modes, derive model instances from policy/runtime semantics with a checked correspondence, or provide a bounded implementation-level verification of the dispatcher and effect interpreter. Keep the current PRISM result labeled as canonical counterexample replay and avoid letting the mere presence of a probabilistic model checker imply implementation verification.

4. **Theorem 3's existence wording is stronger than its assumptions justify.** Location: `main.tex:392–410`, PDF p. 4; corresponding T3 in `formal/FRAMEWORK.md`. Pointwise minimization for a fixed finite multiplier and the subsequent sufficient optimality condition are correct, but known risks and positive incremental cost alone do not ensure that an optimum admits such a multiplier. For example, let the trusted view be uniform on `(0,1)`, take `r0(x)=x`, `r1(x)=0`, `d(x)=1`, and require risk at most zero: feasibility forces escalation almost surely, whereas every finite multiplier leaves a positive-measure interval with cheap acceptance and positive risk. The proof refers to a 'stated existence premise', but that premise is not explicit in the theorem's initial 'admits' assertion. **Resolution:** state the result solely as conditional sufficient optimality given a measurable policy and multiplier satisfying feasibility and complementary slackness, or supply conditions ensuring multiplier existence and attainment; distinguish that result from the implemented finite-family optimization. This is a repairable theorem-statement issue, not a refutation of the implemented finite selector.

5. **The research questions outstrip both the formal semantics and the empirical substrate.** Locations: RQs (`main.tex:103–126`), design (`main.tex:482–516`), results and limitations; PDF pp. 2 and 5–9. The framework's general hierarchy language becomes a fixed three-layer implementation with two hardcoded predicates, while all semantic labels, scoring, proposal families, and judges are authored within one fixture system. Thus the artifact can demonstrate consistency of selected checks but cannot establish decomposition of realistic agent policies, useful risk/latency/cost optimization, or that the key assumptions hold in practice. Negative results are valuable, but here they leave the paper's principal engineering-value question unanswered. **Resolution:** narrow the RQs to the claims actually answered, or add at least one realistic independently specified task/policy setting with a learned guard and meaningful implementation-fault tests; include controls specifically isolating executable obligation checking from mediation and structured scoring. The existing transparency does not replace this evidence.

## Minor concerns

- Theorem 4 calls the route-sum bound tight without the theorem statement's qualification `sum b_i <= 1`; the proof supplies it. State the universally tight bound as `min(1, sum b_i)` or put the qualification in the statement (`main.tex:428–439`).
- Clarify that the selector minimizes observed calibration mean cost among certified candidates, rather than certified population expected cost: `src/tint/synthesis/__init__.py:16–32` uses the sample mean. The known-cost finite LP is a separate result (`main.tex:419–426`).
- The statement that unsupported predicates are rejected needs a precise syntactic scope: `spec.check` requires `rules` to equal the two supported predicates but only requires `mandatory` to contain them, permitting extra unknown mandatory names (`src/tint/spec/__init__.py:84–87`). Reject unknown mandatory declarations too, or document that they carry no enforcement semantics.
- System II is defined to require timeout handling, but the synchronous runtime calls the scorer/judge without enforcing an elapsed-time deadline; its exception tests simulate failure. Clarify resource/liveness claims and identify which layer is responsible for actual deadlines (Definition 4; `main.tex:462–463`; `safe_judge_call`).
- The title risks suggesting a new general non-transitivity result despite the careful qualification in the text. A title emphasizing certificate applicability and scoped mediation would better match the contribution.
- Some related-work paragraphs describe the authors' verification process rather than the intellectual relationship among results. The foundational contract/reference-monitor lineage needs explicit scholarly treatment, which the paper itself acknowledges.

## Questions for the authors

1. Which executed check discharges a substantive per-edge premise that would remain unchecked in a conventional mandatory reference monitor plus a version-bound whole-policy certificate?
2. What trusted evidence semantics, if any, exists beyond equality/membership of declared strings, and how does a host associate an evidence name with an authenticated fact?
3. Can the formal assurance argument be instantiated directly for the implemented fixed topology, including certificate denial and fallback, without treating the whole dispatcher as a correct monitor by assumption?
4. Is Theorem 3 intended only as sufficient optimality conditional on an existing multiplier? If so, will the statement remove its unconditional existence wording?
5. What result would distinguish the integration from ordinary complete mediation plus calibrated policy selection in an independently specified realistic task?

## Unverifiable or explicitly unestablished claims

- I did not independently establish the chronology of the local preregistration tag or the historical execution environment; the manuscript appropriately does not claim independent timestamping.
- I inspected saved PRISM transcripts but did not independently reproduce the PRISM invocation or validate its binary provenance.
- I did not independently verify literature capabilities, priority claims, source licenses, remote repository publication, or archival availability. These are not treated as established by this review.
- The primary synthesis records omit the calibration counts needed to recompute those particular bounds; the manuscript discloses this and the amended records improve the situation.
- Correctness of arbitrary plugin implementations, faithful implementation version identities, calibration/deployment stationarity, semantic-oracle validity outside the authored fixtures, and realistic agent efficacy are not established by the fingerprint or the local tests. The paper generally states these limitations accurately.
- No proof of Python/PRISM refinement or learned-judge semantic correctness is present; I found no basis to infer either from the successful finite checks.
