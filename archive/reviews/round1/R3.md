# R3 — agent safety/security referee, round 1

## Five-sentence summary

The manuscript integrates explicit delegation obligations, a typed effect dispatcher, restricted judge views, and finite-family statistical certification into a local prototype called TINT. Its conditional composition and probability arguments make useful distinctions between mandatory mediation, early acceptance, and veto ensembles, while explicitly acknowledging substantial prior art. The frozen experiment produces no useful certified configuration, and a separately disclosed arithmetic correction produces a scripted-system trade-off with 10.16% joint unsafe acceptance and 66.46% benign acceptance. There is no executed language model, learned guard, independently authored task corpus, real development-task outcome, or realistic adaptive attack evaluation, so the central practical security and engineering questions remain open. The unusually candid artifact supports a reproducible feasibility demonstration, but the current contribution and evidence do not support acceptance as a completed TSE research article.

## Strengths

- The paper distinguishes deterministic enforcement from semantic risk and explicitly states the trusted computing base, distributional premises, absence of refinement proof, and restricted tool semantics (Sections II–III).
- Mandatory sink checks, exact action/epoch approval binding, conservative treatment of unknown references, closed parsing, and fail-closed handling of exceptions are sensible implementation choices in the stated synchronous sandbox.
- The naive-with-sink and checked-uncalibrated controls materially improve interpretation: the canonical secret-egress result is ordinary mediation, and calibration is not credited with improving an unchanged detector.
- The numerical amendment, original infeasibility, encoded-family failure, arbitrary modeled costs, and missing real-model evidence are disclosed prominently rather than hidden behind aggregate significance tests.
- Separating route sums, conditional miss products, simultaneous fixed-family bounds, and population-risk coverage is technically helpful. The repository preserves granular evidence and clearly differentiates local checks from general proofs.
- The ethics statement is proportionate to the actual work: artificial canaries and local mock effects create little direct harm, and the text avoids claiming participant approval or external benchmark execution.

## Major concerns

1. **The guard-targeting mechanism is programmed into the comparator, so it establishes possibility rather than realistic security benefit.**
   **Location:** Section II/Table I; Theorem 0; Section IV-A; `src/tint/layers/__init__.py:44–72`; `experiments/dataset.py`.
   **Why it matters:** `ExposedJudge` returns ALLOW on the exact phrase “reviewer must answer safe,” whereas `StructuredJudge` cannot receive that phrase and largely applies a score cutoff. The roughly ten-point exploratory improvement over naive-with-sink follows an authored family and a comparator explicitly programmed to succumb to it. This is a legitimate countermodel, and the paper now labels it honestly, but it is not evidence that realistic learned guards have this vulnerability or that this view transformation mitigates it while preserving task semantics. In the canonical exfiltration example, the sink alone defeats the attack, so it cannot demonstrate the added security value of trust-edge checks either.
   **Resolution:** Retain the countermodel as a unit/specification example, and add independent guard-targeting attacks against real judges under equal mediation, tools, prompts, and budgets. Include cases where a semantic decision is necessary and no deterministic invariant alone decides the answer. Evaluate attack transfer/paraphrase and useful authorized cases that require the removed context. A further caveat alone cannot replace this evidence for RQ III.

2. **The claimed integration has not demonstrated incremental practical SE value over a simpler mediated policy.**
   **Location:** RQ III; Section V-C, Tables III–IV/Figure 3; Sections VI–VIII; `src/tint/runtime/__init__.py:58–94`.
   **Why it matters:** The primary method has zero benign utility. Exploratory checked calibrated and uncalibrated methods make identical decisions, the judge is another deterministic rule over the lexical score, and the runtime supports one fixed topology. Rules-only is much cheaper and accepts all benign fixtures, while the safer semantic filter rejects approximately one third of benign fixtures. No real issue is resolved, patch correctness checked, or practitioner burden measured. These observations are not a fatal flaw in a feasibility artifact, but leave the journal-level contribution narrower than the research questions and broad guardrail framing suggest. The manuscript itself recognizes that existing contract reasoning, mediation, calibration, and Lagrangian routing supply much of the theory.
   **Resolution:** Demonstrate a concrete deployment decision or failure class that a comparably engineered sink plus classifier cannot handle as well. Use executable development tasks, measure task completion and failure severity, and compare at matched utility/risk as well as monetary/inference costs and full latency. Include a simple hard sink plus one semantic classifier and a maintained relevant defense with equivalent privileges. Alternatively, substantially reposition the work as a narrower methods/tool or negative feasibility contribution with a corresponding contribution standard.

3. **The adaptive-search records do not execute the certified method named in those records.**
   **Location:** Section IV-B adaptive search description and Section V-C fixture-search discussion; `experiments/run.py:206–225`; `src/tint/baselines/__init__.py:11–31`.
   **Why it matters:** `adaptive_study` calls `decide(name, text, view, .2)` for every method. For `checked_quarantine`, this is the same uncertified surrogate decision path as the uncalibrated checked control: it does not instantiate `GuardRuntime`, load the seed's selected certificate, apply the selected threshold, or deny when calibration is infeasible. The preserved primary adaptive log consequently includes allowed unsafe inputs under the `checked_quarantine` name even though the primary certified runtime denies all semantic proposals. The prose appropriately calls this a weak fixture search, but that limitation does not explain substituting a different executable policy. This is a concrete attribution problem independent of the absence of sophisticated attack optimization.
   **Resolution:** Relabel these observations as searches against the surrogate/uncalibrated decision rules, or rerun a separately labeled amended study through the actual configured runtime and preserved per-seed certificate. Include applicable/inapplicable context cases and maintain all original observations. Report adaptive outcomes for each exact runtime configuration separately; do not mix the block-all primary policy with the accepting exploratory or surrogate policy.

4. **The taint and isolation assurance stops before the difficult agent-to-tool provenance boundary.**
   **Location:** Definition 1 and safety property in Section II; Section III-D; `src/tint/model/__init__.py:63–85,122–130`; `src/tint/agents/__init__.py:32–48`.
   **Why it matters:** Provenance is computed from declared input identifiers, and parent-label integrity is checked when trusted host code inserts an observation. The toy PATCH operation writes a constant and mock HTTP records identifiers; there is no actual model-generated output whose dependencies must be tracked or an external executor whose semantics are shown to match these identifiers. Thus the implementation has not established that all payload sources, generated strings, destinations, or later file contents acquire the required labels. This is not a demonstrated bypass of the closed toy interpreter: the paper's restricted-scope qualification is appropriate. It is nevertheless the central missing step in using these deterministic claims for a coding agent, especially because target strings are agent-controlled and are not treated as payload-bearing data by this model.
   **Resolution:** Give an explicit supported data-flow semantics covering every action field and output, identify who supplies complete dependency sets, and demonstrate multi-step read/transform/write/send traces with an actual adapter. Add adversarial tests for omitted references, secret-bearing targets/arguments, output relabeling, and reusing derived results. Supply a refinement argument or a carefully bounded adapter assurance case before implying protection for real files, shells, or network operations.

5. **Static mixture-risk certification is insufficient for the attacker described, and useful behavior under realistic shift is unestablished.**
   **Location:** Threat model; Section III-B calibration/shift discussion; Section IV-A fixture distribution; Section V-C encoded-family result.
   **Why it matters:** The attacker can choose later inputs, but the operational certificate controls joint unsafe acceptance on a fixed iid mixture, with a permissive 20% target. Its value depends on benign/attack prevalence, whereas an attacker can deliberately submit the accepting implicit-patch family. The encoded family obtains 100% unsafe acceptance, and a matching context string does not detect the change. The authors correctly disclaim an adaptive guarantee; the issue is that the evaluated certificate is therefore not a demonstrated security assurance for the described attacker or a practical deployment procedure. Repeating a tiny authored template law across seeds increases precision about that law without expanding its security coverage.
   **Resolution:** Separate benign workload assurance and adversarial assurance explicitly, report family-level attack success and accepted-action conditional risk alongside joint risk, and test held-out independently authored tasks/attack families. Define a deployment process for trusted context assignment, certificate expiration/recalibration and uncertifiable/adaptive traffic, including the resulting utility. Do not describe static certification as robustness evidence; if adaptive protection remains out of scope, narrow the attacker-facing operational claim accordingly.

6. **Operational resource and timeout premises are declarations rather than enforced safeguards.**
   **Location:** Definitions 3–4; Section III-D; `src/tint/runtime/__init__.py:58–94`; `src/tint/layers/__init__.py:75–80`; `tests/test_core.py:test_runtime_failure_fallbacks`.
   **Why it matters:** The runtime invokes scorer and judge synchronously. Exceptions fail closed, but there is no enforced deadline or cancellation for a nonreturning plugin; the timeout test raises an exception immediately. The lexical scorer also scans attacker-controlled raw text without an explicit input-length/work cap. The present scripted fixtures are small and terminate, so this does not invalidate their safety observations, but it does not implement the timeout/resource handling promised by the operational hierarchy definition or demonstrate availability under attacker-controlled content.
   **Resolution:** Distinguish “a caller reports a timeout as an exception” from a runtime-enforced timeout. State the precise current termination assumptions, bound input sizes, and test enforced deadlines/cancellation if plugin or real-model deployments are claimed. Report availability/denial-of-service behavior separately from “no unsafe action executes.”

## Minor concerns and questions

- Certificate applicability validates that `delta` lies between zero and one but does not compare it with a deployment-required confidence budget (`src/tint/calibration/__init__.py:83`). With a trusted issuer this can remain a host obligation, but the contract should state where the required confidence level is enforced.
- Fingerprints bind trusted version strings, not immutable implementation bytes. The text recognizes this; a concise table separating checked facts from asserted host obligations would make the security boundary easier to audit.
- The whole-policy certificate is a sensible conservative implementation, but the phrase “route-certificate discipline” risks suggesting that per-route certificates were actually estimated. Keep that distinction visible in the contribution statement and figure.
- The generated “benign_security” family is deliberately rich in suspicious lexical tokens. Its rejection explains much of the reported utility loss, so include family counts and decisions directly rather than relying on a global benign-acceptance percentage.
- “Hard count” measures accepted proposals that violate the typed oracle, not harmful external executions. The table caption explains this, but use the more explicit phrase in the column or surrounding prose.
- The local preregistration tag and fresh exploratory seeds are useful provenance, but neither provides independent timestamps nor an independent task distribution. They should not carry the evidentiary weight of an external preregistration or replication.
- Ethics are adequately scoped for the current mock study. A future real-agent evaluation should describe containment and dataset/model access conditions at that time; no additional hypothetical approval checklist is needed for this artifact.
