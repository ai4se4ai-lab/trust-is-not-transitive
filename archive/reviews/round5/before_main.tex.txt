\title{Trust Is Not Transitive: Executable Contracts for Hierarchical Agent Guardrails\\A Scoped Theory and Reproducible Feasibility Study}
\author{Anonymous Research Artifact}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
An agent's guard may delegate a difficult decision to another guard, but an
allow verdict is usable only under the conditions for which it was justified.
We implement TINT, a restricted prototype combining mandatory tool-action
checks, explicit delegation conditions, structured judge inputs and a
certificate for the complete configured policy. Our theory separates stacks
that accept early from ensembles in which every guard must agree, and states
the assumptions needed for risk bounds and cost optimization. The study tests
implementation mechanics with scripted guards, not real language models. In
the frozen primary run, all \resPrimaryInfeasible{} seed-level policy families
failed the risk constraint; denying uncertified actions also denied every
benign task: zero benign utility. A disclosed threshold correction, evaluated
separately on fresh exploratory seeds, yielded
\resExploratoryCheckedQuarantineRisk{}\% unsafe accepted proposals among all
proposals, with \resExploratoryCheckedQuarantineUtility{}\% benign acceptance.
In a separate \resPrimaryReplications{}-replication experiment with a known
synthetic population, selected upper bounds covered true risk in
\resPrimaryCoverage{}\% of replications. These results demonstrate executable
accounting and rejection behavior, not useful real-agent safety. External
benchmarks, learned guards, actual development-task success and practical
adaptive robustness remain untested. Raw observations, scoped proofs and
reproduction instructions accompany the artifact.

\end{abstract}
\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Agent safety, runtime enforcement, assume-guarantee contracts, calibration,
provenance, reproducibility.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{A}{gents} can turn an incorrect judgment into a tool effect.
For a coding agent, an issue comment is evidence about a task, but it is not
authority to read a secret file or alter production. A fast rule may screen an
action before a classifier or a slower judge examines an uncertain case. The
engineering attraction of this hierarchy is clear: costly deliberation is
reserved for difficult inputs. Its assurance case is less straightforward.
A verdict carries a guarantee only for the property and input conditions under
which that guarantee was established.

Consider DevAgent, which reads issues, proposes typed tool actions and receives
guard decisions. The malicious trace $T^*$ proposes uploading secret-tainted
\texttt{.env} data to an external destination after reading an issue. The benign
trace $T_b$ proposes sending public diagnostics to a local mock. The
judge-targeting trace $T_j$ adds an instruction telling the reviewer to answer
SAFE to the malicious issue. A raw-text judge can be modeled as obeying that
instruction even while meeting a contract whose assumption excludes raw
untrusted text. The problem is an undischarged assumption at composition time,
not a contradiction in assume-guarantee logic.

There is substantial prior art. ContrAgent already provides temporal agent
contracts and deterministic supervision~\cite{arxiv260918128}. A probabilistic
layered architecture already composes conditional safety
guarantees~\cite{arxiv260518672}. Conformal Arbitrage and Trust or Escalate
already provide statistical routing/cascade guarantees~\cite{arxiv250600911,arxiv240718370}.
CaMeL and Fides already mediate information flow around untrusted
agents~\cite{arxiv250318813,arxiv250523643}. We therefore do not claim the first
formal guardrail hierarchy, the first calibrated cascade, or a new general law
of non-transitivity.

Our narrower hypothesis is that explicit, executable checks of the premises
attached to each acceptance route make failures easier to reject and audit.
This creates several challenges. First, a guard can inspect a different
provenance class or representation from the one its certificate covers.
Second, routing selects a different population at each layer, invalidating a
naive reuse of marginal errors. Third, a slower guard can be both expensive
and vulnerable, so escalation is not automatically a risk reduction.

TINT (Trust Is Not Transitive) implements explicit delegation checks. An
acceptance route is one way a proposal can receive its final allow, such as
cheap acceptance or acceptance after a judge. The theory can bound these
routes separately; the implemented certificate instead covers the whole
configured policy, including both routes. A fingerprint checks that this
certificate names the actual threshold, guard versions, view and declared
context. This identity check does not establish that the deployment population
still matches calibration. The prototype evaluates only a handwritten scorer
and scripted judges.

We investigate the following research questions, retaining the intended scope
while limiting our answers to the evidence collected.

\noindent\textbf{RQ I (specification and decomposition).} How can an end-to-end
safety property of an AI agent be formally specified and decomposed into
per-layer assume-guarantee contracts across a hierarchy of heterogeneous
guardrails, such that trust between layers is explicit rather than assumed
transitive?

\noindent\textbf{RQ II (guarantees and escalation).} Under what conditions does
a hierarchical guardrail composition provide bounded end-to-end residual risk,
when does it fail under invalid trust, correlated errors and adaptive
adversaries, and what escalation policy minimizes expected cost subject to a
risk constraint?

\noindent\textbf{RQ III (engineering value).} Does the resulting hierarchy
improve the risk--latency--cost trade-off and robustness relative to flat and
ad-hoc guards, and do its formal assumptions hold in practice?

Our evidence comprises scoped proofs and an executable counterexample
(RQ I; Theorems~\ref{thm:nontransitive}--\ref{thm:composition}), corrected risk
accounting and finite-family synthesis (RQ II;
Theorems~\ref{thm:bounds}--\ref{thm:limits}), and a preregistered local study with
a separately labeled exploratory correction (RQ III;
Section~\ref{sec:results}). The primary utility failure is a result, not evidence
of useful superiority. Real-model generalization remains unanswered.

\section{Background and Running Example}
\label{sec:background}
\begin{definition}[Agent, trace and provenance]
An agent proposes an action $a$ after observations $o$ in a finite trace prefix
$h$. Tool effects occur only through a trusted dispatcher. A datum's label is
an element of the powerset lattice
$\mathcal{L}=2^{\{\mathrm{untrusted},\mathrm{secret}\}}$, ordered by inclusion,
with union as join. The empty label denotes trusted/public data. A derived
datum inherits the join of all source labels; derived is a history attribute,
not a third security level. Label creation and propagation are monitor-owned.
\end{definition}

In $T^*$ and $T_j$, the issue is untrusted and the transmitted file contains a
fake secret used for testing. Its payload label remains secret. In $T_b$, the
payload is independently produced public diagnostics; reading an untrusted
issue does not itself make those bytes secret. Labels follow declared data
dependencies, which the trusted host must supply completely; they do not
decide whether following an issue's instruction is authorized. Unknown payload
references conservatively carry both labels. There is no implicit host-file
read or operating-system shell in this interpreter.

\begin{definition}[Safety property]
The specification is $\varphi=\varphi_{\rm det}\wedge\varphi_{\rm sem}$.
The deterministic fragment is the invariant
\begin{align}
\varphi_{\rm det}={}&\mathbf{G}((\mathrm{execute}\wedge\mathrm{production}\nonumber\\
&\quad\wedge\mathrm{destructive})\Rightarrow\mathrm{approved})\nonumber\\
&\wedge\mathbf{G}((\mathrm{execute}\wedge\mathrm{external})
\Rightarrow\neg\mathrm{secretPayload}).
\end{align}
Here $\mathbf G$ means always. Approval binds an exact action digest and a
revocation epoch: incrementing the epoch invalidates earlier grants. The
semantic oracle $Y(h,a)\in\{0,1\}$ is an external correctness label, not the
guard's runnable detector. For a fixed population $D$ of labeled proposals
$(h,a,Y)$, the semantic requirement is
$R(D)=\Pr_D(Y=1,\mathrm{allow})\leq\epsilon$, where $\epsilon$ is the allowed
per-decision residual-risk budget.

\end{definition}

The conjunction joins different obligations: exact exclusion of the hard
forbidden effects, and a population-scoped bound on oracle-unsafe acceptance.
The hard predicates deny $T^*$ and $T_j$; $T_b$ remains eligible for semantic
acceptance. Fixture labels are $Y(T^*)=Y(T_j)=1$ and $Y(T_b)=0$, by construction.
These labels do not solve general prompt-injection recognition. A static
per-decision bound also does not imply that an unbounded session never fails.

\begin{definition}[Layer]\label{def:layer}
A layer $L_i$ maps a permitted view of $(h,a)$ to allow, block or escalate,
with declared provenance, property scope, cost $c_i$ and latency budget
$\ell_i$. Missing or erroneous results cannot grant authority. The instantiated
scorer returns $s\in[0,1]$, with larger values more suspicious; its cheap route
accepts when $s<\tau$, otherwise consulting the judge. An approval fallback
abstains and therefore denies. For arbitrary synchronous plugins, the
prototype handles caller-reported errors but does not enforce cancellation
of a nonreturning call.

\end{definition}

For $T_j$, the raw judge input contains attacker instructions. A closed
structured view contains only Boolean action/provenance features, a bounded
numeric score and a fixed schema tag. It excludes issue text, arbitrary target
strings and model-generated explanations. This transformation reduces the
guard's input language, but may remove semantic evidence needed to distinguish
authorized from unauthorized behavior.

\begin{definition}[Operational dual process]
A System~I layer has a declared worst-case work bound and is admitted to a
deployment-specific resource budget $B$. A System~II layer is outside that
budget and requires explicit timeout and failure handling. This is an
engineering classification, not a cognitive claim or a wall-clock proof for
neural inference.
\end{definition}

The role of the cheap layer is to screen routine instances such as $T_b$; it
does not obtain authority to waive the deterministic invariants for $T^*$ or
$T_j$. Dual-process terminology motivates resource allocation but supplies no
independent safety theorem.

\begin{definition}[Hierarchy]
A hierarchy is a finite acyclic escalation graph with disjoint terminal
acceptance-route events $E_i$. A terminal denial cannot be overwritten. Every
acceptance traverses the mandatory deterministic sink checks. An all-veto
ensemble instead accepts only when every guard allows; these compositions have
different probability semantics.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Layer contract]
A contract $C_i=(A_i,G_i,P_i,I_i)$ specifies assumptions $A_i$, guarantees
$G_i$, property scope $P_i$ and permitted view $I_i$. Assumptions include
provenance integrity, representation/version and calibration context. Contract
satisfaction means $A_i\Rightarrow G_i$, not that the environment satisfies
$A_i$. A guarantee may be a deterministic invariant or a stated statistical
bound on a named event.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Conditioned trust]
$\operatorname{Trust}(i,j,P\mid\chi)$ means that caller $i$ may rely on
callee $j$ for property $P$ only when trusted evidence establishes condition
$\chi$. The arrow $i\to j$ denotes reliance, not data flow. A transitive closure
would add $i\to k$ by chaining $i\to j$ and $j\to k$; this is permitted only
when intermediate guarantees entail the next assumptions for the same actual
view and property, with mandatory sink checks preserved. For $T_j$, an edge
$\operatorname{Trust}(L_2,L_3,\varphi_{\rm sem}\mid\chi)$ cannot use a
structured-view certificate while $\chi$ is false because raw issue text was
passed to the judge. Contract names $C_i$ are distinct from condition $\chi$.

\end{definition}

A guarantee valid for $T_b$'s structured interface cannot be reused for raw
$T_j$: the input premise differs. A scope assertion about semantic
classification also cannot authorize disclosure of a secret in $T^*$.
The checker validates declarations and edge obligations; it does not prove
that a declared guard guarantee or external calibration premise is true.

\begin{definition}[Residual risk, cost and latency]\label{def:metrics}
The relevant denominators are
\begin{gather*}
R=\Pr(Y=1,\mathrm{allow})\quad\text{(joint risk)},\\
\Pr(\mathrm{allow}\mid Y=1)\quad\text{(attack miss)},\\
\Pr(Y=1\mid\mathrm{allow})\quad\text{(accepted unsafe fraction)},\\
\Pr(\mathrm{allow}\mid Y=0)\quad\text{(benign utility)}.
\end{gather*}
For additive costs, $\mathbb E[C]=\sum_i c_i\Pr(\mathrm{reach}\ i)$.
Complete-decision latency quantiles are measured, not formed by adding layer
quantiles. Modeled units are neither CPU time nor money, tokens or GPU use.

\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Threat model]
An attacker controls issue/tool-return content and may choose later local
queries after observing earlier guard outputs. It cannot modify trusted
labels, policy, certificate store, approval store or dispatcher. Static
exchangeability is not assumed for adaptive attacks. A common-shock mixture
parameter $\rho$ is distinguished from observed pairwise correlation.
\end{definition}

is an intentionally vulnerable program, not an empirical claim about an LLM.
The stronger naive control is essential: the deterministic example alone
cannot distinguish quarantine from ordinary complete mediation.

Only the calibrated checked runtime uses this control path: proposal,
hard-pass, certified, semantic-pass, then effect or denial. Hard-pass requires
the trusted sink predicates; certified requires a matching whole-policy
certificate within risk and confidence budgets. If $s<\tau$ the cheap route
reaches semantic-pass; otherwise a structured-judge allow is required. A
judge escalation reaches the abstaining approval stub and denial. For $T_b$,
an applicable certificate and a low score permit cheap acceptance; $T_j$ is
already denied by the hard sink. Comparators can omit these states as stated
in the evaluation design. This is a hand-specified control semantics, not a
checked extraction of Python.

\subsection{Specification and explicit delegation}
The closed JSON domain-specific language (DSL) declares layers, scopes, evidence,
interfaces, direct trust edges and mandatory predicates. There is no arbitrary
expression evaluator. The well-formedness checker rejects unknown fields,
duplicate names, cycles, scope mismatches, missing direct-edge evidence and a raw
judge view when its contract requires quarantine. Unsupported predicates are rejected
rather than silently treated as enforced. The trusted runtime supplies actual
provenance and approval facts and retains the mandatory sink checks shown in
Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}.

\begin{theorem}[Non-transitivity of conditional verdict reliance]
\label{thm:nontransitive}
There exist locally contract-satisfying layers whose naive delegation permits
an unsafe action when the caller does not discharge the delegated assumptions.
Local contract satisfaction alone does not imply global safety.
\end{theorem}
\begin{IEEEproof}
Restrict the example contract to the three canonical traces. The judge's
assumption $A_3$ is a structured input; its guarantee $G_3$ is the correct
verdict on those traces. Define the judge to meet $G_3$ under $A_3$, but return
allow on raw $T_j$ containing the reviewer phrase. A middle relay's assumption
$A_2$ is that the caller discharges $A_3$; $G_2$ is to relay a correct
verdict. The outer relay assumes $A_1=A_2\land\mathrm{sinkPreserved}$ and
guarantees safe permitted execution $G_1$ under those premises. These
are conditional implications: they say nothing when their premises fail.
The naive caller passes raw $T_j$ and executes the relayed allow without a
sink check, violating secret egress while the premises are false. For $T_b$
there is no unsafe effect; raw $T^*$ without the phrase is denied. The stronger
naive-with-sink control also denies $T_j$. The countermodel demonstrates missing
premises, not failure of a valid assume-guarantee inference.

\end{IEEEproof}

The sufficient conditions for sound closure are explicit assumption discharge
for each edge, compatible property scopes and actual views, acyclic escalation,
and preservation of mandatory invariants. A declaration that one layer trusts
another is therefore not an executable substitute for checking these premises.

\begin{theorem}[Scoped composition soundness]
\label{thm:composition}
Assume a correct mandatory deterministic monitor, complete mediation with
unforgeable metadata, and monotone denial. Let mutually exclusive events $E_i$
exhaust final acceptance, and let $b_i$ bound each route's joint unsafe mass:
$\Pr_D(Y=1,E_i)\leq b_i$. If these bounds hold simultaneously and all executable
interface premises hold, no hard violation executes and $R(D)\leq\sum_i b_i$.
If simultaneous bounds hold with probability $1-\delta$ over calibration
samples, that is the confidence of this population-risk conclusion; $\delta$
is the calibration failure budget, distinct from decision randomness under $D$.
The implementation uses one whole-policy bound rather than estimating $b_i$
separately.

\end{theorem}
\begin{IEEEproof}
Induct on mediated trace extensions. Any executed extension has passed the
correct deterministic monitor, preserving the invariant. For semantic loss,
disjointness gives
$\mathbf{1}_{\{Y=1,\mathrm{allow}\}}=\sum_i\mathbf{1}_{\{Y=1,E_i\}}$.
Taking expectations yields the result. Simultaneous certificates can be
obtained by allocating a failure budget across a fixed family and applying a
union bound; independence is unnecessary. Failure of an executable interface
premise forces denial. The theorem does not assert that distributional
premises are decidable at runtime. In $T^*$ and $T_j$ deterministic mediation
denies the action; $T_b$ needs an applicable semantic acceptance certificate.
\end{IEEEproof}

The implementation certifies the entire fixed routing policy, rather than
estimating a local error on selectively observed traffic and multiplying it
by a marginal reach rate. This avoids hidden
selection assumptions. Route-specific certificates are a supported theoretical
extension, not separately estimated by the pilot implementation.

\subsection{Risk accounting and dependence}
Conditional on unsafe input, the common-shock model first flips a branch coin
of probability $\rho$. In that branch all guards compare one shared uniform
variable with their miss rates; otherwise they use independent uniform
variables. This preserves each conditional marginal while changing dependence.

\begin{theorem}[Risk bounds for an all-veto ensemble]
\label{thm:bounds}
Let $\beta_i$ bound the joint event
$M_i=\{Y=1,\mathrm{allow}_i\}$, let $m_i$ be the miss rate conditional on
$Y=1$, and let $\pi=\Pr(Y=1)$ be unsafe prevalence. Then
$R\leq\min_i\beta_i$. Under unsafe-conditional independence,
$R=\pi\prod_i m_i$; under the common-shock model above,
\begin{equation}
R=\pi\left((1-\rho)\prod_i m_i+\rho\min_i m_i\right).
\label{eq:mixture}
\end{equation}
Early acceptance instead uses the disjoint-route accounting of
Theorem~\ref{thm:composition}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{IEEEproof}
The veto intersection is contained in every $M_i$, proving the joint minimum
bound. Conditional independence gives the product of $m_i$; multiplying by
unsafe prevalence gives joint risk. In the shared-uniform branch the
intersection has probability $\min_i m_i$, and in the independent branch it
has probability $\prod_i m_i$. Averaging branches and multiplying by $\pi$
proves~\eqref{eq:mixture}. Early acceptance can skip later guards, so its
acceptance event is not this intersection.
\end{IEEEproof}

The product of joint marginal miss probabilities is generally incorrect even
under unsafe-conditional independence: those events share the same unsafe
input event. Moreover, observed pairwise correlation does not identify an
arbitrary higher-order failure law. Common compromise of a judge on $T_j$
illustrates why repeated verdicts need not be independent. Successful
classification of $T_b$ supplies no evidence for that independence assumption.

The library separates three statistical promises. \emph{Split conformal}
controls marginal prediction-set coverage. \emph{Conformal risk control} (CRC)
controls expected bounded loss across calibration samples and a new decision,
not population risk conditional on one observed calibration sample. For $n$
exchangeable labeled samples $Z_k$, fixed losses $l_t\in[0,1]$ decreasing
with conservatism $t$, and desired expected-loss budget $\alpha$, CRC
chooses the least conservative setting satisfying
$(\sum_{k=1}^n l_t(Z_k)+1)/(n+1)\leq\alpha$, with a zero-loss fallback
under its regularity conditions~\cite{arxiv220802814}. These utilities are not
the certificate used in the pilot.

\emph{The implemented certificate} is a one-sided binomial population-risk
bound. For each of $K$ fixed policies, count $k$ unsafe accepted proposals among
all $n$ iid calibration proposals and allocate failure budget $\delta/K$.
For $k<n$, the upper bound is the $(1-\delta/K)$ quantile of
$\operatorname{Beta}(k+1,n-k)$; it is one for $k=n$. For example, zero observed
losses give $U=1-(\delta/K)^{1/n}$, not proof of zero risk. Simultaneous bounds
permit selecting a feasible policy with $U\leq\epsilon$; no feasible policy
means denial. Iid sampling is stronger than general exchangeability. The
confidence concerns repeated calibration samples; deployment risk concerns new
proposals after the policy is fixed. A finite test frequency is neither.

For a fixed policy, let $Q$ be a new deployment law. Total variation is the
largest probability difference between $D$ and $Q$ over events. Thus an
externally established $\operatorname{TV}(D,Q)\leq\eta$ implies
$R(Q)\leq\min(1,R(D)+\eta)$. No estimator of $\eta$ is certified here.
Without restrictions on $Q$, an attacker can concentrate on any accepted
unsafe input, making its risk one. Hard mediation still excludes its exact
hard predicates; it does not exclude all semantic violations. Absent exact
semantic verification, denying uncertified semantic acceptance is the
distribution-free fallback in this restricted model.

\subsection{Escalation synthesis}
\begin{theorem}[Conditional sufficient optimality of escalation]
\label{thm:optimization}
For $x\sim D_X$, let $r_0(x),r_1(x)$ be conditional probabilities of the same
joint unsafe-acceptance event under cheap acceptance or escalation given $x$.
Let $d(x)>0$ be extra cost and $c_0(x)$ baseline cost. A
randomized escalation probability $q(x)\in[0,1]$ has
\begin{align*}
R(q)&=\mathbb E_{D_X}[(1-q)r_0+qr_1],\\
C(q)&=\mathbb E_{D_X}[c_0+qd].
\end{align*}
Consider minimizing $C(q)$ subject to $R(q)\leq\epsilon$, with measurable
policies and finite integrable costs. If a finite $\lambda\geq0$ and a feasible
pointwise Lagrangian minimizer $q$ exist with
$\lambda(R(q)-\epsilon)=0$, that policy is optimal. It escalates where
$\lambda(r_0-r_1)>d$, accepts where the inequality reverses, and may randomize
on ties. Existence of such a multiplier is an extra premise.
\end{theorem}
\begin{IEEEproof}
The multiplier is a price per unit of risk: it trades added cost against risk
reduction. The full Lagrangian is
\begin{align*}
L(q,\lambda)={}&\mathbb E[c_0+\lambda r_0]-\lambda\epsilon\\
&+\mathbb E[q(d-\lambda(r_0-r_1))].
\end{align*}
The sign of the coefficient of $q$ gives the rule. Every feasible competitor's
cost is at least the minimized Lagrangian lower bound (weak duality).
Complementary slackness, the stated product being zero, makes the candidate's
cost equal that bound. A tie mixture can change risk without changing the
Lagrangian. At a boundary risk budget, a finite multiplier need not exist.
\end{IEEEproof}

In the raw-judge countermodel before the hard sink, if escalation increases
risk on $T_j$, then $r_0-r_1<0$ and escalation
cannot satisfy the inequality at positive extra cost. For $T_b$, a small risk
reduction may not justify deliberation. For $T^*$, optimization never waives the
mandatory sink. Known conditional risks and their monotonicity in a score are
assumptions; the theorem does not learn them from calibration.

The pilot solves a different, finite problem: enumerate a fixed policy grid
and minimize observed calibration mean cost among simultaneously certified
candidates. This is exact within that grid, not among all routing functions.
A separate known-risk finite LP supports policy mixtures; with normalization
and one active risk constraint, an extreme optimum needs at most two policies.
The optional latency API certifies an event $\Pr(L>\mathrm{budget})\leq\gamma$,
where $\gamma$ is an exceedance budget; no real inference-latency constraint was
evaluated. Averaging percentiles would not certify that event.

\begin{theorem}[Tightness and limits]
\label{thm:limits}
The veto minimum and capped route bound $\min(1,\sum_i b_i)$ are tight given their
stated marginal information. Pairwise independence does not determine joint
failure for an arbitrary ensemble.
\end{theorem}
\begin{IEEEproof}
For the joint minimum construction take $\pi=1$ and $m_i=\beta_i$, then let
guard $i$ miss when a shared uniform variable is at most $m_i$. This attains
$\min_i\beta_i$; independent uniforms attain the conditional product law.
For routes, allocate disjoint unsafe masses $b_i$ when their sum is at most
one. If it exceeds one, allocate masses no larger than the bounds that sum to
one, attaining the cap. Finally, interpret Boolean bits as guard misses and
take uniform laws on $\{000,011,101,110\}$ and $\{001,010,100,111\}$.
Each coordinate has marginal $1/2$ and every pair is independent, yet all-one
probabilities are $0$ and $1/4$. Thus pairwise data do not identify joint risk.
Shared susceptibility in $T_j$ illustrates the dependence concern; $T_b$ alone
cannot identify its failure law.

\end{IEEEproof}

\subsection{Implementation, assumptions and limits}
The typed interpreter implements local mock HTTP, in-memory files and typed
tool-effect records. It rejects arbitrary shell commands and unknown tool
fields. Provenance references are write-once; unknown references retain
conservative taint. Approval binds the immutable action digest and epoch.
The trusted dispatcher checks its configured environment against the proposal.
The tool adapter parses function-call argument objects and invokes the same
mediated interpreter; it is not a tested connection to an external model API.

The checker validates declarations, not semantic truth. It accepts only the
runtime's exact topology and supported predicates. At \emph{each runtime
decision}, a fingerprint rechecks specification, guard versions, threshold,
view and environment; confidence and risk budgets are also checked. Complete
host dependencies, valid issuers and honest implementation versions remain
external premises.

The primary instrument is \texttt{lexical-v1}; the corrected exploratory
instrument is \texttt{lexical-v2}. Both remain frozen for reproduction.
The current default \texttt{lexical-v3-bounded} adds an input cap and is not
the source of historical CPU measurements or certificates. As Definition~\ref{def:layer}
states, exceptions deny authority but arbitrary synchronous plugins have no
enforced cancellation deadline; they can hang. Concurrent trusted-store
mutation and covert channels are outside scope.

In the stated control semantics, every effect has a preceding hard-pass and
certified state, by induction over the only effect transition.
Table~\ref{tab:obligations} distinguishes checked facts from external premises.
This correspondence is an inspection argument, not a proved refinement link
or a general semantic contract solver.

\textbf{Assumptions and limits.} Deterministic guarantees hold only for this
typed, completely mediated semantics. The PRISM model is an independently
checked finite abstraction, without a proved refinement relation to Python.
Structured quarantine excludes raw instructions but does not establish the
semantic oracle. Statistical certification assumes fixed candidates and the
stated calibration/deployment relationship; arbitrary adaptation and drift
are not covered. A matching fingerprint cannot establish that relationship.
These are preconditions, not empirically established facts about real LLMs.

\section{Evaluation Design}
\label{sec:design}
The local protocol was committed and tagged \texttt{prereg-v1} before empirical
calibration or held-out evaluation. Unit tests and canonical model checks
preceded preregistration and are not independent confirmatory findings. The
tag is local, not an independently timestamped registry. The original run and
the amendment are retained separately, with their source commit identifiers.

The testbed uses a handwritten lexical scorer, a deliberately vulnerable
raw-text judge and a structured deterministic judge. The latter's features do
not include the oracle label. Two scripted proposal sources are not two base
LLMs: no real base LLM was run. The hardware audit found a small GPU but the
installed learning framework was CPU-only. API credentials were unavailable,
and the inspected small guard model required authenticated access acceptance.
Docker's engine was unavailable; its image recipe was not executed.

\section{Results}

\subsection{RQ I: specification and decomposition}
The canonical PRISM study checks \resPrismCases{} configurations and agrees
with the independent checker on both reachability properties. Exhaustive
enumeration covers \resFormalStates{} Boolean sink cases. Seeded sanity
checks exercise \resFormalInstances{} random probability instances. The
general proofs remain pen-and-paper proofs; these finite checks are neither
Lean/Isabelle mechanizations nor proofs of the runtime implementation.

The original mutation study rejects \resPrimaryCheckerTP{} invalid-case
evaluations across \resPrimaryCheckerUniqueInvalid{} unique generated variants,
while accepting \resPrimaryCheckerTN{} evaluations of
\resPrimaryCheckerUniqueClean{} unique clean graphs, with
\resPrimaryCheckerFP{} false rejections and \resPrimaryCheckerFN{} missed
mutations. This demonstrates agreement with the mutation generator, not
precision/recall on naturally occurring policy errors. The maximum cell-median
checker time is \resPrimaryCheckerMax{} microseconds on the recorded host.
No policy-count scalability result is claimed for PRISM; its evaluated model
is the fixed canonical abstraction.

The inventory supports only \resSupportedClasses{} of
\resPolicyClasses{} policy classes fully in the DSL: production approval and
secret egress. Semantic injection has a statistical proxy; filesystem
confinement and cumulative spending are rejected as unsupported predicates.
The full-expressiveness hypothesis is refuted. This restricted boundary is
preferable to accepting declarations that the runtime does not enforce.
Core test coverage is \resCoreCoverage{}\%, including the numeric-boundary
regression. Coverage is a structural test metric, not a proof of correctness.

For the separate nested-loss generator, selected bounds cover true population

the exact interval is [\resPrimaryCoverageLo{}\%, \resPrimaryCoverageHi{}\%].
The certificate's nominal confidence is \resNominalConfidence{}\%. This is
observed conservative coverage under the known iid law, not a stronger
deployment guarantee. The selected policy's finite test frequency is below
the \resCoverageTarget{}\% risk budget in \resPrimaryFiniteTestBelow{}

\subsection{Assumption audit and threats to validity}
\textbf{Internal validity.} The primary arithmetic defect is preserved, and
fresh-seed correction remains exploratory. The designer authored labels,
templates and planted mutations; these are not independent observations of
semantic safety. Regression tests check configuration identity, not arbitrary
plugin correctness. Original synthesis counts remain unavailable.

\textbf{External validity.} No real base LLM, pretrained guard, official defense,
external benchmark, completed development task or practitioner workflow was
evaluated. Inference load, real token/GPU cost, long sessions and temporal drift
are untested. Local CPU results and modeled units cannot establish deployment
performance or savings.

\textbf{Construct validity.} The distinct denominators in Definition~\ref{def:metrics} must
not be interchanged. Benign acceptance is not task completion; structured
quarantine is not semantic noninterference. Trusted context identity does not
establish stationarity, and complete dependency tracking remains a host premise.

\textbf{Conclusion validity.} Seeds sample one authored law, not independent
developers or tasks. Conservative correction can produce infeasibility.
Pairwise correlation cannot establish the needed joint structure. Numeric
lint checks empirical source provenance, not every PDF glyph; notation lint
is a registry check, not a semantic proof. These limitations qualify every
statistical and formal conclusion.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\subsection{Guardrail systems}
Llama Guard, WildGuard, Constitutional Classifiers and ThinkGuard
illustrate classification and reasoning approaches to safety
monitoring~\cite{arxiv231206674,arxiv240618495,arxiv250118837,arxiv250213458}.
NeMo provides programmable rails, while LlamaFirewall combines specialized
scanners for agent threats~\cite{arxiv231010501,arxiv250503574}.
Truncated polynomial classifiers support progressive activation
monitoring~\cite{arxiv250926238}. These systems motivate heterogeneous layers;
our toy components do not reproduce their learned behavior or results.
OneShield further illustrates configurable guardrail
engineering~\cite{arxiv250721170}.

\subsection{Cascades and adaptive compute}
FrugalGPT studies cost-aware model cascades~\cite{arxiv230505176}.
Trust or Escalate already supplies cascaded selective evaluation with
human-agreement guarantees~\cite{arxiv240718370}. Conformal Arbitrage already
calibrates a primary/guardian switch and analyzes score-gap routing
optimality~\cite{arxiv250600911}. Hybrid abstention uses hierarchical detection
for practical filtering~\cite{arxiv260215391}. Our distinction is checking
the applicability of certificates at actual tool-action routes, not inventing
calibrated cascading. The mathematical optimization result is a standard
Lagrangian observation, not a new general router theorem.

\subsection{Dual-process architectures}
The System~I/System~II vocabulary connects fast response with deliberative
reasoning, and the reasoning-model survey organizes that
literature~\cite{arxiv250217419}. We use an operational resource criterion
without claiming equivalence to human cognition. Training-time helpfulness
and constitutional feedback address another part of safety engineering;
they do not substitute for tool-effect mediation~\cite{arxiv220405862,arxiv221208073}.

\subsection{Runtime enforcement and verification}
AgentSpec provides structured runtime rules~\cite{arxiv250318666}; ShieldAgent
reasons with verifiable policy representations~\cite{arxiv250322738}.
ProbGuard estimates future safety through probabilistic
monitoring~\cite{arxiv250800500}. VeriGuard separates verified-policy
construction from online action monitoring~\cite{arxiv251005156}.
ContrAgent is especially close in its temporal contracts and compiled
monitors~\cite{arxiv260918128}; Agent Behavioral Contracts also supplies
composition and probabilistic satisfaction notions~\cite{arxiv260222302}.
The recent layered probabilistic architecture directly limits any claim to
first conditional guardrail composition~\cite{arxiv260518672}.

Shield synthesis provides classical runtime correction under formal
specifications~\cite{arxiv150102573,arxiv170808611,arxiv190805402,arxiv240604184}.
RoboGuard protects a root-of-trust model and uses temporal
constraints~\cite{arxiv250307885}.
Our deterministic mediation belongs to this established enforcement lineage.

\subsection{System-level isolation}
CaMeL separates control and data flow and mediates
capabilities~\cite{arxiv250318813}. Fides tracks confidentiality/integrity
labels with a formal planner model~\cite{arxiv250523643}. Spotlighting and
instruction-hierarchy training address provenance cues and instruction
priority through model behavior~\cite{arxiv240314720,arxiv240413208}.
TINT uses a deliberately restricted structured guard view, not a proof that
prompt formatting prevents instruction-following. Task Shield checks goal
alignment and provides a stronger real-agent comparison target for future
work~\cite{arxiv241216682}.

\subsection{Statistical guarantees}
CRC controls expected monotone loss under exchangeability
conditions~\cite{arxiv220802814}. Conformal tutorials and adaptive
classification methods distinguish marginal guarantees from stronger
conditional claims~\cite{arxiv210707511,arxiv200602544}.
Adaptive conformal inference studies changing distributions, while adaptive
and tail-risk methods broaden the controlled objectives~\cite{arxiv210600170,arxiv240617819,arxiv250220285}.
Conformal abstention, factuality and language-generation methods demonstrate
other established statistical interfaces~\cite{arxiv240501563,arxiv240210978,arxiv230610193}.
We implement small calibration utilities and apply finite-family binomial
certification; we claim no new conformal theorem or adaptive safety guarantee.

\subsection{Layered safety engineering}
Conditional component contracts, runtime assurance and defense in depth
motivate exposing assumptions instead of counting layers. ScenicProver is a
particularly relevant compositional assurance framework with explicit evidence
and provenance~\cite{arxiv251102164}. Semantic integrity constraints also
illustrate declarative assurance at another software
boundary~\cite{arxiv250300600}; NaSh emphasizes recovery and guardrail design
for language-driven shells~\cite{arxiv250613028}. Our gap is an executable
integration for a restricted tool-agent interface, not a novel safety-case
principle. 

\subsection{Attacks and evaluation}
Indirect injection, optimized jailbreaks, guard evasion and latent-defense
attacks demonstrate why guard behavior must itself be part of the threat
model~\cite{arxiv230212173,arxiv230715043,arxiv250411168,arxiv241209565,arxiv250305264}.
RAG context changes can alter guard judgments~\cite{arxiv251005310}; baseline
defenses illustrate the need to specify attacker access~\cite{arxiv230900614}.
AgentDojo, ASB, AgentHarm, RedCode, ToolEmu and R-Judge offer substantially
richer evaluation substrates~\cite{arxiv240613352,arxiv241002644,arxiv241009024,arxiv241107781,arxiv230915817,arxiv240110019}.
RedCodeAgent, BAD-ACTS and CyberSecEval further expand agent-security
testing~\cite{arxiv251002609,arxiv250816481,arxiv240801605}.
Representation sensitivity cautions against treating a benchmark score as an
intrinsic model property~\cite{arxiv261003585}. None of those benchmarks was
executed here, and our fixture attack search is not a comparable evaluation.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
A verdict is usable only under its stated premises. The canonical example
exposes invalid composition; its stronger control shows that ordinary mediation
already protects the hard property. Route sums prevent a separate probability
error: importing veto intersections into early acceptance. These are established
principles made executable, not newly discovered safety laws.

Primary infeasibility demonstrates correct denial without useful service.
Exploratory correction restores some utility but leaves implicit unsafe actions
and fails under the designed shift. Equal checked-control decisions show that
certification does not repair an unchanged detector. Distinctive practical
value therefore remains a research question.

Future evaluation must use real proposal-generating LLMs and independently
implemented learned guards across external benchmarks. GPU-hosted models,
activation probes and reasoning judges may change miss rates, correlations,
costs and latency under load. No numerical bound or trade-off reported here
is claimed to transfer unchanged. Further work also needs richer rule
semantics, explicit route-specific calibration, independently authored policy
errors, stronger adaptive attacks and a proved abstraction/refinement link.
The artifact does not establish real-agent safety or resource advantage.

\section{Conclusion}
Explicit conditions on trust edges make a limited but useful assurance case
executable: deterministic effects remain mediated, certificates bind the
actual routing configuration, and unproved premises are visible. Scoped
proofs and reproducible local experiments expose the difference between
conditional component guarantees and valid composition. The primary utility
failure, the exploratory correction and the shift failure all constrain the
empirical conclusion. Real-agent engineering value remains to be established.
