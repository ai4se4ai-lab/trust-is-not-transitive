# Review — Reviewer 3 (SE for AI / LLM developer tools; presentation and relevance)

Manuscript: "Trust Is Not Transitive: Checked Assume-Guarantee Composition of Hierarchical Guardrails for LLM Agents" (TSE submission; 17 pages including about 2.5 pages of references).

## 1. Summary

The paper argues that layered guardrail stacks for tool-using LLM agents (rules, then a cheap classifier, then an LLM judge, then a human) are usually justified by per-layer accuracy, while what the stack as a whole guarantees is never stated. Each guard's guarantee depends on assumptions (what the guard sees, how it was calibrated), and those assumptions are not carried along when one layer relays another's verdict. The authors propose TINT, a discipline plus a small Python library with four parts. Inter-layer trust edges are declared in a closed JSON DSL and checked syntactically. A deterministic monitor (the "sink") enforces two information-flow and approval policies on every action. The LLM judge receives only a closed structured record ("quarantine"). Clopper–Pearson risk certificates are bound to a hash of the exact configuration. Five elementary propositions back this up: a countermodel, scoped soundness, veto-versus-cascade risk algebra, non-identifiability from pairwise statistics, and a Lagrangian escalation rule, together with finite-family certified selection. The evaluation is a preregistered study in a fully synthetic, typed sandbox with scripted guards and seven one-template trace families. In the frozen primary run nothing could be certified, because of a floating-point defect in the scorer, so TINT blocked everything. In an exploratory fresh-seed rerun after the fix, TINT halved joint unsafe acceptance relative to a sink-plus-exposed-judge control (20.77% to 10.16%). That difference is entirely one family, the one whose text is addressed to the judge, and it follows by construction from the scripted judge. Certification changed no decision, and every pipeline accepted every proposal from an unseen encoded family. The authors are unusually candid about all of this.

**Overall recommendation: Major revision.**

The paper is honest, reproducible and well organized at the macro level. In its current form, however, a PhD student would come away unsure what the trust-edge machinery actually buys, and a practitioner would not know how to apply quarantine to a real LLM judge. Both problems can be fixed without new LLM experiments. Some can be fixed with analyses the authors can run today: worked route accounting on DevAgent, and a mapping of one real guardrail configuration into a TINT spec.

## 3. Strengths

- **S1. Honest reporting of a negative primary result.** The primary run certified nothing, and that is reported prominently, in the abstract, rather than buried. The defect fix is labeled exploratory, run on fresh seeds and governed by a dated amendment. This sets a standard TSE should encourage.
- **S2. The strong control B5 is the right design decision.** The authors explicitly decline to credit quarantine and certification with what the sink alone achieves (Section III-D, H4). Many guardrail papers fail exactly here.
- **S3. Joint risk is defined clearly.** $R=\Pr[Y=1\wedge\textsf{allow}]$ is distinguished from the miss rate and from accepted conditional risk. The numeric example ("one proposal in ten ... $R=0.05$") is the kind of gentle explanation a PhD student needs.
- **S4. Prop. 3(d) and Prop. 4 make a useful correction to folk reasoning.** Early-accept cascades are a route sum, not a product or minimum, and pairwise statistics do not identify joint risk. "Measure the pipeline, not the parts" is a lesson practitioners can act on immediately.
- **S5. The "Where the numbers come from" paragraph and Table VIII.** Tracing every family through every layer makes the results fully explainable. The assumption audit (Table X) is also an excellent device that more SE-for-AI papers should copy.
- **S6. Verifiability.** Macro provenance, replay of 336,000 decisions, a Docker image and a 5-second smoke test.

## 4. Major issues

### M1. Label and notation overloading defeats the intended reader

The paper introduces at least nine label families: C1–C3, F1–F3, RQ1–3, H1–H8, (A1)–(A4), B1–B5, A1, amendments A1/A2, and $L_1$–$L_4$. Several collide:

- **"A1" means three different things.** It is the assumption "(A1) complete mediation" in Prop. 2, the comparator "A1 TINT, no certificate" in Table VI, and "Amendment A1 changed only the score arithmetic" (Section V-F). Table IV mixes them in adjacent rows: "Sec. III, (A1)" and "Comparators B1–B5, A1". While reading I genuinely misattributed "A1 introduced the defect fix" (Environment and protocol paragraph) to the comparator.
- **$m$** is the number of acceptance routes ("$E_1,\dots,E_m$", "$m=1$"), the per-guard miss rate $m_i$, and a vector "With $m=(0.1,0.2,0.3)$".
- **$\pi$** is $\Pr[Y=1]$ in Section IV-C and the candidate policies $\pi_j$ in Section IV-D, two paragraphs apart.
- **$\lambda$** is the provenance label $\lambda(a)$ and the Lagrange multiplier in Prop. 6.
- **$n$** is the number of calibration samples and also the number of guards in "$\pi^{n}\prod_i m_i$" (Prop. 3(b)).
- **$k$** is matched indicator tokens (Table VII), observed losses $k_j$ (Eq. 2) and the route index $E_k$.
- **$A_i$** is defined as "assumptions about the layer's view, its inputs' provenance and ... calibration context" (Def. 2), but Def. 3 then requires "$A_j\subseteq E$". That treats assumptions as a set of evidence tokens. The reader has to guess that assumptions are represented as named evidence items (the `requires` field of Fig. 3).

*Why it matters.* The paper's whole argument is that obligations must be named unambiguously. Its own naming makes that hard to follow, and the target reader will stall.

*Fix.*
1. Rename the comparator A1 to **B6 ("TINT without certificate")** or "TINT−cert". Rename the amendments **Am1/Am2**. Rename the assumptions **(P1)–(P4)** ("premises"), which matches the paper's own vocabulary of "premises".
2. Use $N$ for the number of guards, $r$ (or $|\mathcal{K}|$) for the number of routes, $\theta$ or $\pi_Y$ for the base rate, $\mu$ for the multiplier, and $j$ or $c$ for token counts.
3. In Def. 2, say explicitly: "We represent $A_i$ as a finite set of named evidence items (e.g., `quarantine`, `calibrated_context`); each item stands for a predicate on the view and calibration context that trusted code must establish."
4. Drop C1–C3 or F1–F3. They are a one-to-one relabeling of each other ("correspond to challenges C1–C3"). One set of labels is enough.

### M2. The paper's central concept (conditioned trust edges) is not load-bearing in its main guarantee

Prop. 2 (scoped soundness) has premises (A1) mediation, (A2) correct deterministic monitor, (A3) no overwrite and (A4) route certificates. **None of them mentions trust edges, Definition 3 or Algorithm 1.** The implementation then uses $m=1$, a whole-policy certificate on end-to-end joint risk ("so that $b_1$ bounds $R(D)$ directly"). That bound would hold for *any* pipeline whose end-to-end behavior is calibrated, whether or not its edges are declared. Meanwhile the checker "establishes a syntactic fact" about declared strings, and the runtime "refuses any topology other than $L_1\to L_2\to L_3$", so declared `provides` items "become true by construction".

After reading, I could not answer the student's natural question: *what would go wrong if I deleted the `trust` field from Fig. 3 and kept the sink, quarantine and an end-to-end certificate?* As far as I can tell, nothing in the evaluated system changes. The checker's evaluation (H2) shows only that it catches its own generator's mutations.

There is also a conceptual tension with the title. In TINT, $L_1$ still acts on a verdict that includes $L_3$'s, relayed via $L_2$. Trust is therefore still chained; each link is merely discharged. The paper says "non-transitivity is a design rule here, not a theorem", which a reader can only read as "we do not compose edges automatically". That is a much weaker and more ordinary claim than the title suggests.

*Why it matters.* Without this link, the novelty claim reduces to "sink + quarantine + end-to-end Clopper–Pearson + config hash". All four are known.

*Fix.*
1. Add an explicit bridge. Either state a premise in Prop. 2, "(P5) every verdict consumed on route $k$ crosses only edges accepted by Algorithm 1, and the runtime establishes each edge's evidence", and show where it is used (e.g., it is what guarantees that the view premise of the certificate holds), or state honestly that in the $m=1$ instance the edges serve reviewability and topology validation, not the bound.
2. Add a **worked per-route example on DevAgent** for $m=2$. Route 1 is "accept at $L_2$ ($s<t$)"; route 2 is "judge allows". Compute $\Pr[Y=1\wedge E_1]$ (implicit patch, 10%) and $\Pr[Y=1\wedge E_2]$ (0% under quarantine, 10% for B5's judge-targeting family). Show that an undischarged `quarantine` edge invalidates exactly the route-2 certificate. This one paragraph would make Prop. 2, Prop. 3(d), the checker and quarantine fit together. Today the route decomposition is never applied to the running example.
3. Reframe the title claim in the introduction. For example: "We do not prevent chains of trust; we require each link to be declared and discharged by evidence available at that link, and we never infer a link from two others."

### M3. The headline RQ3 result holds by construction, yet it is presented with inferential statistics and as an effect

The abstract says "TINT reduced joint unsafe acceptance from 20.77% to 10.16% relative to a strong rule-plus-judge control". Section V-F is headed "After the fix, quarantine halved risk relative to the strong control", and it reports a bootstrap CI, a Holm-adjusted Wilcoxon $p$ and $\hat A_{12}=1.00$. But `ExposedJudge` is a test double that "returns allow when the raw text contains the judge-targeting phrase", and the structured judge "blocks when secret and external both hold or when $s\ge0.40$". The 10-point difference is therefore the judge-targeting family's designed mass (10%) plus sampling noise. The authors acknowledge this ("the entire difference ... is therefore one family"; "What the statistics mean"), but only after the effect has been framed as a finding.

Also: (a) the quarantined judge is a second threshold on the very scorer it follows, so "quarantine" here equals "ignore the text and re-threshold $s$". (b) 336,000 "logged decisions" in the abstract suggests a scale of evidence that the 7-family × 10-seed design does not have.

*Why it matters.* A TSE reader skimming the abstract and the section headings will believe that an empirical risk reduction was measured. In fact a designed property was confirmed. That is a presentation and rigor problem, and it is the kind that reviewers in the guardrail community will notice at once.

*Fix.*
1. Report RQ3 as **analytical predictions confirmed by replay**. Population values follow from Table VII (e.g., "TINT: 10% risk = implicit-patch mass; B5: 20% = implicit patch + judge targeting"). Give the seed-level spread as a single sentence. Move the Wilcoxon/Holm/$\hat A_{12}$ numbers to the artifact or to one footnote, since the paper itself says they are at their floor.
2. Rename the section heading to "After the fix, quarantine removed the judge-targeting family, as designed".
3. Rewrite the abstract sentence: "In an exploratory fresh-seed rerun after the fix, quarantine removed the one family whose text targets the judge, halving joint unsafe acceptance relative to a sink-plus-exposed-judge control (20.77% to 10.16%) by construction of the scripted judge; certification changed no decision, and every pipeline accepted an encoded attack family absent from calibration."
4. Replace "(336,000 logged decisions)" in the abstract with a description of the design ("seven scripted trace families, ten seeds per study").

### M4. Quarantine, as implemented, removes what makes an LLM judge useful. The paper does not tell practitioners how to resolve this

$\varphi_3$ is "never act on instructions embedded in untrusted content". By definition, judging it requires reading the untrusted content. The quarantined judge receives "the six Boolean facts and the $L_2$ score, and nothing else". The paper twice concedes the cost ("The price is information loss"; "in a real system the right projection is a design problem of its own"), but Lesson 3, "do not let the judge read what the attacker wrote", is stated as a general recommendation. A practitioner who follows it literally would replace their LLM judge with a rule over the classifier score, which is exactly what the study did.

Two further points:
- The term inverts established usage. In the dual-LLM pattern and in CaMeL (cited), the *quarantined* LLM is the one that **does** read untrusted data and has no tool privileges. In TINT, the "quarantined judge" is the one that **does not** read it. Readers from the agent-security community will be confused. The dual-LLM pattern (Willison, 2023) is not cited at all, although it is the obvious practical precedent for "structured view to the privileged component".
- Nothing in the evaluation tells us whether *any* structured view preserves enough semantics for $\varphi_3$. The implicit-patch family shows the opposite: the information needed is gone.

*Fix.*
1. Recast Lesson 3 as a trade-off with a concrete design pattern. For example: "Give the judge a typed projection produced by a component that reads attacker text but cannot issue verdicts (a quarantined extractor in the dual-LLM sense), and treat the extractor's output fields as untrusted inputs with their own contract."
2. Discuss at least one concrete projection for DevAgent (e.g., the diff's touched files, whether the patch changes CI, secrets, permissions, or network targets; whether the issue's requested change matches the diff) and explain which families it would or would not separate.
3. Rename "quarantined judge" to "**isolated judge**" or "**structured-view judge**", or explicitly contrast the term with CaMeL's Q-LLM, and cite the dual-LLM pattern.

### M5. Practical impact for SE is asserted, not demonstrated. No real guardrail stack is mapped into TINT

The lessons begin "Most stacks we examined in the literature describe their layers but not these edges". There is no account of which stacks were examined, how, or what was found. A TSE audience would act on this paper if it showed, for one or two *real* configurations (e.g., a NeMo Guardrails rail config, LlamaFirewall's PromptGuard → AlignmentCheck → CodeShield chain, or a Claude Code / Copilot-style permission hook plus LLM reviewer), what the TINT spec looks like, which edges cannot be discharged, and which premises would remain "assumed" in the audit table. This needs no API access, only reading the public configurations and documentation. It would also give H8 (expressiveness) real content: today the DSL covers 2 of 5 policy classes, and filesystem confinement and spending limits, the policies coding-agent users care about most, are inexpressible.

*Why it matters.* As it stands the paper reads as a security/formal-methods feasibility note. The SE contribution, a reviewable artifact that makes implicit design assumptions explicit, is precisely what a case-based mapping would show.

*Fix.* Add a subsection, "Applying TINT to existing guardrail configurations", with one or two qualitative case mappings: a spec figure, the checker's verdict, and an audit-table column. Either replace "Most stacks we examined" with the result of that exercise or delete it. Briefly say how a team would adopt TINT incrementally: start with mandatory sink plus a pipeline-level acceptance test, then add edges.

### M6. The certification "gate" claim is overstated, and the counterfactual is weak

The abstract says "the certification gate had exposed a floating-point defect", and Section V-F says "The certification gate turned a silent defect into a visible, safe failure" and calls this "the most useful engineering observation of the study". But (a) the gate did not expose the defect; it refused to deploy, and the authors found the defect by "inspecting the raw counts". (b) The fair counterfactual to "no certificate" is not "ship blindly". Standard practice is an end-to-end acceptance test on held-out labeled traces with a risk threshold, and that test would have shown the same ~30% risk. What the certificate adds over such a test is the finite-sample guarantee and the configuration binding, and neither of those was what caught the defect.

*Fix.* Reword the claim to "the certification gate refused to deploy a pipeline whose calibrated risk exceeded the budget; inspecting the calibration counts then revealed a floating-point defect". In the Discussion, state the counterfactual explicitly: any pipeline-level acceptance test with a budget would have caught this; the certificate's specific contribution is the confidence statement and the fingerprint binding. Lesson 5 should credit "measure the pipeline against a budget before deployment" accordingly.

### M7. Figures and story do not carry the core idea; there is room for a better teaching figure

- **Fig. 1 (countermodel)** draws the naive path as "$T_j$ → $L_3$ → $L_2,L_1$ → execute". That is the reverse of the online order in Fig. 2, where $L_1$ comes first. The intro then says "the judge further down the guardrail stack" and "the layers above it relay its verdict". A student cannot tell who calls whom, or in which direction verdicts flow. The difference between "caller/callee" (Def. 3) and "earlier/later layer" (Fig. 2) is never drawn.
- **Fig. 4 (checker scaling)** shows that a linear loop over 2,500 edges takes 10 ms. That is one sentence of content and does not earn a figure. ("Ten seeds per point" also suggests variance where the generator is deterministic.)
- **Fig. 5 (dependence)** plots a simulation of the closed form in Prop. 3(c). It confirms algebra, and its point (product vs. min) is already made by the "0.006 vs 0.1" example.
- **What is missing** is a figure contrasting a **veto ensemble** with an **early-accept cascade**, with acceptance routes $E_1$, $E_2$ marked and the DevAgent family masses flowing along them. That figure would teach Prop. 3(d), Prop. 2(ii) and the RQ3 result at once (see M2.2).
- **Fig. 3 (spec)** is good, but it raises questions the text does not answer: why $L_1$ has `semantic` in its scope; why `mandatory` and `rules` list the same items; why $L_1$ `provides` `quarantine` when $L_1$ never calls $L_3$; and where $L_4$ is (it appears in Fig. 2 but not in the spec).

*Fix.* Redraw Fig. 1 with the same left-to-right layer order as Fig. 2, showing calls going down and verdicts coming back. Replace Fig. 4 with a sentence. Replace or merge Fig. 5 into the new veto-vs-cascade/route figure. Add 2–3 sentences under Fig. 3 explaining the four oddities above. The paper has room: it is at about 14 pages of body text against a 15–25 page target, so adding the route example (M2), the case mapping (M5) and a better figure keeps it well within range.

## 5. Comprehension check

I answered these from memory after a single read, then checked them against the paper.

**(1) What problem does the paper solve?**
*My answer:* Layered guardrail stacks for agents state no joint guarantee. Each layer's guarantee is conditional, and those conditions are silently assumed when verdicts are relayed. TINT makes the conditions explicit and checked, enforces deterministic rules exactly, and certifies end-to-end risk for the exact configuration.
*Check:* Correct. The problem statement in Section III-E (i)–(v) is clear. The abstract and Section I convey it well.

**(2) What does "trust is not transitive" mean, and why does it matter?**
*My answer:* If $L_1$ trusts $L_2$ and $L_2$ trusts $L_3$, $L_1$ cannot conclude that $L_3$'s verdict is valid, because $L_3$'s assumptions (e.g., seeing a structured view) may not hold. In $T_j$, the judge is told to answer SAFE and every layer satisfies its contract vacuously.
*Check:* Correct, but my answer was vaguer than it should be, for reasons the paper causes. (a) In TINT, $L_1$ *does* consume $L_3$'s verdict through $L_2$, so I was unsure in what sense TINT is "non-transitive" rather than "per-link discharged". The sentence "Non-transitivity is a design rule here, not a theorem" (end of Section III-B) did not resolve this (see M2). (b) I first remembered the countermodel as the judge failing on the semantic property. Prop. 1, however, states a $\varphi_{\mathrm{det}}$ violation, and the paper itself notes that the sink alone defeats $T_j$. A reader can easily leave believing that quarantine is what stops $T_j$; Fig. 1(b)'s title "sink first, judge quarantined" reinforces this.

**(3) What are the three RQs and their answers?**
*My answer:* RQ1, specification and decomposition: decompose into a deterministic fragment (sink) and a semantic fragment with checked edges; the checker caught all planted errors; the DSL is narrow. RQ2, guarantees: risk is bounded under premises, cascade risk is a route sum, and selection is exact within a finite family. RQ3, engineering value: the sink prevented all deterministic violations, quarantine removed judge targeting, certification blocked everything in the primary run and did nothing after the fix, and everything fails under shift.
*Check:* Mostly right. **Vague:** the escalation half of RQ2 ("which escalation policy minimizes expected cost subject to a risk bound"). I could not recall what the answer was. The answer paragraph for RQ2 mentions only "the cost-minimal policy can be selected exactly" within a finite family and drops the Lagrangian result (escalate when risk reduction per cost exceeds $1/\lambda$, which is not a score threshold). RQ2 also bundles three questions into one, which makes its answer diffuse. *Suggestion:* split RQ2 into RQ2a (guarantee conditions and failure modes) and RQ2b (cost-optimal escalation), each with a one-sentence answer.

**(4) What is the key soundness result and its assumptions?**
*My answer:* Prop. 2. Under complete mediation with unforgeable labels, a correct deterministic monitor on every acceptance, no overwriting of blocks, and route certificates holding jointly with probability $1-\delta$, no executed action violates $\varphi_{\mathrm{det}}$, and $R\le\sum_k b_k$ with probability $1-\delta$.
*Check:* Correct as to the content. **Got wrong:** I listed exchangeability between calibration and deployment as an explicit premise. It is not in Prop. 2. It is hidden inside "a certificate guarantees $\Pr_D[\dots]\le b_k$" in (A4) and appears only in Prop. 5 ("If calibration proposals are drawn independently from $D$"). For the target reader the most important assumption, and the one that fails in the shift experiment, is invisible in the headline theorem. *Fix:* add to (A4) "where the certificates were computed on calibration data exchangeable with $D$". I also briefly confused (A1) with comparator A1 when reading the audit table (M1).

**(5) How was the approach evaluated, and what were the headline results?**
*My answer:* PRISM plus enumeration for the sink; mutation testing of the checker; Monte Carlo coverage (about 99.6% vs. 95% nominal); and a paired comparison of seven pipelines over ten seeds in a synthetic sandbox. Primary: nothing certified, block-all, defect found. Replication: TINT about 10% vs. B5 about 20% risk at equal utility (66%); shift gives 100% acceptance for all pipelines.
*Check:* Correct. **Vague:** I initially remembered "six comparators" and "seven pipelines" as two different things. Section I says "six comparators" and Table VI says "seven pipelines"; the reconciliation (six plus TINT) is never stated in one place. I also remembered the 10-point reduction as an empirical effect until I reread "Where the numbers come from" (M3).

**(6) What are the main limitations?**
*My answer:* No real LLMs, learned guards or benchmarks; a synthetic one-template-per-family fixture with labels by construction; a scripted judge; a single runtime topology; a DSL covering only 2/5 policy classes; no defense against shift, which the context ID cannot detect; an uninformative adaptive experiment; preregistration as a local git tag; no refinement proof.
*Check:* Correct and complete. The paper is very clear here; limitations are restated in four places (Section I "What we do not claim", Section IV-E, Table X, Section VII). Arguably that is too often (see m5).

## 6. Minor issues

**m1. Abstract, key sentence is hard to parse.** "the certification gate had exposed a floating-point defect in a scorer that the same pipeline without certification shipped with 29.83% unsafe acceptance." *Rewrite:* "Certification refused to deploy because a floating-point defect in the scorer made every candidate policy too risky; the same pipeline without the certification gate would have shipped at 29.83% joint unsafe acceptance."

**m2. Inconsistent comparator naming.** B5 is called "a strong rule-plus-judge control" (abstract), "strong control", "sink + exposed judge" (tables), and "naive hierarchical guardrails" (RQ3). B5 also contains the scorer, so "rule-plus-judge" is inaccurate. B4 appears as "Invalid delegation", "naive relay" (Fig. 1) and "naive routing" (H1/PRISM). B1 appears as "Sink only" and "a rule-only pipeline". *Fix:* use one name per pipeline everywhere (e.g., "B5 sink+scorer+exposed judge") and define "naive routing" = B4 once.

**m3. "Sink" collides with the taint-analysis meaning.** In IFC/taint analysis a sink is the dangerous endpoint (here, the HTTP call). TINT's "sink" is a monitor that runs *first*. The external host is also literally named `sink.invalid`. "Enforced exactly by a mandatory sink on every accepted action" (abstract) suggests an end-of-pipeline check. *Suggest:* "deterministic gate" or "mandatory policy monitor", and rename the host `exfil.invalid`.

**m4. "System 1 / System 2" (Section II-A) is introduced and never used again.** Delete it.

**m5. Redundant hedging and repetition.** "Ten seeds suit a pilot, not a powered study" (Section V-C) and "Ten seeds suffice for a pilot" (Section VII). The claim that the results do not transfer to real agents appears in the abstract, Section I, Section V intro, Answer to RQ3, Section VI, VII and VIII. Keep it in the abstract, Section I and Threats, and cut the rest.

**m6. H7 and H8 as preregistered hypotheses.** H7 is "not runnable in our environment" and H8 is "refuted, as we expected when we registered it". Preregistering a hypothesis one knows cannot be run, or expects to fail, needs a sentence of justification ("registered to bind ourselves to report it"). Otherwise it reads as padding the hypothesis table.

**m7. H6 wording is vague.** "TINT changes judge-targeting success and trades utility for lower risk". State the directional prediction, e.g., "TINT accepts no judge-targeting proposal, while B5 accepts all of them".

**m8. Over-precise reporting.** Table V gives two decimals (29.83, 20.77, 10.16) for quantities whose population values are 30, 20 and 10 by construction. Report one decimal, and give the population value alongside it.

**m9. Table VIII caption** says "Exploratory Replication by Family" but includes an "$n$ prim." column. Either drop the column or retitle: "Test Proposals per Family in Both Studies, and Acceptance (%) of B5 and TINT in the Replication".

**m10. "Six comparators" vs. "seven pipelines".** State once: "six comparators (B1–B5 and the ablation A1) plus full TINT".

**m11. Prop. 1 proof.** "Let $L_3$ be a judge that blocks every action with $Y=1$ when it receives the structured view". That premise is impossible for the actual structured judge, which misses implicit patches. Add "(an idealized judge; the point is the vacuous discharge, not judge quality)".

**m12. Section IV-D, escalation rule.** "which the minimizer drives to its sign" is unclear. *Rewrite:* "so the minimizer sets $q(x)=1$ where this coefficient is negative and $q(x)=0$ where it is positive."

**m13. The horizon statement** "the probability of at least one unsafe acceptance is at most $HR$ under a per-step version of the same premise" is unexplained and never used. Either explain what "per-step premise" means (non-adaptive, each step drawn from $D$) or drop it.

**m14. "Most stacks we examined in the literature" (Section VI).** This is unsupported (see M5).

**m15. Data Availability** refers to "the project repository" without a link. For double-blind review, provide an anonymized link (e.g., anonymous.4open.science). Otherwise referees cannot exercise the verifiability that is the paper's strongest point.

**m16. The CPU comparison** (103.4 µs vs. 2.1 µs median) is described as "negligible next to an LLM judge's latency, which we did not measure". The last clause undercuts the first. Write "...likely negligible next to an LLM judge's latency (typically hundreds of milliseconds), which we did not measure."

**m17. Fig. 3 / spec explanation.** See M7: explain `semantic` in $L_1$'s scope, the duplication of `mandatory` and `rules`, and the absence of $L_4$.

**m18. Section I "What we do not claim"** is placed before the roadmap and partly repeats the Limitations. Consider shortening it to two sentences and pointing to Section IV-E.

**m19. Related-work table (Table XI).** TINT rates itself "Y" in five of six columns while relying on a reading of others in which "U" is common. Give one sentence on the coding procedure (who rated, what counts as "established"), or drop the table in favor of the prose.

**Sentences that read unnaturally or mechanically.** The manuscript has a recurring tic: a short declarative followed by an "X, not Y" correction. There are 18 occurrences of ", not " in the source, plus many "no ... ; no ..." triplets. Individually they are fine. Together they give a clipped, machine-like rhythm. Specific sentences to rewrite:

## 7. Questions to the authors

1. If the `trust` field were removed from the DevAgent specification while sink, quarantine and the end-to-end certificate were kept, which result in the paper would change? If none, what is the evaluated contribution of the checker beyond topology validation (M2)?
2. Can you instantiate Prop. 2 with $m=2$ routes on DevAgent and report the per-route bounds? Does the union over two route certificates give a tighter or looser end-to-end bound than the whole-policy certificate at the same $\delta$?
3. What structured view would you give a *real* LLM judge for $\varphi_3$ on coding tasks, and how would its contract's assumptions be discharged when that view is itself produced by a model reading untrusted text (M4)?
4. Have you tried writing a TINT specification for an existing open guardrail configuration (NeMo Guardrails, LlamaFirewall)? Which edges could not be discharged?
5. Besides failing closed, could the certification step have *diagnosed* the floating-point defect, e.g., by reporting that all 11 thresholds had identical calibration losses? Would you recommend such a "degenerate grid" warning as part of the discipline?
6. How should a practitioner choose the context identifier so that it is more than a configuration string, e.g., by binding it to a drift test on scorer-score distributions? Did you consider a lightweight two-sample test on the shift split as an optional runtime premise?
7. For multi-step agent sessions, the $HR$ horizon bound requires non-adaptive steps, but agent sessions are inherently adaptive: later actions depend on earlier tool outputs. What, if anything, does TINT guarantee for a session?
8. Why was B5's threshold fixed at $t=0.20$ rather than tuned on development data? Would a tuned B5 change any conclusion?
