# Review — Reviewer 1 (formal methods, runtime verification, assume-guarantee/contract-based design)

**Manuscript:** "Trust Is Not Transitive: Checked Assume-Guarantee Composition of Hierarchical Guardrails for LLM Agents" (IEEE TSE submission)

Line numbers below refer to `paper/main.tex`. Code citations are to the repository at its current state. I re-ran `scripts/process_results.py --verify`, which reported "Processed summary re-derived byte-for-byte from both preserved raw runs". `pytest --collect-only` collects 56 tests, as claimed.

---

## 1. Summary

The paper looks at layered guardrail stacks for tool-using LLM agents: rules, then a cheap scorer, then an LLM judge, then a human. It argues that these stacks usually consume one layer's verdict without discharging the assumptions that verdict depends on. It proposes TINT, which has four parts: (i) a closed JSON DSL of layers and "conditioned trust edges", checked by a syntactic checker that never infers edges; (ii) a mandatory deterministic sink for two typed information-flow/approval policies, evaluated first on every action; (iii) a "quarantined" judge that receives only a closed record of six Boolean facts and the scorer's score; and (iv) Clopper–Pearson certificates with a union bound over a finite threshold grid, bound to a SHA-256 fingerprint of the configuration and re-checked at runtime. The formal part is a countermodel (Prop. 1), a scoped soundness statement (Prop. 2), veto-versus-cascade risk identities (Props. 3–4), a certified-selection lemma (Prop. 5) and a Lagrangian escalation rule (Prop. 6). The evaluation is a preregistered study in a fully scripted, typed, in-memory sandbox. In the primary run nothing could be certified, so TINT blocked everything; this exposed a floating-point boundary defect in the scorer. In a fresh-seed replication after the fix, TINT's joint unsafe acceptance was 10.16% against 20.77% for a sink-plus-exposed-judge control. The authors themselves attribute the whole difference to one designed fixture family and show that every pipeline fails on an encoded shift family.

## 2. Scores and recommendation

| Criterion | Score (1–5) | One-line justification |
|---|---|---|
| i) Novelty | 2 | Each mechanism is a known instance (reference monitor, dual-LLM/CaMeL-style quarantine, Learn-then-Test-style family certification, configuration hashing). What is new is how they are combined and the honest negative evidence, but the paper does not isolate this sharply, and important prior work on probabilistic contracts and on trust non-transitivity is missing. |
| ii) Rigor | 2 | The propositions are mostly correct but elementary. The central "checked composition" claim has no theorem connecting the checker to the guarantee. Evidence atoms have no semantics. Several statements are imprecise or unproved. Two pieces of "formal" evidence (the PRISM model and `formal/check.py`) are vacuous. The checker's complexity claim is contradicted by the code and the paper's own data. |
| iii) Relevance | 4 | The problem matters to TSE readers, and the engineering lessons (write edges down, sink first, measure the pipeline, bind evidence) are useful. |
| iv) Verifiability & transparency | 4 | The artifact is exemplary: generated macros, byte-identical re-derivation, decision replay, preserved defective run, dated amendments, candid assumption audit. Points are lost for formal artifacts that verify less than the text implies, and for a calibration path that is not the deployed code path. |
| v) Presentation | 4 | Clear, well organised and unusually candid. Some notation is overloaded, a few headings and abstract sentences overclaim, and a few code/paper mismatches remain. |

**Overall recommendation: Major revision.**

Most of my concerns can be fixed with the existing evidence by tightening the formalism, correcting several claims and re-framing the empirical results. The question of whether the contribution is substantial enough for TSE depends on that re-framing. As it stands, the formal core is thinner than the title ("Checked Assume-Guarantee Composition") promises, and the empirical gains are true by construction of the fixtures.

## 3. Strengths

- **Unusual honesty about scope and failure.** The primary null result is preserved (no certificate; block-all, l. 1055). The defect fix is evaluated on fresh seeds and labeled exploratory. Shift failure is reported (l. 1078). The adaptive instrument is declared uninformative and its misattribution corrected (l. 1080). There is a "What we do not claim" paragraph (l. 446). This is a model for the field.
- **The distinction between joint risk, miss rate and accepted conditional risk** (l. 547) is stated precisely and used consistently. This alone would clear up confusion in much of the guardrail literature.
- **The strong control B5** (sink + exposed judge) is designed so that quarantine is not credited with what the sink alone achieves (l. 581, l. 997). This is exactly the right ablation discipline.
- **The cascade-versus-veto clarification** (Prop. 3(d)) and the parity example (Prop. 4) are simple but practically important corrections to the "miss rates multiply" folklore.
- **The assumption audit** (Table 9, l. 1090–1112) separates "checked", "true by construction" and "assumed". This is the most valuable table in the paper.
- **Artifact engineering is excellent.** Generated macros with provenance, `process_results.py --verify` (re-ran successfully), `validate_replay.py` re-deriving all 336,000 decisions, a closed parser rejecting duplicate keys (`src/tint/spec/__init__.py:49-57`), and a fail-closed judge wrapper (`src/tint/layers/__init__.py:78-83`).
- **The finite-multiplier counterexample to Prop. 6** (l. 814) is correct and is the kind of premise-checking that is often skipped.

## 4. Major issues

### A. Issues fixable by revising the manuscript with existing evidence

**M1. No theorem connects the checked specification to the end-to-end guarantee; the "checked composition" claim is not formalised.**

*Where.* Prop. 2 (l. 739–746), the paragraph at l. 724, Contribution 2 (l. 434).

*Problem.* Prop. 2 is the paper's soundness result, yet it mentions neither trust edges, the DSL, nor the checker (Alg. 1). Its premises are:
- A1, complete mediation;
- A2, a correct sink on every acceptance;
- A3, no overwrite;
- A4, route certificates.

None of these is established by the checker:
- A2 ("every acceptance passes a deterministic monitor") is a property of the routing graph. Alg. 1 does not check it. Line 2 of Alg. 1, and `src/tint/spec/__init__.py:84-87`, only compare `mandatory` and `rules` to the literal set `{'production_approval','secret_egress'}`.
- A2 and A3 are established by the hard-coded runtime topology (`src/tint/runtime/__init__.py:38-42`, `:64-71`), not by any specification-level reasoning.

Consequently the claim "every trust relation between layers is an explicit assume-guarantee obligation that is checked before deployment" (abstract) does no logical work in any proposition. The proof of Prop. 2(ii) also never uses the runtime premise checks listed in A4: given "a certificate guarantees Pr_D[Y=1 ∧ E_k] ≤ b_k", the conclusion is pure arithmetic. The fingerprint check is what should make the *deployed* event E_k equal (or be contained in) the *certified* event, and the proposition hides exactly that step.

*Fix.*
1. Add a definition of an *implementation* of a specification. Each evidence item e carries an interpretation ⟦e⟧ (a predicate on the callee's input/view, or on the calibration relationship). An implementation *realises* an edge (i,j,p,E) if ⟦e⟧ holds of every invocation of j through that edge, for every e ∈ E.
2. State and prove a "checked composition" theorem: if `check(S)` accepts and the implementation realises every edge, then A_j holds on every invocation of j, so G_j holds. If, in addition, every route to `allow` traverses the sink and no block is overwritten (which you must either add to Alg. 1 as a graph condition or state as a runtime premise), then conclusions (i)–(ii) of Prop. 2 follow.
3. Add a one-line monotonicity lemma: if the runtime's acceptance set is contained pointwise in the acceptance set of the certified policy, then R_runtime ≤ R_certified. State that the fingerprint check is how containment is argued, and that it is only as strong as M6 allows.

Without this, the title's "Checked Assume-Guarantee Composition" is not justified.

**M2. Evidence items are uninterpreted strings; Definition 3 has a type mismatch; distributional assumptions cannot be "discharged" at an edge, and the shift experiment is an instance of failure mode F1 inside TINT.**

*Where.* Def. 3 (l. 563–566); Fig. 3 (l. 679–699); l. 724; l. 801; l. 1078; Table 9 row "Context matches distribution".

*Problem.*

(a) Def. 3 requires "A_j ⊆ E". A_j is a set of assumptions (predicates about views, provenance and the calibration context; Def. 2, l. 558), while E is a set of evidence *items*. Inclusion between them is only meaningful under an unstated identification of predicates with names. The checker indeed compares strings (`spec/__init__.py:99`).

(b) Because items have no semantics, "discharge" is nominal. The runtime does not read `requires` or `provides` at all. It checks only layer names, views and the edge triple set (`runtime/__init__.py:38-42`). So a specification in which L3 requires nothing, and edge L2→L3 carries no evidence, is not accepted by the runtime only because the edge evidence happens to be absent from that check. Its quarantine behaviour would be identical in any case, since `quarantine()` is called unconditionally (`runtime/__init__.py:85`). The declarations are reviewable documentation, not obligations whose truth the system depends on.

(c) Evidence is attributed to the *caller layer* ("every item of E is provided by i"). In Fig. 3, L2, the raw-text scorer, "provides" `quarantine`, and L1, a metadata rule layer, "provides" `calibrated_context`. Neither layer can establish these facts. They are established, if at all, by the trusted mediator (the runtime builds the quarantined record; the host supplies the certificate). In contract-based design this is the role of a *connector* or the composition operator, not of a component.

(d) Most importantly, `calibrated_context` is a *distributional* assumption: calibration and deployment are exchangeable. No trusted code at an edge can discharge it per decision. The paper concedes this at l. 801 and in the shift result (l. 1078): "The certificate was still accepted, because the context identifier is a configuration string". But this means that under shift, TINT consumed L2's and L3's verdicts while an assumption declared discharged was false. That is precisely F1 (l. 613), the failure mode the paper sets out to eliminate.

*Fix.* Make the DSL distinguish the three kinds of evidence that Table 9 already distinguishes informally:
- *checked* — a runtime predicate with a named checking function;
- *by construction* — established by the fixed topology or the code path;
- *assumed* — external, e.g. exchangeability.

Have the checker output the residual set of *assumed* obligations per edge as part of its verdict. Attribute evidence to edges or connectors (trusted mediator), not to caller layers. Then restate Problem (i) (l. 617) and the abstract so that "checked" applies only to the first kind. Explicitly acknowledge in Sec. V-RQ3 that the shift failure is an undischarged-assumption failure of the same type as F1, which the discipline makes *visible* (via the audit) but does not *prevent*.

**M3. Contract semantics: per-run and distribution-level assumptions are mixed; there is no composition operator; the meaning of a hierarchical guarantee, and hence of "non-transitivity", is ambiguous.**

*Where.* Def. 2 (l. 557–559); l. 565–568; Fig. 3 caption ("There is no edge from L1 to L3, and none is inferred", l. 698).

*Problem.* Def. 2 says "L_i satisfies C_i if G_i holds whenever A_i holds". G_i may be "Pr_D[Y=1 ∧ L_i allows] ≤ b_i", a property of a distribution, while A_i includes "the view" of an individual action, a per-run property. "Whenever A_i holds" is therefore ill-typed for statistical guarantees: over which runs, or which D, is the probability taken when A_i holds only on some runs? Probabilistic and stochastic contract theories exist precisely to make this precise (assumptions as sets of environments or distributions, guarantees as probabilistic properties, with defined refinement and composition). The paper defines no composition operator ⊗ on contracts, so "composition" is used informally.

A related ambiguity concerns what G_2 is. Operationally, L1's final verdict *is* L3's verdict whenever the request is escalated (`runtime/__init__.py:85-89`). So L1 does consume L3's verdict, via L2, without an L1→L3 edge. This is sound only if G_2 denotes the guarantee of the *subsystem* {L2, L3} (hierarchical composition) rather than of L2 alone. In that reading, trust *is* composed, legitimately, by A/G composition. The paper's slogan ("Edges are never derived: from i→j and j→k nothing follows about i→k") is then a statement about the *syntax* of the DSL, while the semantics relies on composition. The paper itself notes that non-transitivity "is a design rule here, not a theorem" (l. 568). The rhetoric of the title and introduction suggests more.

*Fix.*
1. Restrict Def. 2 to one of two forms: (a) per-run contracts with hard guarantees; (b) distributional contracts where A_i is a set of input distributions (e.g., {D : D = D_cal}, or TV(D, D_cal) ≤ η) and G_i is a probabilistic property of the composite.
2. Define the composition of a caller with its callee subtree, and state explicitly that L1→L2 trusts the *composite* guarantee of {L2, L3}.
3. Reword l. 565–568 and the Fig. 3 caption accordingly: "trust is composed only along declared edges whose obligations are discharged; it is never short-circuited".
4. Position this against stochastic/probabilistic contract frameworks (see M10).

**M4. Proposition 1 is near-trivial as stated and illustrates the wrong mechanism.**

*Where.* Prop. 1 and proof (l. 572–579); l. 581; H4 (l. 997).

*Problems.*

(a) Any contract with a false assumption is vacuously satisfied. "Every layer satisfies its contract and the composition is unsafe" therefore follows for any composition in which assumptions are not established, so the existence statement carries almost no information. What carries information is *which composition rule is violated*: the circular or undischarged use of G_3 by L2 without A_3. The proposition should say so.

(b) The countermodel violates φ_det, so the fix it motivates is the sink, which the authors acknowledge (l. 581, l. 997). The trace that motivates *quarantine* is the judge-targeting family: a patch with φ_det true and Y=1 (l. 905). Prop. 1 should be built on that trace. As it stands, the formal countermodel and the empirical quarantine result are about different traces.

(c) The L3 in the proof "blocks every action with Y=1 when it receives the structured view". This requires Y to be a function of the structured view. The paper's own implicit-patch family shows this is false in DevAgent (l. 905). The existence claim is still fine (choose a law in which Y is view-measurable), but say so explicitly.

(d) In the proof, L1 is a "relay". In Sec. II-E and everywhere else, L1 is the rule layer. Please use a different name or explain.

*Fix.* Restate as: "There is a composition in which each L_i satisfies C_i, every executed action satisfies φ_det, and an action with Y=1 executes, because L2 uses G_3 although A_3 is not established." Use the judge-targeting patch, keep the current φ_det version as a remark, and name the violated rule (e.g., Abadi–Lamport's composition theorem requires each A_j to be implied by the environment's guarantees).

**M5. Propositions 3 and 4: missing premises, an unproved negative claim, undefined events, and missing standard citations.**

*Where.* l. 753–778.

- (c) "all guards share one uniform random variable with probability ρ" does not by itself determine the misses. The formula requires the threshold model "guard i misses iff U_i < m_i", which appears only in FRAMEWORK.md (T4) and in `experiments/run.py:153-157`. Add it to the statement.
- (d) "neither the minimum in (a) nor the product in (b) is a valid bound" is asserted, but the proof only says L3's miss rate does not enter its route. Give the one-line argument. In a cascade where L2 never blocks (as implemented: `runtime/__init__.py:81-85`), R ≥ Pr(M_2), and Pr(M_2) can exceed Pr(M_3), so the minimum is not an upper bound. Give a concrete numerical instance.
- For cascade layers with three outputs, "L_i allows" in M_i is undefined. Does "allow" mean "accept" or "not block"? For L2 in the implemented cascade it must mean s < t. Define it.
- The tightness claims (l. 778) are the Fréchet–Hoeffding upper bound; Prop. 4 is Bernstein's classical example of pairwise-but-not-mutually-independent events. Cite both. This does not diminish their pedagogical value but positions them honestly as known facts.
- The "tightness" of the route sum is an identity, not a bound. It is exact by Prop. 2(ii), so "no smaller bound follows from route certificates alone" needs the quantifier stated: over all laws consistent with the certificates.

**M6. Certificate binding is overclaimed: the fingerprint binds self-declared names, the view is a constant, and the certified code path is not the deployed code path.**

*Where.* l. 801; Fig. 2; Prop. 2 (A4: "the runtime checks each certificate's view, version and context premises"); abstract (iv); l. 1082.

*Problems.*
- The fingerprint hashes the specification, the threshold, and the *self-reported* `version` attributes of the scorer and judge (`runtime/__init__.py:55-56`, `:72-73`, `:100-106`). The "view" component is the literal string `'structured-v1'` (`:105`), and `applies` is called with that same literal (`:74`). So the view check compares a constant with a constant. The context check compares two host-supplied constants (`context_id` constructor argument versus certificate field).
- Recomputation per decision recomputes a hash of the same `self` fields. It detects only in-process mutation of those fields, not, for example, replacement of `self.scorer` by an object with the same version string.
- The calibration losses that the certificate summarises are computed by `tint.baselines.decide('checked_quarantine', ...)` (`experiments/run.py:63-70`), with the module-level `LexicalScorer` monkey-patched (`run.py:238-239`). The deployed policy is `GuardRuntime.evaluate`. These are two implementations of the same policy; nothing binds the certificate to the function that generated its evidence. `validate_replay.py` checks retained decisions, which is good, but that is an after-the-fact consistency check, not binding.

The paper acknowledges "plugin version strings are honest" (l. 820), but the abstract and l. 801 ("bound to the exact configuration") read as if the binding were content-based.

*Fix.*
1. Reword: "bound to a fingerprint of the specification, threshold and *declared* component identities"; state that view and context are configuration constants.
2. Either hash component code (e.g., module source or wheel digest) or explicitly scope the binding to declared identities.
3. Compute calibration losses through `GuardRuntime` (with certificate checks disabled) so that the certified and deployed functions are the same object; failing that, state the equivalence assumption in Table 9.
4. Remove "re-checks the certificate's premises" from Fig. 2's caption, or qualify it ("checks that the certificate's recorded configuration equals the running configuration").

**M7. Parts of the formal evidence for H1 are vacuous.**

*Where.* H1 (l. 960, l. 966); Table 3 row "Finite abstraction"; Table 9 row "Sink correctness".

*Problems.*
- `formal/cascade.pm` is a deterministic five-state chain whose transitions are keyed on the *trace index*: `s=1 & checked & trace!=1 -> (s'=3)` (block). The outcome of each canonical trace is written into the model. Checking "P=? [F bad_execute]" therefore verifies a lookup table. It does not model the sink predicate, the judge or the routing.
- `formal/check.py:29-33` enumerates 64 bit vectors, but its six bits are (destructive, production, approved, secret, external, *semantic_allow*), not the sink's six facts. It defines `execute = semantic_allow and not bad` and then asserts `not (execute and bad)`, which is a tautology. It never calls `tint.layers.deterministic`.

The genuinely meaningful check is the pytest `test_all_deterministic_cases` (`tests/test_core.py:72-76`), which calls the real sink on all 64 `ActionView`s. Note that the sink correctness it verifies is itself almost definitional: φ_det is defined over the same six facts the sink computes.

*Fix.*
1. Attribute the 64-input enumeration to `tests/test_core.py`.
2. Either drop the PRISM claim, or replace it with a model that verifies something. A suitable model is an MDP in which the agent's action fields, the provenance labels, and the scorer and judge verdicts are all *nondeterministic*. Then show P_max[F bad_execute] = 0 under checked routing (for every judge behaviour, including adversarial), and > 0 under naive routing. That would be a real, cheap, judge-agnostic verification of Prop. 2(i) over the abstraction, and the paper would gain from it.
3. Clarify in Prop. 2 that (i) reduces to A1 plus the definitional correctness of the sink.

**M8. The checker's linear-complexity claim is contradicted by the code and by the paper's own data.**

*Where.* l. 702 ("linear in the number of layers and edges, multiplied by the size of the evidence sets"); l. 968 ("Checking time grows linearly with the number of edges"); Fig. 4 caption.

*Problem.* The scope test is `edge.property not in caller.scope & callee.scope` (`src/tint/spec/__init__.py:97`). It builds the intersection of the two scope sets for *every edge*, costing O(min(|P_i|, |P_j|)). In the scaling study every layer's scope contains all |P| properties and there are |P|·(L−1) edges (`experiments/run.py:170-172`), so the check is Θ(|P|²·L).

The paper's own macros show this (l. 313–315):
- 2 layers: 250→500 properties, 0.39→1.99 ms (×5.1 for ×2 edges);
- 6 layers: 1.96→9.74 ms (×5.0).

The log–log slope at the upper end is about 2, not 1.

*Fix.* Either change the test to `edge.property in caller.scope and edge.property in callee.scope` and re-run the (cheap) scaling study, or correct the claim to O(|E|·max|P_i| + Σ|E_e|). Fit a slope to Fig. 4 and report it. The milliseconds are small either way; what matters is the accuracy of the claim.

**M9. The empirical results for quarantine and certification hold by construction, and two framings overclaim.**

*Where.* Abstract; l. 1055 ("The certification gate turned a silent defect into a visible, safe failure"); the l. 1057 heading "After the fix, quarantine halved risk relative to the strong control"; l. 1074–1076; Table 5; RQ3 answer (l. 1084).

*Problems.*

(a) The `ExposedJudge` returns `allow` iff the issue contains the literal string "reviewer must answer safe" (`src/tint/layers/__init__.py:68-71`). The judge-targeting family contains exactly this string (`experiments/dataset.py:36`). The 10-point gap between B5 and TINT is the mass of that family (l. 1074), so it is fully determined by the authors' choice of backdoor and family weight (`dataset.py:7`). "Quarantine halved risk" is therefore not an empirical finding. It is an executable restatement of the design. The authors say much of this, but the heading and the abstract still present it as an effect.

(b) The quarantined judge sees only the six facts and the score s (`layers/__init__.py:41-44`, `:51-61`), and decides `block` iff (secret ∧ external) or s ≥ 0.40. For sink-passing actions, the whole semantic cascade therefore reduces to "accept iff s < 0.40". Any threshold t ∈ [0.1, 0.4] gives identical decisions, which is why "certification contributed nothing" (l. 1074). Also, "every seed certified threshold 0.10" is a lexicographic tie-break among equal-cost candidates (`synthesis/__init__.py:31`), not a finding.

This exposes a conceptual point that deserves discussion. A quarantined judge whose view is (facts, s) can never add information beyond the scorer and the rules, so in this design the cascade theory (Prop. 3(d), Prop. 6) is not exercised at all. What would a quarantined view contain in practice for the judge to be useful: typed plan values in the style of CaMeL, structured diffs, a capability set? This is the crux of whether quarantine and an LLM judge can coexist.

(c) "The certification gate had exposed a floating-point defect" (abstract) overstates. The gate detected that every candidate's bound exceeded the budget (calibrated risk about 30% versus ε = 20%). The defect was diagnosed by humans inspecting counts (l. 1055). With ε = 35% the gate would have certified the defective scorer. Any pre-deployment held-out evaluation with a risk budget would have flagged the same problem; A1's 29.83% is exactly such an evaluation.

(d) Because decisions are deterministic per family, the Wilcoxon, Holm and Â12 machinery (l. 958, l. 1057, l. 1076) adds nothing. The authors say this, but they still report p-values and effect sizes as if informative. Similarly, the primary "difference" of −29.83 points between TINT (block-all) and the comparators is a block-all artefact.

*Fix.*
1. Retitle the l. 1057 paragraph, e.g., "After the fix: quarantine removed the designed judge-targeting family".
2. In the abstract, replace "the certification gate had exposed a floating-point defect" with "the certification gate refused to deploy, and inspection of the calibration counts revealed a floating-point defect".
3. Replace the inferential statistics with exact population values (which the authors already compute) plus multinomial sampling intervals, or move them to a footnote with the caveat.
4. Add a paragraph on the information-theoretic limit of the quarantined view in this design (judge = second threshold on s) and on what a richer structured view would need to contain.

**M10. Novelty and positioning: important prior work is missing, and the comparison table self-scores generously.**

*Where.* l. 422, l. 446, Sec. VIII (l. 1146–1186), Table 10.

*Missing or under-discussed work that a TSE formal-methods reader will expect:*
- **Non-transitivity of trust is a classic phrase in security.** Christianson and Harbison, "Why isn't trust transitive?" (Security Protocols Workshop 1996, LNCS 1189), and the trust-management literature (e.g., Jøsang's work on trust transitivity). The title makes this the central slogan; the paper cites only Misra–Chandy and Abadi–Lamport (l. 446).
- **Probabilistic and stochastic contracts.** Delahaye, Caillaud and Legay, "Probabilistic contracts" (FMSD 2011); Nuzzo et al., stochastic assume-guarantee contracts for CPS (ACM TECS 2019); Incer et al. on contract algebra, hypothesis testing of contracts and Pacti. These address exactly the per-run versus distributional assumption problem in M3.
- **Contracts for learning-enabled components.** E.g., perception contracts (Astorga et al., OOPSLA 2023) and statistical assume-guarantee work for DNN components.
- **Runtime enforcement theory beyond Schneider.** Ligatti, Bauer and Walker's edit automata; Falcone et al. on runtime enforcement monitors. These are relevant to "sink first, no overwrite".
- **Conformal or statistical runtime monitoring.** E.g., Lindemann et al., conformal prediction for STL runtime verification (ICCPS 2023), and statistical model checking.
- **The "dual LLM" pattern** (Willison, 2023), which is the direct precursor of the quarantined judge, alongside CaMeL.
- **Learn-then-Test** (Angelopoulos et al., cited as `angelopoulos2021learn`). The certified selection of Sec. IV-D is a special case (finite family, union-bound FWER, Clopper–Pearson p-values). Say so explicitly next to Prop. 5.

*On Table 10.* TINT is marked "Y" in every column, including "Stat." and "Trust". Under M1, M2 and M6, TINT's "Trust" is syntactic and the statistical guarantee is a single whole-policy Clopper–Pearson bound. Meanwhile CaMeL/Fides, which have a stronger data-flow story (as the authors acknowledge at l. 1154), get "U" for "Stat." A fairer table would use "S" for TINT's "Trust" (syntactic declarations; runtime-enforced only for one topology) and add a column for "compositional semantics / proof".

*How to sharpen the contribution.* Its most defensible novel element is this: certificate applicability is treated as an *explicit, runtime-checked premise of a trust edge*, so that statistical evidence and deterministic enforcement are kept in separate compartments, together with an honest empirical demonstration of where that separation helps and where it does not (shift). Say this in one sentence in the introduction, demote the elementary propositions to "supporting lemmas", and give the three-kind evidence typing of M2 as the conceptual contribution. That would be a crisper and more defensible claim than "a checked composition discipline with explicit premises".

**M11. Several statements in Sec. IV-E and Sec. III-A are asserted without proof or precise quantification.**

- l. 823: "against an unconstrained adaptive attacker, no non-trivial distribution-free bound exists short of exact deterministic mediation or blocking". Give the two-line argument: if the policy accepts some x with Y(x) = 1, the attacker plays x, so R = Pr[Y=1]. Also define "non-trivial".
- l. 822: the TV bound applies to a *fixed* policy. For the selected policy it should read R_Q ≤ u_j + η on the 1−δ event. Say this.
- l. 547: "over a horizon of H decisions, the probability of at least one unsafe acceptance is at most HR under a per-step version of the same premise". State the premise: each step's (h, a) has marginal D conditional on the past, or at least Pr[Y_t = 1 ∧ allow_t] ≤ R for every t. Note that agent trajectories rarely satisfy this.
- Prop. 2(i): "Induction over trace prefixes extends this to every prefix". Because φ_det is a per-action state invariant, induction is unnecessary. What *is* needed is atomicity between the check and the effect: approval and provenance state must not change between `evaluate` and the effect. This holds in `Sandbox.call` (`src/tint/agents/__init__.py:32-45`, single-threaded, same call) and should be stated as part of A1.
- Prop. 5 requires i.i.d. calibration from D *and* that the candidate loss vectors are computed by the deployed policy functions (see M6). Background (l. 471) says that all these guarantees assume exchangeability. Clopper–Pearson needs i.i.d. Bernoulli trials; exchangeability is the conformal premise.

### B. Issues that would require new experiments (flagged separately)

- **N1.** A judge that can add information under quarantine: a learned or LLM judge on a richer structured view. Without it, the cascade and escalation theory (Prop. 3(d), Prop. 6) is not exercised (M9(b)).
- **N2.** Per-route certificates with m > 1 (Prop. 2(ii) as stated) are never instantiated; the implementation uses only m = 1 (l. 748). Either exercise m > 1, or simplify Prop. 2 to the whole-policy case and move the route version to a remark. The latter needs no experiment.
- **N3.** A meaningful model-checking run with nondeterministic scorer and judge (M7). This is cheap.
- **N4.** Checker validation beyond four syntactic mutations matched one-to-one to the checker's own reject lines: bounded-exhaustive generation of all small specifications (e.g., ≤ 3 layers, ≤ 2 properties, ≤ 2 evidence atoms), compared against an independent definitional implementation of Def. 3. The current H2 result (perfect precision and recall) holds by construction. There is also no mutation for "E ⊄ provides(i)", the raw-view/quarantine rule, or duplicate edges (`experiments/run.py:174-177`).
- **N5.** An adaptive attacker that actually uses the guard's responses (acknowledged at l. 1080).

## 6. Questions to the authors

1. What exactly is G_2 in the DevAgent specification: the guarantee of L2 alone, or of the composite {L2, L3}? If the former, how does L1's acceptance of an L3-decided action follow from declared edges without an L1→L3 edge?
2. Which party establishes `quarantine` and `calibrated_context`: the caller layer (as Def. 3 says) or the runtime/host? Would you accept moving `provides` from layers to edges or connectors?
3. Can the checker be extended to verify A2 (every route to `allow` contains a mandatory-sink layer) as a graph property, so that Prop. 2(i) follows from `check(S)` plus a realisation premise rather than from the hard-coded topology?
4. Why are calibration losses computed with `baselines.decide` rather than with `GuardRuntime` (`experiments/run.py:63-70`)? Has equivalence of the two decision functions been established beyond the replay check?
5. Is there any configuration in your design space in which a *quarantined* judge changes a decision relative to "accept iff s < 0.40 and the sink passes"? If not, what is the intended content of a structured view for a real LLM judge?
6. Under per-route certification (m > 1), how would you allocate δ across routes, and what is gained over a whole-policy certificate, given that changing L3 changes the events E_k of all routes downstream of L2?
7. Would you consider re-running the checker scaling study after replacing the per-edge set intersection (`spec/__init__.py:97`) with two membership tests, and reporting the fitted slope?
8. How does the discipline relate formally to stochastic assume-guarantee contracts (Nuzzo et al.) and probabilistic contracts (Delahaye et al.)? Could Def. 2 be instantiated in one of those frameworks to obtain a composition theorem for free?
