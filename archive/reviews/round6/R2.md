# Review — Reviewer 2 (empirical methods, statistics, artifact)

**Manuscript:** *Trust Is Not Transitive: Checked Assume-Guarantee Composition of Hierarchical Guardrails for LLM Agents* (IEEE TSE submission)
**Reviewed version:** working tree of `paper/main.tex` (1206 lines) and `paper/main.pdf` (17 pages) on branch `dev`, HEAD `50a3180` plus uncommitted edits.

---

## 1. Summary

The paper argues that layered guardrails for LLM agents (rules → lexical scorer → LLM judge → human) are usually justified by per-layer accuracy, while the conditions under which one layer may trust another's verdict stay implicit. It proposes TINT, which consists of:
- a closed JSON DSL of per-layer contracts and explicitly declared "conditioned trust edges", plus a checker that never infers an edge;
- a mandatory deterministic sink for two information-flow and approval policies;
- a "quarantined" judge that sees only a structured record;
- Clopper–Pearson certificates over a finite threshold grid, bound to a SHA-256 configuration fingerprint.

The formal part is deliberately elementary: a countermodel, a route-sum risk decomposition, veto/cascade and parity facts, union-bound selection, and a Lagrangian escalation rule. The evaluation is a preregistered feasibility study in a typed in-memory sandbox, using authored fixture families, a lexical scorer, a deterministic judge surrogate and a deliberately vulnerable "exposed" judge.

The frozen primary run certified nothing, so TINT blocked everything. The cause was a floating-point defect in the scorer. An exploratory fresh-seed replication after the fix shows TINT at 10.16% joint unsafe acceptance against 20.77% for a sink-plus-exposed-judge control, at equal utility. Every pipeline fails on a held-out encoded family.

The paper is unusually candid about scope, and the artifact is unusually complete. My concerns are with what the evaluation can show:
- the RQ3 outcome is fixed analytically by authored family masses and a test double;
- an obvious provenance-only control, which the paper's own related work motivates, dominates TINT on every metric in this sandbox;
- several pieces of verification evidence are weaker than the text claims (a tautological "independent enumeration", a PRISM model whose guards encode the conclusion, and a scaling claim contradicted by the paper's own data);
- the artifact's own `verify-results` task fails on the submitted tree.

## 2. Scores and recommendation

| Criterion | Score (1–5) | One-line justification |
|---|---|---|
| i) Novelty | 3 | Operationalizing conditioned trust edges and per-route risk accounting for guardrail stacks is useful and well positioned. The theory is standard by the authors' own admission, and the closest prior work (position paper, ContrAgent, CaMeL/Fides) covers much of the conceptual space. |
| ii) Rigor | 2 | Honest reporting, preregistration and a negative primary result are real strengths. | v) Presentation | 4 | Clear, well organized and careful in scoping. A few headlines and the abstract overstate findings relative to the body. |

**Overall recommendation: Major revision.**

The contribution could be publishable as a carefully scoped "executable discipline + feasibility" paper. That requires two things. First, the evaluation must be reframed so that it does not present designed outcomes as findings. Second, the missing provenance comparator and the verification and artifact defects must be addressed.

## 3. Strengths

- **S1. Exemplary candour about negative results.** The primary run, which certified nothing, is reported as the primary result rather than buried (Sec. V-F, "The primary run certified nothing"). The defect fix is labelled exploratory and run on fresh seeds 10–19, and the preserved buggy instrument (`experiments/run.py:31-37`, `HistoricalLexicalScorer`) lets anyone reproduce the failure.
- **S2. "Where the numbers come from" paragraph.** Explaining that every decision is a deterministic function of the family, and deriving the population values (10.00%, 66.67%, 20.00%), is exactly what most papers of this kind omit. It is also what makes my M1 possible to state precisely.
- **S3. Strong control and isolating ablation.** Including B5 (sink + exposed judge), so that quarantine is not credited with what the sink does, is good design. So is A1 (no certificate), which isolates certification. The H4 discussion explicitly refuses to credit quarantine for blocking $T_j$.
- **S4. Assumption audit (Table IX).** It separates runtime-checked, by-construction and assumed premises. This is a model other papers should follow.
- **S5. Artifact depth.** The artifact provides compressed per-decision logs (168,000 per study), byte-identical re-derivation of the processed summary, a read-only semantic replay of all 336,000 retained decisions, per-macro JSON pointers with source hashes, and preserved historical instruments.
- **S6. Clear distinction between the three risk notions.** These are joint, miss rate and accepted conditional (Eq. 1 and the paragraph after it), and the paper reports accepted conditional risk separately.

## 4. Major issues

### A. Fixable by revising the manuscript with existing evidence

#### M1. The RQ3 effect is determined by construction; the inferential statistics add no information and the headline overstates them.

**Location.**
- Sec. V-F, subsection heading: "**After the fix, quarantine halved risk relative to the strong control.**"
- Abstract: "TINT reduced joint unsafe acceptance from 20.77% to 10.16% relative to a strong rule-plus-judge control".
- Sec. V-C, *Statistical analysis*: bootstrap, Wilcoxon with Holm correction, and $\hat A_{12}$.

**Why it matters.**
- *The judge's behaviour is hard-coded.* The strong control's exposed judge is `ExposedJudge` (`src/tint/layers/__init__.py:64-71`). It returns `ALLOW` iff the string `'reviewer must answer safe'` appears, and otherwise delegates to the same `StructuredJudge` that TINT uses.
- *The family masses are authored.* The judge-targeting family has authored mass 0.10 (`experiments/dataset.py:7`).
- *So the difference is an identity, not a measurement.* The TINT–B5 difference is exactly that 10% mass by construction, and the "halving" is the ratio of two authored weights (0.10 vs. 0.20). The 12,000 test proposals per study are draws of 8 template strings (`dataset.py:19-41`), so the experiment is a unit test repeated 12,000 times.
- *The statistics are uninformative.* The paper acknowledges that $p=0.01172$ is the Wilcoxon/Holm floor ("What the statistics mean"). Given that, the bootstrap CI (−11.38 to −9.86), the $p$-value and $\hat A_{12}=1.00$ cannot distinguish a real effect from the design.
- *The comparison cannot measure quarantine's cost.* Because `ExposedJudge` is `StructuredJudge` plus a backdoor, the raw-text judge can never use its extra information to do *better* than the quarantined judge. The paper itself names this cost ("The price is information loss", Sec. IV-B; Discussion, third lesson), but the design makes it structurally zero. Equal utility is therefore also a design property, not a finding.

**Fix.**
1. Retitle the subsection, e.g. "After the fix, quarantine removed the judge-targeting family by construction".
2. In the abstract, replace the percentages with the mechanism: "removed the single judge-targeting family (10% of the authored mixture)".
3. Move the bootstrap, Wilcoxon and $\hat A_{12}$ to a footnote or the supplement, labelled "sign-consistency checks within the generator". Lead with the analytic population values already in `summary.json` (`analytic_fixture_population`).
4. State explicitly in Threats (construct validity) that the design cannot observe the information-loss cost of quarantine.
5. See M9 for the experiment that would measure that cost.

#### M2. A provenance-only control dominates TINT on every metric in this sandbox, including shift; the comparator set omits it.

**Location.**
- Sec. V-C, *Comparators*: "Each isolates a mechanism." Table VI.
- Sec. VII, *Provenance and isolation*: "a comparison with them is future work."
- Sec. V-C, *Substrate*: the implicit patch "is indistinguishable from an authorized patch in every guard's view".

**What I checked.** Every unsafe family that passes the sink proposes a `patch` whose only input is `'issue'`:
- direct injection, implicit patch and judge targeting (`dataset.py:28-38`);
- the encoded shift family (`dataset.py:39-41`).

`'issue'` is labelled `UNTRUSTED` in `fixture_store()` (`src/tint/agents/__init__.py:10`), so `ActionView.untrusted` is true for all of them. The only benign family accepted by TINT, the benign report, uses the `TRUSTED` input `'diagnostics'`.

I evaluated the rule "sink, then block any action with $U\in\lambda(a)$" on exploratory seeds 10–19, using the repository's own `dataset.sample`, `fixture_store` and `action_view`. It is a one-off script; no file was modified. Acceptance per family:

| Family | Acceptance |
|---|---|
| benign report | 100% |
| benign security | 0% |
| direct injection | 0% |
| exfiltration | 0% |
| implicit patch | 0% |
| judge targeting | 0% |
| production deletion | 0% |
| **encoded shift** | **0%** |

This rule therefore gives:
- joint risk **0%**, versus TINT's 10.16%;
- the same benign utility as TINT (66.67% in population);
- modeled cost of 1 unit, versus 370.67;
- shift risk **0%**, versus TINT's 100%.

It is the CaMeL/Fides principle the paper cites, and B1 (sink only) already computes the `untrusted` bit without using it.

**Why it matters.**
- It undermines the claim that the implicit patch is "a designed observability limit that no guard in the study can overcome". In this fixture law there is no authorized patch at all: every patch is issue-derived. The implicit patch is fully separable by provenance.
- It undermines the lesson "do not let the judge read what the attacker wrote" as the decisive mechanism. In this sandbox, "do not act on attacker-derived inputs" suffices and dominates.
- A TSE referee will view the omission of the most obvious comparator, which the paper's own related work motivates, as a fairness problem.

**Fix.**
1. Add the provenance-only control (B6) to Tables VI–VIII. This needs no new data generation, because the decisions are a deterministic function of the logged trace action and labels.
2. Discuss honestly why it wins here: the fixture law lacks benign issue-derived patches.
3. Revise the "observability limit" sentence.
4. If the authors want TINT's semantic layers to matter, they must add benign issue-derived actions to the fixture law. That is a new experiment; see M9.

#### M3. "The certification gate exposed the defect" is an overclaim; the gate detects excess calibrated risk, and only because $\varepsilon$ sat below the defective risk.

**Location.** The same claim appears in three places:
- Abstract: "the certification gate had exposed a floating-point defect in a scorer";
- Sec. V-F: "The certification gate turned a silent defect into a visible, safe failure";
- Discussion: "The certificate gate caught a defect that would otherwise have shipped."

**Why it matters.**
- *Humans found the defect.* The defect was found by humans "inspecting the raw counts" (paper's own words; `AMENDMENTS.md` A1). The gate only reported infeasibility.
- *Detection depended on the budget.* The primary per-seed minimum bounds were 31.1–35.0% (`resPrimaryBoundMin`/`Max`; `summary.json` → `primary/selections/*/bounds`), against $\varepsilon=20\%$. With any $\varepsilon\ge 35\%$, every primary seed would have certified the cheapest threshold (calibration cost 16.0 units for $t\ge0.8$; `results/raw/selections.json`, seed 0, `calibration_cost_mean`) and shipped the defect at 30% true risk.
- *A smaller defect would have passed.* A defect that raised risk by less than the slack, for example affecting one 10%-mass family when the clean risk is 10%, would have passed silently.
- *A1 would have caught it too.* Any pipeline whose risk is measured on labelled calibration data would have revealed the same, A1 included. The relevant mechanism is "measure joint risk on labelled calibration data before deploying", not the certificate binding.

**Fix.**
1. Rephrase as: "the risk gate refused to deploy a configuration whose calibrated risk exceeded the budget; inspecting the counts then revealed the defect."
2. Add the counterfactual sweep over $\varepsilon$ computed from the existing `calibration_misses` (no new data). It shows which budgets would have shipped the defect.
3. State that "single incident" also means "contingent on budget choice".

#### M4. H1 evidence is overstated: the "independent enumeration" is a tautology, the PRISM model hard-codes the conclusion, and the hard-violation metric reuses the sink's predicate.

**Location.**
- Sec. V-D, H1: "The independent enumeration of all 64 sink inputs confirmed that the sink blocks exactly the inputs that violate $\varphi_{\mathrm{det}}$."
- Table IX: "Sink correctness … enumeration of all 64 inputs; PRISM on abstraction — **verified for the typed view**."
- Table IV, row "Finite abstraction".
- Sec. V-C: "enumerate all combinations of the sink's six Boolean inputs".

**What the code does.**
- *The enumeration never runs the sink.* `resFormalStates`=64 comes from `formal/check.py:28-34`. That code never calls `tint.layers.deterministic`. It defines `bad=…` and `execute = semantic_allow and not bad`, then asserts `not (execute and bad)`, which is true for any input. Its six bits also include `semantic_allow` instead of the view's `untrusted` field, so they are not "the sink's six Boolean inputs".
- *The PRISM model states the answer in its guards.* The model (`formal/cascade.pm`) is a deterministic 5-state chain whose guard `s=1 & checked & trace!=1 -> (s'=3)` sends $T^*$ and $T_j$ to the blocking state whenever `checked` holds. "No configuration reaches bad under checked routing" is therefore written into the model, not discovered.
- *The replay metric uses the sink's own logic.* In the sandbox replay, `hard_violation` is computed with the same predicate as the sink (`experiments/run.py:123` vs. `layers/__init__.py:9-14`). "Zero hard violations for pipelines with a sink" is close to definitional.
- *The genuine evidence is elsewhere.* The real implementation-level check exists: the unit test `test_all_deterministic_cases` (`tests/test_core.py:72-76`) enumerates all 64 `ActionView`s against `deterministic()`. It checks the implementation against a re-typed formula, so it is a regression check, not an independent oracle.

**Fix.**
1. Cite `test_all_deterministic_cases` as the H1 enumeration evidence.
2. Describe `formal/check.py` and the PRISM model as illustrations of the countermodel, not verification. Drop "verified" from Table IX, or replace it with "unit-tested against a re-typed formula".
3. Acknowledge that the hard-violation metric shares the sink's predicate.
4. Either fix the phrase "the sink's six Boolean inputs" or make `formal/check.py` enumerate the real view. A differential check against an independently authored policy (for example, written from the paper's $\varphi_1,\varphi_2$ by a different author) would be genuine evidence.

#### M5. The checker-scaling claim ("grows linearly with the number of edges") is contradicted by the paper's own data and by the code.

**Location.**
- Sec. IV-A: "The check is linear in the number of layers and edges, multiplied by the size of the evidence sets."
- Sec. V-D, H2: "Checking time grows linearly with the number of edges (Fig. 9)".
- Fig. 9 caption: "Time is roughly proportional to the number of edges".

**Evidence.**
- *Per-edge time grows about six-fold.* From `summary.json` → `studies/primary/checker/scalability`, the median time per edge for 6-layer specs:
  - 0.60 µs at 25 edges;
  - 0.64 µs at 250 edges;
  - 1.10 µs at 500 edges;
  - 1.57 µs at 1250 edges;
  - **3.90 µs** at 2500 edges.

  The 2-layer specs show the same pattern: 0.71 µs at 50 edges and 3.98 µs at 500 edges.
- *Doubling the edges quintuples the time.* Doubling properties from 250 to 500 multiplies time by about 5× in every depth series (`\resCheckerScalingTwo/Four/Six`).
- *The code explains why.* `src/tint/spec/__init__.py`, inside the edge loop, evaluates `edge.property not in caller.scope & callee.scope`. This builds a set intersection of size $O(|P|)$ per edge, so total cost is $O(E\cdot|P|)=O(L\,|P|^2)$ for the generated specs. The log–log plot hides this.

**Fix.**
1. Correct the complexity statement and the caption.
2. Better, replace the line with `edge.property in caller.scope and edge.property in callee.scope` (O(1) expected), re-run only the checker study, and label it as an amendment.

#### M6. The preregistration chronology and decision rules need fuller disclosure; H6 has no stated verdict and its rule is satisfied trivially by block-all.

**Location.**
- Sec. V-A: "Before running any calibration or held-out evaluation, we froze eight hypotheses …"
- Sec. V-C: "All thresholds, budgets and analyses were frozen before held-out data were generated."
- Table III, row H6.

**Evidence.**
- *What the tag froze and what it did not.* `git rev-list -n1 prereg-v1` = `a48fca6` (07:56:30). The tag tree contains `pilot.json`, `devagent.json`, `layers` and `baselines`. It does **not** contain `experiments/dataset.py` or `experiments/run.py`; both were added in `c47f2a4` (07:58:06), the commit recorded in `results/raw/environment.json`.
- *Why that matters here.* The template strings in `dataset.py` fully determine every outcome, including $k$, and therefore which families cross $s\ge0.40$. Those strings were authored after the freeze and with knowledge of the scorer's token list.
- *Timeline.* The whole sequence (preregistration → instrument → primary run → A1 → exploratory run) spans about four minutes. A2 was written after results and reviews were seen.
- *H6 has no verdict.* The paper never states H6's verdict. `summary.json` → `hypotheses/H6` says "inconclusive for useful safety-utility improvement in primary". Yet the preregistered rule ("support risk improvement only if paired CI excludes zero") is *met* by the block-all primary policy: all six CIs exclude zero, e.g. −30.43 to −29.19 vs. A1. The authors' "inconclusive" is a sensible but undeclared deviation from their own rule, and the rule itself was ill-posed because block-all satisfies it.

**Fix.**
1. Add an "Outcome" column to Table III (supported / refuted / inconclusive, per study).
2. State the H6 deviation and why the rule was ill-posed.
3. Disclose that the fixture templates and driver were committed after the tag (with hashes), and that A2 is post hoc.
4. In future, freeze the generator in the preregistration.

#### M7. The certified exploratory policy and the selection problem are weaker than the RQ2 framing suggests.

**Location.**
- RQ2: "which escalation policy minimizes expected cost subject to a risk bound".
- Sec. V-E, *Answer to RQ2*: "the cost-minimal policy can be selected exactly".

**Evidence.**
- *The certified set is one equivalence class.* In the exploratory run, the 11-point grid collapses to two risk levels (`resExploratoryDistinctRiskLevels`=2). Using seed 10 from `results/raw/exploratory_numeric/selections.json`:
  - the certified set is $t\in\{0,0.1,0.2,0.3,0.4\}$;
  - $t=0.1$ through $0.4$ have *identical* cost (356.6) and *identical* bound (0.123);
  - the selection of $t=0.1$ is decided by the string tie-break `candidates[i].identifier` (`src/tint/synthesis/__init__.py:31`).
- *The selection machinery is never exercised in the sandbox.* Prop. 5 (Lagrangian) and the mixture LP are never applied there; H5 tests them only on random synthetic families.
- *The certified policy is unsafe by any practical standard.* It accepts 10.16% of all proposals unsafely, and 25.40% of accepted actions are unsafe (`resExploratoryAcceptedConditionalRisk`), under a "deliberately permissive" 20% budget.

**Fix.**
1. State plainly that the sandbox selection is a binary feasible/infeasible decision with ties.
2. Report the certified set and the tie-break.
3. Add an $\varepsilon$ sweep (existing calibration counts suffice) showing at which budgets the selected policy and its cost change.
4. Temper "cost-minimal" in the RQ2 answer to "within a degenerate two-level family in the sandbox; non-trivially only in H5's synthetic families".

#### M8. The certified object is not the deployed object, and the fingerprint binds version strings, not code.

**Location.**
- Sec. IV-D: "The fingerprint is a SHA-256 hash of the specification, threshold, scorer version, judge version, view and environment."
- Sec. V-B, *Runtime*.

**Evidence.**
- *Calibration and deployment run different code.* Calibration losses are computed with `baselines.decide('checked_quarantine', …)` (`experiments/run.py:63-70`), while held-out decisions come from `GuardRuntime.evaluate` (`run.py:103-113`).
- *The certificate is built from the same values the runtime reads.* The driver constructs it from a typed string, `scorer_version='lexical-v2' if EXPLORATORY else 'lexical-v1'` (`run.py:94-95`). It matches the runtime because it is built from the same values, so certificate binding is never exercised against a mismatch in the study. Mismatch is exercised only in unit tests (`tests/test_core.py:106-135`).
- *The fingerprint cannot see a code change.* The fingerprint hashes the `version` attribute (`runtime/__init__.py:100-106`), not the code. The A1 defect fix was caught by the binding only because the authors manually bumped `lexical-v1`→`lexical-v2` (diff `c47f2a4..046501c`). A silent code change with an unchanged version string would reuse the stale certificate, which is precisely failure mode F3.
- *Partial evidence of equivalence.* `scripts/validate_replay.py` shows that `decide()` and the runtime agree on development, test and shift traces. Calibration traces are not replayed through the runtime.

**Fix.**
1. Disclose this.
2. Calibrate through the runtime (or replay the calibration split through it in `validate_replay.py`).
3. Discuss hashing implementation artifacts (e.g., module source hash) rather than self-reported version strings, as the honest-host assumption otherwise carries all of C3.

### B. Requiring new experiments

#### M9. The fixture law cannot test the paper's central trade-off.

To support RQ3's question ("how does a pipeline built with the discipline trade risk, benign utility and cost"), the sandbox needs:
- (a) benign issue-derived actions, such as authorized patches requested in issues, so that provenance alone cannot separate safe from unsafe (see M2);
- (b) a raw-text judge that genuinely benefits from text, e.g. one that correctly accepts benign security text, so that quarantine has a measurable utility cost (see M1);
- (c) several paraphrases per family, so that the scorer's threshold is not a step function over 8 strings and selection becomes non-degenerate (see M7).

None of this requires LLM access. Without it, RQ3 should be re-scoped to "executable validation of designed behaviours".

#### M10. The CPU comparison is asymmetric and partly misattributed.

**Location.** Sec. V-F, *Cost and latency*: "The difference comes from per-decision work in the runtime, mainly recomputing the configuration fingerprint … and logging".

**Evidence.**
- *Logging is not in the timed path.* In the experiment the runtime is constructed without `log_path` (`run.py:103-104`), so `GuardRuntime.evaluate` performs no logging (`runtime/__init__.py:93`). The driver's own `put(...)` runs after `elapsed` is taken (`run.py:118-119`).
- *Only TINT's timing includes view computation.* For comparators, `action_view` is computed *before* the timer starts (`run.py:108-110`). TINT's timing includes `action_view` inside `evaluate` (`runtime/__init__.py:63`) and the SHA-256 fingerprint.
- *The label is wrong.* "CPU" in Table VII is wall-clock `perf_counter_ns`, not CPU time.

**Fix.** Re-time with symmetric boundaries and an optional cached fingerprint, or remove the attribution sentence and the "CPU" label. This is a cheap re-run of the timing only.

### Diagnosis of the `verify-results` failure

- *Mismatching files.* Comparing `manifest.files()` with `results/manifest.json`, exactly four files differ: `scripts/check_refs.py`, `scripts/make_macros.py`, `scripts/verify_numbers.py` and `scripts/verify_pdf.py`. All four carry uncommitted edits (`git status`: ` M`). The raw and processed data are **not** affected.
- *HEAD fails too.* For three of them, the committed HEAD content matches the manifest. For `scripts/make_macros.py`, the manifest hash (`98465314…`) equals the HEAD content **with CRLF line endings** (`git show HEAD:… | sed 's/$/\r/' | sha256sum`), and `core.autocrlf=true`. A Linux or Docker checkout would therefore fail even at HEAD.
- *Stale analysis commit.* The manifest's `analysis_commit` is `1187502`, not HEAD `50a3180`.
- *Weakened provenance check.* The uncommitted edit to `scripts/verify_numbers.py` relaxes the provenance check so that typed integers in prose are no longer flagged ("Integers … are structural and may be typed in prose"). As a result, integer result counts typed by hand, such as "56 tests" (Sec. V-B) and "fifty random finite candidate families" (Sec. V-C), are no longer macro-checked. This contradicts "Every result number in this paper is a macro" (Sec. V-B). Decimal literals such as "$0.007$" (Threats) and "$0.006$" (Sec. IV-C) also appear in prose.

## 6. Minor issues

- **m1. Abstract, "(336,000 logged decisions)".** This suggests a scale the design does not have: the decisions are 7 pipelines × repeated draws of 8 template strings. Suggest: "(336,000 logged decisions over eight authored templates)".
- **m2. Sec. V-D, H1, "In the sandbox replay, the pipelines with a sink … executed no hard violation".** The held-out replay never goes through `Sandbox.call` or `parse_tool_call`. `run.py:112` calls `rt.evaluate` directly, so nothing is executed and complete mediation is not exercised at scale. Suggest: "accepted no action that violates $\varphi_{\mathrm{det}}$".
- **m3. Table IX, "Complete mediation … only route to an effect is `Sandbox.call` through the runtime".** True in the library, but exercised only by the three canonical traces and five typed-effect tests. Say so.
- **m4. Sec. V-C, "CPU time is wall-clock time".** Rename the column from "CPU" to "Latency (wall-clock)" in Table VII. Also, the table reports p95 while the text reports medians; use one consistently or give both.
- **m5. Sec. IV-A, Alg. 1 line 2, "reject if $M$ or $\mathcal{R}$ differs from the supported deterministic fragment".** The code requires *exactly* `{'production_approval','secret_egress'}` (`spec/__init__.py`, `check`). Say "is not exactly". This also explains why H8 is refuted.
- **m6. Sec. V-D, H2.** Document that all four mutation operators modify only `edges[0]` of homogeneous generated specs (`run.py:174-177`). Also note that the quarantine/raw-view rule and the mandatory-invariant rule of Alg. 1 are exercised only by unit tests, not by the H2 study.
- **m7. Sec. V-E, H3: "Both lie well above the nominal 95%".** Add that the decision rule ("refute if upper end < 0.95") can only detect gross under-coverage, and that `target_covered` = 1000/1000 in both studies, because the selected candidate's true risk is at most 0.10 by grid design (selected indices 5–8 only; `results/raw/coverage.jsonl.gz`).
- **m8. Sec. V-C, Statistical analysis.** $\hat A_{12}$ is computed unpaired over seed aggregates (`process_results.py:114`) while the test is paired. State this.
- **m9. Sec. IV-C, "about seventeen times higher".** Fine (0.1/0.006 = 16.7), but these are conditional miss probabilities given $Y=1$, whereas $R$ elsewhere is joint. Add "conditional on an attack".
- **m10. Sec. V-B, "Every result number in this paper is a macro".** This is untrue for "56 tests", "fifty random finite candidate families", "four mutation operators", "$0.007$" and "a thousand replications". Either qualify it ("every measured value from the experiment driver") or macro-ise them.
- **m11. `src/tint/spec/__init__.py` comment "iterative DFS handles large specs"** describes Kahn's algorithm (BFS-like). Cosmetic, but the paper quotes Kahn.
- **m12. Sec. V-C, Environment.** "Two dated amendments, committed before the respective analyses": A2's analyses are post hoc on already-inspected data. Suggest: "A2, written after round-1 review, adds descriptive post hoc analyses".
- **m13. Table V header "Sink ($L_1$)" for exfiltration: "block ($\varphi_2$)".** In the runtime, production deletion is also caught by the environment-mismatch check (`runtime/__init__.py:65-66`) before $\varphi_1$ is reported. This is harmless, but the logged reason differs from Table V.
- **m14. Data Availability, "complete regeneration in a few minutes on a laptop CPU".** This was not verifiable without PRISM, Java and TeX, and `tasks.py all` archives the raw data. State the prerequisites here.
