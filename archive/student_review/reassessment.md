## Full-paper comprehension recall without looking back

The following answers were composed after reading/visual inspection without reopening the manuscript. They reflect the revised understanding; I do not supply a reconstructed proof or instrument detail I cannot recall.

1. **Problem.** A guard's allow is justified only for certain views, scopes, provenance and calibration contexts. Delegating a difficult action to another guard does not establish those premises. TINT puts hard effect checks and identity/applicability checks into a restricted typed dispatcher, while leaving implementation honesty and deployment-distribution validity as external assumptions.

2. **Meaning and importance of nontransitivity.** A trusts B and B trusts C do not alone authorize A to execute C's verdict. Each edge's actual interface/property premises and hard sink obligations need establishing. The canonical raw-judge counterexample can satisfy locally conditional contracts while executing secret exfiltration because the caller supplied raw injected text and omitted the sink. This is missing-premise composition, not a failure of valid assume-guarantee logic. The empirical judge-targeting attack is instead an unauthorized patch with no exact hard violation, so a sink alone can protect the canonical trace but not that empirical semantic attack.

3. **RQ I: specification/decomposition and answer.** The work asks how to express agent safety with per-layer contracts and explicit trust. It provides exact production-approval and secret-egress rules, an external-label semantic risk objective, direct conditioned reliance and restricted declarations. The generic composition argument is scoped; only two of five policy classes are fully supported and there is no general decomposition solver or proved refinement of the runtime from the model.

4. **RQ II: guarantees/escalation and answer.** Exclusive early-allow routes sum their unsafe masses; requiring all guards to allow instead intersects their miss events. A product requires unsafe-conditional independence and one prevalence factor, while the common-shock interpolation belongs only to its stated generator. Known conditional risks and a suitable finite multiplier yield a sufficient cost-optimal escalation rule: added cost must be lower than multiplier-weighted risk reduction. The pilot instead selects among a finite fixed grid using simultaneous binomial certificates and observed calibration costs. Arbitrary adaptive/shifted populations are not covered merely by matching a fingerprint.

5. **RQ III: engineering value and answer.** General practical value is unanswered. All primary candidate families fail certification, so denial achieves zero risk and zero benign utility. A fresh-seed arithmetic correction produces roughly 10% joint risk and two-thirds benign utility on the authored mixture, but checked calibrated and uncalibrated decisions coincide. Some semantic patches still pass, and every attack in the designed all-attack shift passes. No real models, external benchmarks, development-task completion or strong adaptive robustness are measured.

6. **Key theorem and premises.** Correct hard checks on every effect, complete mediation with authentic metadata, irreversible denials, exclusive exhaustive acceptance routes and simultaneously valid risk bounds yield no hard violations and joint semantic risk at most the route-bound sum. Calibration-sample probability controls confidence in that population-risk assertion, distinct from the decision risk itself. TINT's implementation estimates one whole-policy bound rather than individual route bounds. Configuration equality does not prove the statistical relation between calibration and deployment.

7. **Evaluation and headlines.** The local study uses handwritten scoring, scripted raw/structured judges, finite model checks, generated mutation tests, ten-seed fixture replay, separate repeated nested-loss calibration, and finite selection/LP consistency checks. I recall 12,000 primary test proposals, all ten primary policy families infeasible, 99.60% selected-bound coverage over 1000 replications of a separate generator, and 10.16% exploratory joint risk with 66.46% benign utility. The exploratory example has 186 losses in 1800 calibration proposals, selected threshold 0.10 and a simultaneous upper bound of 12.34%. Uncertified baselines use threshold 0.20. These exact values were memorable because they are now connected to defined quantities; I do not recall the entire risk/cost grid or timing table.

8. **Correction mechanism.** Three listed feature matches intend a score of 0.40. Historical floating arithmetic gives slightly less, so the structured judge's cutoff misses direct/judge-targeting patch cases. Integer hundredths align stored score and cutoff, causing denial and reducing checked-core population risk from 30% to 10%, below the 20% certification target. Old certificates cannot apply because the scorer version changes. Implicit patches remain accepted; calibration itself did not invent a detector improvement. I cannot name the actual lexical features or reproduce the raw phrase from memory because the manuscript did not list those features/strings; this is a remaining minor reproducibility gap, not confusion about the causal route.
